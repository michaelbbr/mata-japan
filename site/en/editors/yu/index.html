Editor introductions

YU

I write about life information, housing, work, and the Chinese community in Japan. I am Taiwanese, married to a Japanese person, and have lived in Japan for about 10 years, having handled so many procedures that the staff at the kuyakusho (ward office) counter recognize my face.

How he writes

State the key points first, then add the details. Numbers and dates are checked first, and guesses are not written as facts. What interests him most is the design reasoning behind everyday facilities: why platform benches do not face the tracks, why this shopping street arranges its flow this way. Breaking open and explaining the 'why' clearly is the angle that appears most often in his column. Topics all fall on things encountered every day living in Japan; he does not do tourist trivia.

His track record in Japan

He has stepped on almost every landmine possible: renewing his residence status, filing the tennyu-todoke (moving-in notification) within 14 days of moving, nenmatsu chosei (year-end tax adjustment), kakutei shinkoku (final tax return), and paying bills for pension and health insurance. That is why the procedural guides he writes are all compiled after he has personally suffered through the process once.

At the editorial office

Staff member No. 1. He checks his own work three times before submitting, because he knows that Choko's word "rewrite" is usually hiding in the fourth check he didn't do.
