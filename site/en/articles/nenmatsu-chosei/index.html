Don't rush to submit your year-end tax adjustment form; filling out these sections incorrectly can cause a lot of trouble.

The 2026 tax year revision raises the basic deduction to 620,000 to 1,040,000 yen, the income wall shifts, and you still need to file your own taxes for side jobs and medical expenses.

There's a lot of paper. Feeling sleepy halfway through is normal; the quick summary is at the end of the article, just check against it once before submitting the form.

The stack of papers your company gives you in November is for the year-end tax adjustment (Nenmatsu Chosei). It’s a simple process: the company compares the total income tax withheld from your salary throughout the year with the actual tax amount calculated at the end of the year. If they withheld too much, you get a refund; if they withheld too little, you pay the difference. This process is only handled by your company if you are a regular employee with an annual salary under 20 million yen and have submitted your deduction declaration forms (such as for dependents). If you didn't submit those forms, you aren't covered by the year-end tax adjustment. This is generally the scope defined by the National Tax Agency.

The scope of deductions handled by the company in the year-end tax adjustment is wider than many people think. The basic deduction, spousal deduction, special spousal deduction, dependent deduction, and income amount adjustment deduction are all included. Life insurance, earthquake insurance, social insurance, and small-scale enterprise mutual aid premiums are also handled here; iDeCo contributions are counted under the small-scale enterprise mutual aid premium deduction. The housing loan deduction can be included in the year-end tax adjustment starting from the second year, but not the first year—this is the easiest rule to get backward.

This year's figures are different from last year's. This year is for the Reiwa 8 tax year. The basic deduction ranges from 620,000 to 1.04 million yen based on total income; for those with a total income of 4.89 million yen or less, it is a flat 1.04 million yen (for those with higher income, the basic deduction is reduced in stages to 480,000, 320,000, and 160,000 yen, and drops to zero if total income exceeds 25.95 million yen). The minimum guaranteed amount for the employment income deduction has been raised to 740,000 yen. With both figures changing, the 'income wall' has shifted: the income requirement for dependents is now 620,000 yen, which translates to 1.36 million yen in employment income. For those whose only income is a salary, the threshold for when income tax begins to be levied is now approximately 1.78 million yen. Note that this is the threshold for income tax; the thresholds for resident tax and social insurance dependency are different, so don't confuse them. Last year's table is obsolete, so check the new figures before filling out your forms.

🧾 For things that cannot be handled in the year-end tax adjustment, file a final tax return yourself between February and March of the following year. Among the targets for final tax returns listed by the National Tax Agency, those relevant to office workers include those with an annual salary over 20 million yen, those with total income other than salary exceeding 200,000 yen, and those receiving salary from two or more sources who meet the filing criteria. The 200,000 yen figure refers to 'income'—the amount after subtracting necessary expenses from revenue. If you have 300,000 yen in side-job revenue and 120,000 yen in expenses, your income is 180,000 yen; if you have 400,000 yen in revenue and 120,000 yen in expenses, your income is 280,000 yen. The conclusions are completely different.

For the first year of medical expense deductions, casualty loss deductions, and housing loan deductions, these items are not included in the year-end tax adjustment, so you must file them yourself if you want to claim them. The same applies to the hometown tax donation deduction, unless you are using the one-stop exception system. There is another point that is often missed: office workers with side income under 200,000 yen may be exempt from filing a final tax return for income tax under certain conditions. However, if you are already required to file a final tax return for other reasons, the situation is different. You must also check residence tax separately; even if you do not need to file for income tax, you may still need to file with your city or ward office.

Before submitting the form, spend a few minutes checking the annual income of your dependents, and don't forget to attach the insurance deduction certificate. If you realize you made a mistake after submitting it, first ask your company if it can be corrected. If the year-end tax adjustment (nenmatsu chosei) is already complete, check if you need to file a final tax return (kakutei shinkoku) yourself.

Quick summary

  • The year-end tax adjustment is when your company reconciles your taxes for you. Most office workers with an annual salary under 20 million yen who have submitted their declaration forms are eligible.
  • Reiwa 8 basic deduction: 620,000 to 1.04 million yen; minimum employment income deduction: 740,000 yen; dependency wall: 1.36 million yen; income tax threshold: approximately 1.78 million yen.
  • Companies can handle insurance deductions and housing loan deductions from the second year onward, but medical expense deductions, casualty loss deductions, and the first year of the housing loan deduction require you to file a final tax return yourself.
  • For side jobs, look at income, not revenue; if the total exceeds 200,000 yen, you must file a tax return. If it is 200,000 yen or less, you usually still need to report it to the city or ward office for residence tax purposes.
  • This year is for the 2026 tax year; check your annual income for dependents and insurance certificates before submitting your forms.

From the editor-in-chief

When that stack of year-end tax adjustment papers arrives, I stuff the whole thing in a drawer every year. I wait until the day before the deadline to take them out and fill them in slowly while looking at the examples. The dependent sections look the same every year, but I have to re-study them each time, only to forget them again and repeat the process next year.
