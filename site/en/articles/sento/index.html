When going to a sento (public bathhouse) for the first time, do not bring your towel into the water.

There aren't many rules; they're all written on the wall, just follow them and you won't feel awkward

Living in Tokyo, when I want to wash away a week's fatigue on the weekend for the least amount of money, I go to the sento (public bathhouse) near my house. With the hot water up to my shoulders, my body completely relaxes. Sitting by the pool watching the steam slowly rise, the exhaustion of the week seems to dissipate along with it.

Many Chinese-speaking friends who just moved here are intimidated by sento (public baths) for the same reason: they're afraid of the rules and being watched for doing something wrong. I accompanied a friend for the first time, and they started getting nervous in the changing room. After going a few times, they realized only a few things really get noticed. You can't put your towel in the bathwater; keep it on the side or folded on your head. Before entering the bath, wash your body thoroughly at the washing station and rinse off all the soap. You can also use 'kake-yu' (pouring warm water over your body) before entering to let your body gradually adjust to the temperature. Remember to tie up long hair so the ends don't soak in the bathwater.

The Edogawa Ward Office's page for foreign residents spells these points out clearly: wash your body before entering the pool, do not put towels in the pool, tie up long hair, and dry your body before returning to the changing room. The pool water is shared by everyone; if you rinse yourself clean, the next person will be comfortable soaking too. When you think of it as water everyone shares, you naturally want to rinse a couple more times; that is probably what the rules are about.

♨️ One more thing I have observed for a long time: rules written on the wall do not mean everyone follows every one of them. Occasionally you see someone rinse off very casually and get straight in, and the regulars nearby just quietly move a little farther away. So on your first visit, there is no need to get so nervous that you memorize every rule; doing the few things above is quite enough.

I like to go on relatively quiet weekday evenings; after soaking, I buy a bottle of coffee milk from the vending machine, stand at the entrance and enjoy the evening breeze for a bit before going home. The glass bottle is ice-cold, held in a hand warmed by the bath, just right. Those twenty minutes are one of the few times in Tokyo life when I don't look at my phone at all.

Quick summary

  • Towels don't go in the bath. Leave them at the side of the tub or fold them and place them on your head
  • Before entering the bath, wash your body thoroughly, then use kakeyu (rinsing yourself with hot water before entering the bath) to adjust to the water temperature.
  • Tie up long hair so the ends do not soak in the pool water
  • Dry your body before returning to the changing room
  • Remember to bring your own small towel, toiletries, and coins (some shops require coins for the hair dryers).

From the editor-in-chief

The first time I went to a sento, I walked toward the bath with my towel on my shoulders. The old man next to me glanced at me, said nothing, and just pointed to the side of the bath. I put my towel down before getting in. I bathed more quietly than usual that day, even keeping my coughs soft.
