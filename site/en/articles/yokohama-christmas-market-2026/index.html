Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.19

The Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse Christmas Market has begun! From December 5, it stays open until 10 PM, and there are free illuminations to see in the seaside area.

From November 20 to December 25, 2026, entry to the main venue starts at 500 yen. Free areas are located at Red Brick Warehouse Park and Shinko Central Square.

Living in Tokyo, I often bundle up at home in the winter. To save on heating bills, I don't turn on the heater, wear two layers of sweaters, and even my fingertips feel cold while sitting on the sofa. Even with a hot cup of tea, the warmth doesn't last long. On weekend nights, I actually feel like going out for a walk. The Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse Christmas Market runs from November 20 to December 25, 2026. Once the seaside lights turn on, standing outdoors with a hot drink makes my shoulders relax, and my body warms up more easily than it does staying at home.

The main venue is the event plaza at the Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse, where German-style wooden huts stand side by side, selling hot food, desserts, and Christmas trinkets. The air is filled with the scent of grilled sausages and mulled wine. Business hours are divided into three periods: 5:00 PM to 9:00 PM on the opening day of November 20; 11:00 AM to 9:00 PM from November 21 to December 4; and extended until 10:00 PM from December 5 to December 25. The last order for food and drinks is 30 minutes before closing. The illuminations light up at 4:00 PM daily. After the sky darkens, the red brick exterior walls are wrapped in warm light. As the sea breeze blows, your face feels cold, but the colors you see are very warm.

Entry fees depend on the date. General admission to the main venue is 500 yen on regular days and 1,000 yen on specific days. Specific days are December 5, 6, 12, 13, 18–20, 24, and 25. Children elementary school age and under enter free with a guardian, and until December 4, junior high students and under are also free. November 25 is Yokohama Citizens' Appreciation Day, with free entry for Yokohama residents. To skip the lines at the entrance, you can buy a Priority Entry ticket for 2,000 yen on regular days or 2,500 yen on specific days. General admission tickets are cash-only at the entrance; if you want to use other payment methods, you'll need to pre-purchase through official ticketing channels. It's safer to keep some cash in your wallet.

There are free areas again this year. The Christmas Village on the Red Brick Warehouse Park side is a free zone, featuring Pokémon illuminations from November 25 to December 25—perfect for those who don't have much time after work and just want to stroll by the sea and see the lights. In front of the warehouse, Shinko Central Square hosts the Christmas Gate, which is also free to enter and features market stalls and a large tree. The operating hours for the free areas and the main venue aren't exactly the same, and the content varies by zone, so check the official page before you go so you don't end up standing confused at the entrance.

Transportation is simple. It's about a 6-minute walk from Bashamichi Station or Nihon-odori Station on the Minatomirai Line, about a 12-minute walk from Minatomirai Station, and about a 15-minute walk from Sakuragicho Station on the JR and municipal subway lines. It's windy by the sea. Bring a scarf and gloves so you won't regret it while waiting for hot food at the stalls. On the way back, walk slowly along the harbor; the Ferris wheel lights spin in the distance, and the cup in your hand still holds a bit of warmth. You don't need to pack a night like this with too much; leaving a little space makes it just right.

From the editor-in-chief

Last year, I planned to only visit the free areas and keep my wallet tightly shut. But after standing in Shinko Central Square staring at that tree for too long, my feet got so cold I couldn't take it, and I ended up buying hot soup and gloves. Calculating my expenses on the train ride home, I hadn't spent a single yen on admission for the free area, but I'd spent more on staying warm than the main venue's ticket would have cost.
