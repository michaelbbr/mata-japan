If you want to throw away old furniture before the end of the year, you need to book a pickup for sodai gomi (oversized garbage) in December.

The reception center is closed from December 29, and reception slots for within the year fill up quickly in every ward

For Chinese people living in Japan, 'spring cleaning' often happens twice: once in December following the Japanese year-end and New Year traditions, and again before the Lunar New Year—it is unavoidable. For the items you clear out, small items should be discarded according to the usual sorting rules. Large items like wardrobes, bookshelves, mattresses, and bicycles usually must be handled as sodai gomi (oversized garbage). Taking Shinjuku Ward and Setagaya Ward from this article as examples, anything with a side longer than 30 centimeters is classified as sodai gomi, and dimensions and item categories must be determined according to each ward's regulations; you cannot apply these rules directly to other areas.

You can't just move oversized trash to the curb and wait for it to be picked up. The following process and fees are based on Shinjuku Ward and Setagaya Ward as examples: You must apply to the reception center of the ward where you live, either by phone or online. When you apply, a pickup date will be set, and the processing fee will be calculated. The amount is categorized by item type and size; in both Setagaya and Shinjuku Wards, fees range from 400 to 3,200 yen. Payment is made via processing stickers, which are sold at convenience stores and supermarkets displaying the 'Oversized Trash Processing Sticker' sign. In Setagaya Ward, you combine 200-yen 'A' stickers and 300-yen 'B' stickers to reach the required amount. Write the pickup date and your name on the stickers, stick them in a visible spot on the item, and move it to the designated location by 8:00 AM on the pickup day. You don't need to wait there; they will collect it according to their list.

What really traps people at the end of the year is time. Shinjuku Ward's announcement this year is very clear: phone reception for bulky waste is closed from December 29 to January 3, and Setagaya Ward's phone reception is also closed during the same period. Online applications are still available 24 hours, but collection itself also stops for a few days over the year-end and New Year period. Even if you can book, there are only a few days left in the year when collection is possible, and everyone who applies after mid-December crowds together, so the date you get often jumps to January already. A compilation of year-end and New Year waste collection announcements from 50 cities shows that among the 29 cities with phone reception data, 21 stopped accepting applications from December 29 to January 3, and the deadline for collection within the year generally falls in late December. If you want to clear out old furniture within the year, complete your application between late November and early December, and you'll have more choice of dates.

The schedule for each of Tokyo's 23 wards is different. This article only checked Shinjuku Ward and Setagaya Ward, so please do not apply this to other wards directly. The method for checking your own ward is consistent: go to the ward office's official website, search for 'nenmatsu nenshi sodai gomi' (year-end and New Year oversized garbage), and find the page for this year. It will state when the telephone reception desk will be closed and how the final collection of the year is arranged. The ward's public relations newsletter for December will also have the same schedule; just flip to the garbage section before you throw away the copy that was delivered to your mailbox.

🧹 People who don't apply in time are easily swayed by flyers in their mailboxes. They promise free collection and same-day pickup, but be careful when you call them. Only operators with a permit for general waste collection and transport, or those commissioned by the ward office, are allowed to collect household waste—the Setagaya Ward website explicitly states this. If an unlicensed operator takes your items, no one knows where they end up; they might be illegally dumped in mountains or rivers, and you could be held responsible for the cost of disposal. Also, disputes where they promise it's free but then demand high fees once the items are loaded onto the truck happen every year. If you really need a private operator, verify they have the ward's permit before hiring them—don't just rely on the phone number on the flyer.

The big cleanup is scheduled for the end of November; the bulky waste has been booked, so in December only small things like wiping the windows are left, and you can take your time.

Quick summary

  • Sodai gomi (oversized garbage) must be applied for before it can be collected; the collection date and handling fee are determined at the time of application via phone or online.
  • Buy the processing stickers at convenience stores or supermarkets, write the collection date and your name and stick them on, and put the item at the designated place before 8 a.m. that day
  • In Shinjuku Ward and Setagaya Ward, telephone reception is closed from December 29 to January 3, and slots for collection within the year are already very full in December
  • For the deadline in your area, search for 'nenmatsu nenshi sodai gomi' (year-end and New Year oversized garbage) on your ward office website to see this year's announcement.
  • When you see a flyer for free collection, don't rush to call. First check whether the operator has the household waste collection permit required in your area, or is a contractor commissioned by the municipality
