A Taiwan license converts to a Japanese license without tests, while the 50 questions facing a China license got harder

Starting October 2025, the knowledge test was changed to 45 correct answers out of 50. The pass rate dropped to 42.8%. If you live in Tokyo, remember to book your appointment at Fuchu or Samezu.

If you just want to know whether you need to take the test, the answer is summarized in the guide at the end of this article. Just scroll down to check.

For those who rely on trains, a driver's license is usually at the bottom of the to-do list. But when you need to rent a car to move or want to visit a suburban shopping mall on the weekend, you might realize that the rules for foreign license conversion changed in October 2025. I have lived in Japan for years and don't drive—I get by fine with trains—but I have compiled these new rules for friends who need to convert their licenses.

The biggest change is in the knowledge test. The Road Traffic Act Enforcement Regulations were revised on October 1, 2025. The National Police Agency's explanation is clear: picture-based questions have been abolished, the number of questions has increased from 10 to 50, and the passing score has been raised from 7 out of 10 to 45 out of 50. You now need a 90% score to pass, and there is only one session in the afternoon. The days of guessing based on pictures are over. 🚗

The skills test has also tightened. Both the National Police Agency and the Ibaraki Prefectural Police mention that new items, such as passing through a crosswalk, have been added to the course, and the scoring is now as strict as it is for new license applicants. Failing to use turn signals or turning incorrectly results in heavier penalties than before. The National Police Agency announced the pass rate for the first 3 months after the reform (October to December 2025) for all applicants: 42.8% for the knowledge test (compared to 92.5% in 2024 before the reform) and 13.1% for the skills test (compared to 30.4% in the previous year). Both figures have dropped by more than half; those who try it with a 'let's see what happens' attitude are now very likely to be sent home at the first hurdle.

The treatment of driver's licenses from Taiwan and mainland China differs based on the official list. The National Police Agency has a list of 29 countries and regions where the knowledge and skills tests are waived, and Taiwan is on that list. To be clear, 'waived' means you don't have to take those two tests, but you still have to pass the document review and aptitude test. Mainland China is not on the list, so both the knowledge and skills tests are required. A common requirement for both is that you must have stayed in the issuing country for a total of at least 3 months after obtaining the license; the entry and exit records in your passport are used to verify this. The Japanese translation of a Taiwanese license must be prepared by the Taiwan-Japan Relations Association (JAF can also handle this), while translations for mainland Chinese licenses must be handled by JAF or other institutions designated by the National Police Agency and prefectural police. If the translation is not done correctly, the counter staff will not accept your application.

The application document stage also became stricter after October 2025. The National Police Agency now requires a juminhyo that includes specific details: nationality, status of residence, period of stay, and residence card number. The juminhyo must be issued within the last six months. In principle, those on short-term stays cannot apply. The Ibaraki Prefectural Police page also states that, with few exceptions, those on tourist or other short-term stays cannot obtain a license. The old practice of applying using a hotel address or a temporary stay certificate is no longer permitted.

If you live in Tokyo, follow the flow on the National Police Agency's website. Foreign license conversion is by appointment only. General applications can be made via the National Police Agency's online portal. The Fuchu and Samezu Driver's License Centers accept licenses from all countries, while the Koto Driver's License Center only accepts those from the 29 exempt countries and regions. Reception hours are weekdays from 8:30 AM to 3:00 PM, with a break from 11:00 AM to 1:00 PM. The skills test requires a separate appointment and cannot be taken on the same day, so factor that waiting time into your schedule. If you are from mainland China and need to retake the test, don't set your car rental or moving dates in stone; leave yourself some flexibility. You will have to wait in line.

Before you leave, print the PDF of required documents from the National Police Agency's website and check off each item. Remember to tell the office staff to include the specific details on your juminhyo; if you miss one thing, you will have to come back another day. For friends from Taiwan, the process is relatively simple; once you have the documents, it is mostly a matter of waiting in line. Friends from mainland China should prepare for a longer process: study the 50-question bank thoroughly and practice the on-course tasks at a driving school. The 42.8% and 13.1% figures are worth taping to your desk.

Quick summary

  • Taiwanese driver's licenses are on the list of 29 exempt countries and regions, so both the knowledge and skills tests are waived. Prepare your documents and Japanese translation according to the National Police Agency's list.
  • Mainland Chinese driver's licenses require both the knowledge and skills tests. The knowledge test requires 45 correct answers out of 50 (42.8% pass rate), and the skills test has a 13.1% pass rate.
  • Applications must include a juminhyo with specific details, issued within the last six months. In principle, those on short-term stays cannot apply.
  • In Tokyo, appointments are required. Fuchu and Samezu accept all countries, while Koto only accepts those on the exempt list. The skills test requires a separate appointment and cannot be taken on the same day.
  • After getting the license you must have stayed in the issuing country for a cumulative 3 months or more, this applies to both Taiwan and mainland China, so organize your passport entry and exit records early
