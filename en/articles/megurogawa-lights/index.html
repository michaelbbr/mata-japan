Meguro River's Winter Sakura lights up on December 4, with 2.2 kilometers of pink on both banks

Meguro River Minna no Illumination 2026: 59 days, approximately 410,000 lights, powered by biodiesel made from recycled cooking oil.

  • Period: December 4, 2026 (Fri) to January 31, 2027 (Sun), 59 days in total
  • Lighting: 17:00 to 22:00, free, no reservation required
  • Location: Shinagawa Ward Gotanda Community Waterside Plaza and along the Meguro River (2-9 Higashi-Gotanda, Shinagawa Ward, Tokyo), about a 6-minute walk from Osaki Station and Gotanda Station

The river crowded with people in spring will bloom once more in December. This time it is lights. The cherry trees along the Meguro River lose their leaves in winter, and pink LEDs are hung on their branches instead, extending from around Gotanda toward Osaki, about 2.2 kilometers counting both banks. This year the lights turn on on December 4 and stay lit until the end of January, with about 410,000 light bulbs, a little more than last year's 390,000.

This stretch of riverbank is, to begin with, many people's commuting route. At five in the evening, just as it gets dark and the lights come on, the whole row of trees on the way to the station is pink, and the river surface reflects a layer of pink as well. Wind blows in from the river carrying a hint of moisture, and with your hands in your pockets your steps slow down on their own. You do not need to plan a special trip to see the lights; you just look up as you pass after work, and that is a view only people who live nearby get.

🌸 The electricity for the lights comes from somewhere a little special. Used cooking oil from restaurants in this area is collected, made into biodiesel, and then used to generate electricity, and all the electricity for the illuminations comes this way. It has been done this way every year since 2010. There is a collection point in the plaza, and you can bring used oil from home in a plastic bottle, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Before you bring it, check the collection method and precautions officially announced. The thought that oil from my own home has become lights along the riverbank crosses my mind every time I pass by.

This year the plaza has an added light that moves with people. The creative team ゼロバイゼロ made a ripple-like installation; as people walk past, the light spreads along the water's edge and the walkway, on display until December 31. The lights themselves stay on until the end of January, so anyone who wants to see this installation needs to go before the end of the year.

The lights stay on until 10 at night, so if you detour after work there is still plenty of time. The wind by the river is stronger than on the street; standing on the bridge watching the reflections, your hands get cold quickly, so buy a hot drink near the station before walking over and hold it while you watch slowly. If you do not want to join the Christmas crowds, I would go on a weekday evening in January. The lights are still on, and you do not need to squeeze in at the same time as everyone else.

From the editor-in-chief

That used can of oil in my kitchen, I was going to solidify it and throw it away, but after learning that the Meguro River lights are powered by burning recycled oil, I stood in front of the sink and hesitated for a moment. In the end I still threw it away; I can never match the recycling point's hours. Other people have lit up 410,000 lights, and I can't even send out one can of oil.
