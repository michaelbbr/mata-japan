Japanese cabbage does not taste right stir-fried, it is originally a vegetable to be shredded and eaten raw

Spring vegetables have a lot of moisture and are suitable for eating raw; if you want to stir-fry, choose the firm winter kind

There's a bit of text. If you just want to know how to buy and how to stir-fry, scroll to the bottom and read the quick summary.

When you buy cabbage at a supermarket in Japan and stir-fry it at home, it comes out watery, with less wok aroma and sweetness, and many people's first reaction is to doubt their own cooking. I used to doubt myself that way too. Later, after buying it a few times and stir-frying it a few times, I realized the problem was the vegetable itself.

The stir-frying style Taiwanese people are used to calls for leaves that are thick, hold up to stir-frying, and get sweeter the more they are stir-fried. The cabbage commonly found in Japanese supermarkets goes in a different direction. What it does best is being shredded and eaten raw, that little mountain of shredded cabbage next to tonkatsu: crisp, juicy, and just right with a greasy main dish. Take that kind of cabbage and stir-fry it over high heat and the moisture comes out all at once; no matter how good your stir-frying is, the texture cannot be saved.

🥬 Cabbage in Japan also varies by season. Spring 春キャベツ has loose leaves, a soft texture, and lots of moisture, and is positioned for raw uses such as salads and light pickles. The 寒玉 type harvested in winter has firm, heavy leaves that hold their texture better after heating, and is clearly more suitable than spring cabbage for stir-frying and stewing. If you want to stir-fry a decent plate of Taiwanese-style cabbage in Japan, which season's cabbage you buy matters more than changing your pan.

For stir-frying, choose winter cabbage with firm leaves that feels heavy in the hand. Change the cutting method too: cut leaves into large pieces, shake off water before putting them in the pan, and don't keep flipping while stir-frying; let the edges fry until fragrant and browned before turning. If you buy spring cabbage, change how you eat it: shred it to go with pork cutlet, or mix it into a light pickle, it's much tastier.

Next time I want to stir-fry Taiwanese-style cabbage, I will look for firm-leaved winter types first. If I buy spring キャベツ, I will save it for a salad or as a side for fried pork cutlet.

Quick summary

  • For stir-frying, choose winter 寒玉 types: firm leaves, heavy in the hand
  • 春キャベツ has a lot of moisture; instead shred it and eat it raw, or make it into lightly pickled vegetables
  • Before stir-frying, cut into large pieces and shake off the moisture. Put it in the pan and let it sear until fragrant and browned before flipping
  • Decide whether you're stir-frying or eating it raw tonight, then choose that season's cabbage

Source materials

  • Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries statistics and explanations on vegetables: キャベツ are divided by harvest season into spring, summer-autumn, winter and other seasonal types; seasonal classification is not the same as variety classification
  • General cultivation and distribution classification summary: the spring type has soft leaves with high water content, suitable for salads, 千切り, and 淺漬; the 寒玉 type has thick, firm leaves, suitable for stir-fries, simmered dishes, and 大阪燒. The texture comparisons in this article are written according to this classification
