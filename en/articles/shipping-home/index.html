Sending a parcel from Japan back to Taiwan, China, or Hong Kong: which shipping method is cheaper?

At the same post office counter, Taiwan and China count as 'Zone 1' and Hong Kong counts as 'Zone 2,' so sending to Hong Kong is inherently a tier more expensive

Living in Japan, there are always times when you need to send things home: buying too much, clearing out for a move, sending to parents. But Japan Post's international mail is not charged by distance, it is charged by 地帯. Taiwan, China, and South Korea are in one group, Zone 1; Hong Kong, however, is placed in Zone 2 (other parts of Asia). The same box, at the same counter, sending to Hong Kong is more expensive than sending to Taipei or Shanghai.

📦 There are three main ways to send. If you are in a hurry or worried about loss, use EMS: fastest, with tracking throughout. If you are not in a hurry and want to save money, use 国際小包: about a week by air, cheap by surface mail but measured in months, both with tracking. The limit for both is 30 kg. For small items under 2 kg, you can consider 小形包装物. The basic sending method does not include tracking; if you need tracking, check what add-on services are available, or choose 国際エアパケット (formerly 国際eパケットライト, renamed in June 2026 and expanded to worldwide delivery). As for SAL, which only old hands know about, it is currently fully suspended; even though the price is still printed on the rate table, you cannot send by it, so do not make a wasted trip.

Looking directly at the cost (current rates, in yen; 2 / 5 / 10 kg): EMS to Taiwan and China 3,400 / 6,400 / 10,600, to Hong Kong 4,550 / 8,150 / 13,350. 国際小包 by air, Taiwan and China 2,750 / 4,850 / 8,350, Hong Kong 3,700 / 7,300 / 13,300. For Hong Kong, a 10 kg air parcel is only 50 yen cheaper than EMS, so in that case just upgrade to EMS. Surface mail is cheapest: Taiwan and China 2,200 / 3,400 / 5,400, Hong Kong 2,600 / 4,100 / 6,600. For small 2 kg items: 小形包装物 by air to Taiwan and China 2,250, Hong Kong 2,660; 国際エアパケット to Taiwan and China 2,620, Hong Kong 3,030 (tracking fee included).

Delivery times are based on official standard times (not guaranteed): EMS to Taiwan is about 3 days; for China and Hong Kong it depends on the recipient city; 国際小包 by air to Taiwan is about 6 days, to Hong Kong about 7 days, and to China (Shanghai, Beijing) about 8 days; surface mail requires patience, 1–2 months to Taiwan, about 1 month to Hong Kong, and about 3 weeks to 2 months to China, depending on the city.

Customs is the real hurdle. Taiwan: if the dutiable value of a postal parcel (price of goods plus shipping) is within NT$2,000, it is exempt from customs duty, commodity tax and business tax; above that, tax is charged on the full amount, and if the same recipient has had tax-exempt clearance more than 6 times in half a year, from the 7th time it is no longer exempt. When sending to Taiwan, remind the recipient to watch for real-name authentication for customs clearance: if the declaration method for this parcel requires EZ WAY 易利委, complete the authentication in the app first so it does not get stuck at declaration. China: the limit for personal postal articles entering the country is RMB 2,000 each time, and tax payable of RMB 50 or less is exempt; exceeding the limit may lead to return shipment or a switch to cargo declaration. Hong Kong is the simplest: as a free port it charges no import customs duty, and only four categories of dutiable commodities, liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils and methyl alcohol, are taxed, so everyday items sent there are basically tax-free.

When sending to Taiwan or China, meat and meat-containing foods are prohibited items that need special attention. 日本郵便's conditions table for each country lists related restrictions, and dried meat, pork floss, and instant noodles containing meat cannot be assumed to be sendable just because they are vacuum-packed. For Hong Kong's related restrictions, you need to check the destination conditions table separately. Before sending gifts to family, read the ingredient list carefully.

Two practical reminders. First, create the label at home first using the '国際郵便マイページサービス' online: the label, customs declaration (CN22 / CN23) and invoice are all generated by the system; print them out and take them to the post office, and 国際エアパケット in particular stipulates that only online labels can be used. Second, write the item name specifically and fill in the price truthfully: '衣類' is not as good as '3 cotton T-shirts'; writing it vaguely is what most easily gets it stuck at the other side's customs.

Finally, remember one point in time: 日本郵便 has announced that from February 1, 2027 it will adjust some international mail fees; for example, EMS to Taiwan and China for 2 kilograms will rise from 3,400 to 4,300. If you have plans to send something, sending it before the end of January at the current fee is the best value.

From the editor-in-chief

The first time I sent a parcel home, I stood at the post office counter and pushed the box over, and only then learned that postage is divided by 地帯. That sending to Hong Kong costs quite a bit more than sending to Taiwan is something I learned by watching the amount jump up. The person at the counter was very calm; I was the only one standing there surprised.
