Sending a parcel from Japan back to Taiwan, China, or Hong Kong: which shipping method is cheaper?

At the same post office counter, Taiwan and China count as 'Zone 1' and Hong Kong counts as 'Zone 2,' so sending to Hong Kong is inherently a tier more expensive

Living in Japan, there is always a time when you need to mail things home: buying too much, clearing out during a move, or sending things to your parents. However, Japan Post's international mail does not charge based on distance, but on 'zones.' Taiwan, China, and South Korea are in one group, counting as Zone 1; Hong Kong, however, is classified as Zone 2 (other parts of Asia). For the same box of items at the same counter, shipping to Hong Kong is more expensive than shipping to Taipei or Shanghai.

📦 There are three main ways to ship. If you're in a hurry or worried about loss, use EMS: it's the fastest and fully trackable. If you're not in a hurry and want to save money, use International Parcel Post: airmail takes about a week, while surface mail is cheap but takes months. Tracking depends on the shipping method and destination; airmail has tracking, but check the official website for surface mail before sending. Both have a 30kg limit. For small items under 2kg, consider Small Packet. Basic shipping doesn't include tracking, so if you need it, check for available add-on services or choose International e-Packet (renamed and expanded globally in June 2026). As for the SAL service that veterans used to know, it has been completely discontinued—don't waste your time even if you see prices on an old rate table.

Looking directly at the costs (current rates, in yen; 2/5/10 kg): EMS to Taiwan/China 3,400/6,400/10,600, to Hong Kong 4,550/8,150/13,350. International Parcel Airmail to Taiwan/China 2,750/4,850/8,350, to Hong Kong 3,700/7,300/13,300. A 10 kg air parcel to Hong Kong is only 50 yen cheaper than EMS, so just upgrade to EMS in that case. Surface mail is the cheapest: to Taiwan/China 2,200/3,400/5,400, to Hong Kong 2,600/4,100/6,600. For 2 kg small items: Small Packet Airmail to Taiwan/China 2,250, to Hong Kong 2,660; International e-Packet to Taiwan/China 2,620, to Hong Kong 3,030 (tracking fee included).

The number of days depends on the official standard (not guaranteed): EMS to Taiwan takes about 3 days; China and Hong Kong depend on the receiving city. International Parcel Airmail to Taiwan takes about 6 days, Hong Kong about 7 days, and China (Shanghai, Beijing) about 8 days. For surface mail, you need to be patient: Taiwan 1–2 months, Hong Kong about 1 month, and China about 3 weeks to 2 months, depending on the city.

Going through customs is the real hurdle. Taiwan: If the tax-inclusive value of a postal package (value of goods plus shipping) is within 2,000 NTD, it is exempt from customs duties, commodity tax, and business tax (this does not apply to tobacco, alcohol, or agricultural products subject to tariff quotas; items requiring inspection or quarantine must also follow separate regulations). If it exceeds this, it is taxed on the full amount. If the same recipient receives more than 6 tax-exempt shipments within six months, the 7th shipment onwards will no longer be tax-exempt. When mailing to Taiwan, remind the recipient to pay attention to real-name authentication for customs clearance: if the customs declaration method for this package requires 'EZ WAY', complete the authentication in the app first so it doesn't get stuck at customs. China: Personal postal items entering the country are limited to 2,000 RMB per shipment. If the tax payable is 50 RMB or less, it is exempt. Exceeding the limit may result in the package being returned or processed as commercial cargo. Hong Kong is the simplest: as a free port, there are no import duties. Only four categories of dutiable goods—liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol—are subject to tax. Daily necessities mailed there are basically tax-free.

When mailing to Taiwan or China, meat and meat-containing products are prohibited items that require special attention. Japan Post's country-specific condition tables list relevant restrictions. You cannot assume that beef jerky, meat floss, or instant noodles containing meat can be mailed just because they are vacuum-packed. For Hong Kong's relevant restrictions, you need to check the destination condition table separately. Before mailing gifts to family, read the ingredient list clearly.

Two practical reminders. First, create the shipping label online at home using the 'International Mail My Page Service': the label, customs declaration (CN22/CN23), and invoice are all generated by the system; just print them and take them to the post office. International Air Packets even require the use of online labels. Second, be specific about the item name and fill in the price truthfully: 'Clothing' is not as good as '3 Cotton T-shirts.' Vague descriptions are the easiest way to get stuck in the other country's customs.

One final point to note: Japan Post has announced that international postal rates will be adjusted starting February 1, 2027. For example, EMS shipping to Taiwan or China for a 2kg package will increase from 3,400 yen to 4,300 yen. If you have any plans to ship items, it is best to send them before the end of January to take advantage of the current rates.

From the editor-in-chief

The first time I sent a package home, I pushed the box across the post office counter and learned that shipping costs vary by zone. I only learned that shipping to Hong Kong is significantly more expensive than to Taiwan when I saw the price jump. The person at the counter was very calm; I was the only one surprised.
