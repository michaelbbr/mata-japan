Weekend outings · AYAKA · 2026.11.19

Walk slowly from Shinjuku, pass through Meiji Jingu to Harajuku, or take the other route through Yoyogi Park to Shibuya.

A walking route that doesn't require queuing for attractions, connecting the shade of the Jingu trees, the grass of the park, and the shopping districts on both sides.

When you don't have any special plans for the weekend, there is a path worth taking slowly. Start in Shinjuku, walk through Meiji Jingu to Harajuku, or wander through Yoyogi Park toward Shibuya. You can combine a stroll with some shopping without having to rush for the train or pack your schedule. This route has been walked many times for a simple reason: the city's noise fades under the shade of the trees, and by the time you reach the exit, you're right back into the bustling streets. Half a day passes just like that; your feet might be a bit sore, but your mind feels at ease.

Walking from Shinjuku Station toward Yoyogi, the streets gradually get quieter. Entering Meiji Jingu from the Sangubashi or Yoyogi gates, the gravel crunches underfoot, and the trees on both sides break the sunlight into cool patches on your shoulders. It's about a 10-minute walk from the entrance to the main shrine (honden). The approach (sando) is longer than you'd think; halfway through, you'll naturally start to slow down, and your breathing follows suit. No running is allowed on the grounds—someone learned this the hard way by almost sprinting in, getting spotted by a guard, and the embarrassing story is still told today. If you're there for a stroll, just walk. Pause before the torii gate, scoop some water at the temizuya, rinse your left hand then your right. The cool water wakes up your spirit—it's just right.

Plan your time carefully. Meiji Jingu's opening and closing times change with the sunrise and sunset each month. In November, it opens at 6:10 AM and closes at 4:10 PM; in December, it opens at 6:40 AM and closes at 4:00 PM. Always check the official website for the latest hours. Since it gets dark early in winter, if you're still wandering around Harajuku after 3:00 PM, it's usually too late to head into the shrine. This route is best if you visit the shrine first and save the shopping for later. If you head out in the morning, the air among the trees feels a bit damp, and there are few people on the path, making the sound of your footsteps on the gravel very clear.

Coming out of the Harajuku exit, Harajuku Station is right in front of you, and the atmosphere shifts instantly. The crowds on Takeshita Street, the display windows on Omotesando, and the small shops in the alleys—there's somewhere to pop into every few steps. The quiet you picked up in the Shrine still lingers, so your pace while shopping will be a bit slower than usual. If you want a sweet treat or to check out this season's clothes, wandering around this area before heading home is a perfect half-day walk and shopping trip combined; no extra planning needed.

Another option is to head toward Yoyogi Park. Located in Shibuya Ward, this metropolitan park covers about 540,000 square meters, is open 24/7, and is free to enter. It's about a 3-minute walk from Harajuku Station, Yoyogi-koen Station, or Meiji-jingumae Station. The park has vast lawns and groves where people picnic or practice instruments on weekends, with sounds drifting through the air without bothering anyone. Eating an onigiri on the grass with the warm sun on your knees is lovely. Once you've rested enough, walk toward Jinnan, and the Shibuya intersections will gradually appear. You can go shopping, have dinner, or just hop on a train home.

Both paths share the same starting point; the only difference is how you want to spend your time that day. If you want some quiet, go into the Jingu; if you want to lie on the grass, go to the park; if you want to shop, save your energy for Harajuku and Shibuya. In my life in Tokyo, this kind of route—no tickets, no reservations, just a pair of comfortable shoes—is the one I repeat most often on weekends. When I take my shoes off at home, I feel like I've truly had a good rest.

From the editor-in-chief

Last time I followed this route, I sat down on the grass in Yoyogi Park to eat an onigiri, stared at the trees after finishing, and by the time I snapped out of it, I hadn't visited a single shop in Shibuya and just headed home from Harajuku. This route is labeled as 'walking and shopping,' but for me, it always ends up being just 'walking'—the grass eats up the shopping half every time.
