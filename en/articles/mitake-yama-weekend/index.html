Weekend outings · AYAKA · 2026.11.21

There is a shrine at the summit of Mitake-san, and the cable car ride is only 6 minutes. Be sure to check the closure dates for December.

Mt. Mitake in Ome City is 929 meters high. There are shops along the approach and a shrine at the summit. If you're heading out in winter, be sure to check for any days the cable car might be out of service.

Mitake-san is still within Tokyo, yet it feels worlds away from the city. As the train heads west, the buildings outside the window gradually shrink, and the very scent of the air changes. When you step off the train, the first thing you smell is the scent of trees and earth. The mountain is in Ome City, at an elevation of 929 meters. Musashi Mitake Shrine is near the summit, and it only takes about 6 minutes to get up by cable car, leaving a perfect amount of hiking for the rest of the way.🌿

The cable car runs from Takimoto Station to Mitake-san Station. The car sways gently, and the greenery outside the window recedes in layers. Fares were revised on October 1, 2026: 700 yen one-way for adults (1,400 yen round-trip) and 350 yen for children (700 yen round-trip). You don't have to complete the round trip on the same day; you have 2 days to use the ticket. It is still recommended to check the official Mitake Tozan Railway website for the latest prices before you leave, as revised figures are often incorrectly written in older articles.

The approach path after getting off the train is an uphill slope with shops on both sides, so it's not boring to walk. Someone once told me that many mountains in Japan are like this—shops at the foot and a shrine at the top—making them much more entertaining to climb than mountains in Taiwan. Mt. Mitake feels exactly like this. With shops selling hot food and souvenirs along the way, and the scent of steam mixed with wood drifting by, your pace naturally slows down. Take a look around before deciding whether to buy anything.

On the way to the shrine, you will pass the Mitake Visitor Center, about a 10-minute walk from the station. It's a good place to stop if you want to check trail conditions or the latest updates from the mountain. The shrine is further up; it takes about 25 minutes from Mitake-san Station to Musashi Mitake Shrine. The final stretch consists of 368 stone steps, worn smooth and shiny by countless shoes. As you climb slowly while holding the handrail, your breathing will become increasingly audible. Once you reach the top, the air turns cool and the shrine hall appears before you—suddenly, the fatigue of the climb feels worth it.

Pick your timing carefully. The cable car will be closed on Wednesday, December 9, and Thursday, December 10, 2026, for overhead wire replacement. On the 9th, it won't be closed all day—only from the first morning train until 8:40 AM. On the 10th, it will be closed all day. It will also be closed from January 18 to 20, 2027, for inspection of the winding machine. On the 18th, it will only operate until the 8:40 AM departure, and it will be closed all day on the 19th and 20th. These are all weekdays, so if you're taking time off to go, check the official website announcement again before you head out for peace of mind.

One last tip for the approach path: it's hard to buy drinks near the summit, so remember to buy water near the path or the station. Keep a bottle in your backpack's side pocket so you won't get thirstier as you climb the stone steps. The air on Mt. Mitake in autumn is dry, and your lips will feel tight quickly. Having water on hand gives you peace of mind. These small details are the easiest to forget, yet the most uncomfortable when you do.

If you still have energy, take a walk toward the Rock Garden in the valley. It's about 1.5 kilometers long, with moss and rocks lining the stream, and the sound of water following you the whole way. The light by the stream is filtered through the leaves, falling in patches onto the wet stones. The air is cool and carries a hint of moss—a different world from the bustle of the shrine approach. The stones by the water are slippery, so shoes with flat soles will struggle there. Wear shoes with good grip, and don't worry if your pant cuffs get a little wet—just take your time. When you take the cable car down, the car sways gently, and the green outside the window slowly turns back into the colors of the city streets. That's how a day in the mountains ends, and when you get home, your shoes will still have a little dirt on them.

From the editor-in-chief

368 stone steps—just looking at this number makes my legs feel weak. When going to places like Mt. Mitake, my most common mistake is forgetting to check for service suspensions. Arriving at Takimoto Station only to find the cable car closed is an embarrassing scenario. Checking the official website announcement the night before is now my standard survival routine.
