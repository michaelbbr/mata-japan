Three things you need to do before taking your dog for a walk in Tokyo

Registration, rabies vaccination, license tags, and vaccination tags—see all the official Tokyo rules at once, including train crate dimensions.

For those who own cats, seeing neighbors walking their dogs often makes it seem like owning a dog just means adding a leash and a bag for picking up waste. Only after you actually move to Tokyo do you realize that once you have a dog, you also take on a new administrative routine. The Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Social Welfare and Public Health website is very clear: for dogs over 91 days old, you must register them with your local ward or city office within 30 days of starting to keep them. This is a one-time process, after which you will receive a dog license tag (kansatsu). 🐕

The second thing is the rabies vaccination. For dogs over 91 days old, you must get the rabies vaccine and receive a vaccination certificate within 30 days of starting to keep them. For dogs that have already been vaccinated, you must get a booster every year between April 1 and June 30. This period will change in 2027; the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare has announced an amendment to the enforcement regulations. Starting March 2, 2027, vaccinations can be given year-round, though the annual requirement remains, and the April to June period will become an intensive vaccination period. Both the license tag and the vaccination certificate must be attached to the dog. The authorities also noted that these two tags serve as identification if the dog gets lost; those two metal pieces on the collar are much more than just decorations.

You must also report moving or changing owners. There are statutory reporting requirements for moving, changing the owner's address, or transferring ownership of a registered dog, and you must also file a death report if the dog passes away. No one usually reminds you of these procedures, and when people miss them, it's usually because they are halfway through the stress of moving or are asked about it by a clerk while taking their dog for a vaccination, only to realize their records are still at their old address.

Microchips are another point of confusion. Since June 2022, dogs and cats sold by pet shops and breeders are legally required to be microchipped before sale, and owners must register their information with a designated registration agency. Some municipalities have adopted a special microchip system where completing the microchip registration is considered equivalent to dog registration, replacing the physical license tag. Check the announcements for your specific ward or city. For dogs that are already microchipped, some municipalities allow you to complete change-of-ownership procedures online; details depend on your local municipal office. Keep the document with the microchip number provided by the shop when you bought your dog, as you will need it for future updates.

When taking a dog on the train, the rules shift to JR East's policy. The dog must be placed entirely inside a dedicated crate or bag, with no part of its head sticking out. The total dimensions of the crate (length + width + height) must be within 120 cm, and the combined weight of the dog and crate must be under 10 kg. You must purchase a 290 yen 'hand luggage' ticket at the station gate before boarding. Holding the dog in your arms or just using a leash is not allowed. For medium-sized dogs and larger, the 10 kg limit is an immediate dealbreaker; for those living in Tokyo with a dog, your transportation options are half-decided the moment you choose your breed.

Finding a place is another hurdle. Even if a listing says 'pets allowed,' you still need to check the contract for limits on the number of pets, their size, and breed; some places even require an additional security deposit. These conditions vary by property and there is no standard figure. It is much easier to provide your dog's details directly to the agent for confirmation before signing than to be reminded of restrictions after you've moved in. Leashes and waste bags are daily trifles, but registration, vaccinations, and crate dimensions are the big issues written on official websites. You need to handle both to truly say your dog is living in Tokyo.

Quick summary

  • For dogs over 91 days old, register with your local ward or city office within 30 days of starting to keep the dog. This is a one-time registration to receive the license tag.
  • The first rabies vaccination must be given within 30 days of starting to keep the dog, followed by an annual shot between April and June. Both the vaccination certificate and the license tag must be attached to the dog. Starting March 2, 2027, vaccinations can be given year-round, though the annual requirement remains.
  • You must report moving, changing owners, or the death of your dog; don't let your records stay at your old address
  • Dogs and cats sold after June 2022 are microchipped. Owner registration and updates must be handled through the designated registration agency.
  • Taking dogs on JR East trains: The total dimensions of the carrier (length + width + height) must be within 120 cm, the total weight including the dog must be under 10 kg, and the dog's head must be fully covered. A 290 yen pet fare ticket is required.

From the editor-in-chief

With the license, vaccination tag, and microchip all taken care of, my dog is administratively more of a proper resident than I am, Choko. I usually just carry a fanny pack when I go out, but when my dog goes out, she has two tags hanging from her collar, and if she gets lost, there's a microchip to track the owner. Fine, it's not embarrassing to lose to a dog. She remembers to update those two tags every year, while my fanny pack has never been organized that diligently.
