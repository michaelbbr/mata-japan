Administrative reminders for next week (YU)

December 31 is the payment deadline for this year's Furusato Nozei. To count for this year, you generally need to complete your payment by December 31. Credit cards and mobile payments usually work until the day of, but deadlines for bank transfers, registered mail (現金書留), or counter payments vary by municipality. If you're in a hurry, check the announcements for each municipality first and don't wait until the last day. After sending the money, there's a second date: the ワンストップ特例 application form must reach the municipality by January 10, 2027. It only counts if it arrives by then. The ワンストップ特例 applies to those who don't normally need to file a final tax return (確定申告) and have donated to 5 or fewer municipalities in a year. If you later file a 確定申告 for reasons like medical expense deductions, your ワンストップ特例 will become invalid, so you must include your Furusato Nozei in that filing. For those donating after mid-December, the application forms often don't arrive in time, so print them yourself from the Ministry of Internal Affairs or municipal websites. The ban on using point rewards to attract donors started last October, so there's no need to wait for year-end campaigns.

The schedule for the December monthly applications for Toei Housing will be announced around December 5, with the application period running from mid-December to the end of the month. This month's opening is for those planning to marry, where all family members in the application must be under 40. This includes engaged couples, couples married within the last six months, and families with eligible children. Certain common-law marriages and partnerships are also included. In addition to age and marital status, there are other requirements regarding residency, income, and housing need, so meeting those two criteria alone isn't enough. There is no monthly application for singles in December; the next one is in January. The lottery is at the end of next month, and if you win, you still have to pass a qualification screening. Actual move-in is usually six to eight months after applying. Companies offering to handle the application for you have no connection to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government or the Tokyo Metropolitan Housing Supply Corporation, and paying them won't make winning the lottery any easier.

Weekend activities and shopping (AYAKA)

The Rikugien Night Viewing runs from November 25 to December 6, from 6:00 PM to 8:30 PM (last entry at 7:30 PM). The park closes once at 5:00 PM, and the evening requires a separate ticket—daytime tickets won't get you in for the lights. Online advance tickets are 1,000 yen, while same-day tickets are 1,200 yen (cash only at the counter). Children elementary school age and younger are free. Advance tickets went on sale November 1, with a daily limit on the number of tickets sold. This year features new light projections on the storehouse (土蔵) and bamboo grove. The lights hit the maples by the pond, reflecting the red leaves in the water. Your shoulders might get chilly if you stand still for long. According to Weather News data from November 24, only about 20% of the park had changed color then, with the peak predicted around December 9—which is after the night light-up ends. If you go this weekend, you'll see the early stages of the colors. If you want to see the whole park in full red, check the latest photos on the Rikugien official website before you go. 🍁

If you want to shop during the day, there are two markets at the United Nations University (UNU) plaza in Aoyama on the same weekend. The Farmers Market is held every Saturday and Sunday from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM, with farmers and food producers from all over Japan setting up stalls with seasonal vegetables and processed goods. Next to it is the 89th Aoyama Weekly Antique Market, featuring a wide array of vintage items, old clothes, and trinkets. The best way to enjoy it is to pick things up, feel the quality, and ask the owners about their history. It's about a 5-minute walk from Omotesando Station Exit B2. Last week, someone walked two kilometers just to compare corn dogs from three different convenience stores, only to forget all the differences.

Another daytime spot is Shinjuku Gyoen, where the autumn leaves stay vibrant from late November through mid-December. The maple trees by the pond in the Japanese garden are a highlight. Admission is 500 yen for adults, and it's open from 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM (last entry at 4:00 PM). Note that November 30 is a Monday, and the park will be closed.
