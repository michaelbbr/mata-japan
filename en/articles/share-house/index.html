I signed just from photos and slept on a share house floor for a week

The room was so small it couldn't fit a single bed, and I even mistook the water heater for being broken.

The story of what went wrong is a bit long; if you only want the conclusion, there is a quick summary at the end, just scroll down.

When I first moved to Japan, I set my sights on a share house. I didn't visit in person and signed the contract based only on photos online. The day I moved in, I laid my suitcase down and the entryway was already full; I had to turn sideways just to squeeze in. When I pushed open the bedroom door, it was as dim as twilight inside. I measured it and the room was so small that a single bed wouldn't fit no matter how I arranged it. Thankfully, I had a sleeping bag in my luggage, so I slept on the floor for that first week. It was winter, and the cold from the floor seeped through the sleeping bag. I woke up several times in the middle of the night, thinking only one thing: you really can't trust photos.

The water heater was the second trap. I only had cold water when I showered, and I thought the machine was broken. I forced myself to take cold showers in the middle of winter until I was shivering. Later, I realized the machine wasn't broken at all; I just didn't know how to turn it on. There is a remote control panel for the water heater on the bathroom wall. You have to turn on the power and set the temperature for the hot water to come out. The instructions were posted right next to it, but I didn't even understand them at the time. It still gives me the chills thinking about it.

🏠 Photos are taken from flattering angles; open a wide-angle lens and a small room can look like a ball field. Equipment systems get replaced, and the way you were used to operating things in Taiwan may not work in a share house in Japan. I learned both of these things in my first week. Now when friends ask me, I tell them this: if you can view the property in person, do so; if you cannot go yourself, at least ask someone to stand inside the room for you and take a photo without wide angle. Before signing, ask clearly for the bed size, in numbers, not by feel. On your first day after moving in, sort out the water heater, gas, and garbage sorting before unpacking your luggage; if the order is reversed, what awaits you that night is a cold shower on the floor.

The first week after moving into a new place is the busiest. Ask clearly about dimensions before signing, and on moving-in day first confirm the water, electricity, gas, and water heater switches, so at least at night you do not end up on the floor with a cold shower like me.

Quick summary

  • If you can do a viewing, do a viewing; if you can't go, ask someone to stand in the room and take a photo without wide-angle
  • Whether a bed will fit: ask for the size numbers directly before signing the contract
  • On your first day after moving in, figure out the water heater, gas, and garbage sorting before organizing your luggage
  • Photos are all about angles. A wide-angle lens can make a small room look like a stadium

The first thing I did at dawn was measure the room to see if it could fit a bed. Both types of bad luck are things you remember for a lifetime.
