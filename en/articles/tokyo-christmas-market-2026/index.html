This year's Tokyo Christmas Market is not at Meiji Jingu Gaien. It has moved to Shin-Toyosu and Shiba Park

Shin-Toyosu runs from November 20 to December 25, 2026, and Shiba Park from December 4 to December 25. Adult admission starts at 1,000 yen on weekdays; check the official announcement for actual prices

Live in Tokyo long enough and your feet learn the way on their own. When December comes, they drift toward Meiji Jingu Gaien, assuming the Christmas market must be lit up somewhere around there. In 2026 that habit needs adjusting. The Tokyo Christmas Market will not be held at Meiji Jingu Gaien this year; the only venues on the official list are Shin-Toyosu and Shiba Park. Our editors actually know the Gaien area well, so well that someone nearly started running inside the Meiji Jingu grounds and caught a guard's attention, a story that still gets told. Head to Gaien out of habit this year and there will be no market, just cold wind under the ginkgo trees.

The Shin-Toyosu venue opens earlier, from November 20 to December 25, 2026, at 6-4 Toyosu, Koto Ward. Regular hours are 11:00 to 21:30, last order at 21:00 and last entry at 20:30, with the first day starting at 16:00. It is about a 2-minute walk from Shin-Toyosu Station on the Yurikamome line. The bay side is windy. Standing at a stall in the evening waiting for a hot drink, steam drifting into your face and the cup warming your palms, the cold slowly eases a little.

The Shiba Park venue starts two weeks later, from December 4 to December 25, 2026, at Shiba Park Section 4, the plaza in front of Onarimon Station. Regular hours are 11:00 to 22:00, last order at 21:30 and last entry at 21:00, with the first day also starting at 16:00. The nearest exit is Exit A6 of Onarimon Station on the Toei Mita Line, about a 1-minute walk. The official page also specifically notes that Shiba Park Station is not the nearest station; getting off at the wrong one means extra walking. The appeal here is simple: look up while you wander and Tokyo Tower is right there above the treetops, and once the lights come on, the tower's orange and the stalls' warm glow overlap, and any photo looks like a postcard.

Ticket prices are the same at both venues; the official announcement governs. Adults, meaning junior high school students and older, pay 1,000 yen on weekdays, 1,500 yen on weekends and holidays, and 2,000 yen on December 24 and 25. Elementary school children pay 500 yen, or 1,000 yen on December 24 and 25, and preschoolers are free. 🎄 Going after work on a weekday versus making a special weekend trip is a 500 yen difference, a small calculation Tokyo residents can factor in when planning.

Which one to stop at after work comes down to where your office and home are. If you work in central Tokyo, Shiba Park stays open until 22:00, so even after working past 19:00 there is time for a glass of mulled wine. If you live on the bay side or in eastern Tokyo, Shin-Toyosu is close to home and a slow weekend daytime stroll is enough. Neither venue has parking or bicycle parking, as the official pages state clearly, so the train is the easiest way.

It is time to fix the calendar. Cross out Gaien, write in Shin-Toyosu or Shiba Park, confirm the dates on the official page, and pick an evening when you are not too tired. Cinnamon and mulled wine in the cold air, wooden huts in a row, light falling on the rim of your cup. Spending a winter evening like that feels just right.

From the editor-in-chief

When a venue changes, the first person caught out is usually me. Last year I stood under the ginkgo trees at Gaien holding an empty cup, waiting for the market lights, and only realized, once my hands were numb, that I was remembering a venue from an even earlier year. I have learned this year. Before going out, I check the venues and dates in this article twice each, and save the thinking until I reach Shiba Park.
