Bicycle blue tickets are in effect: that moment of using your phone while riding costs 12,000 yen

From April 1, 2026, applies to ages 16 and older, fines from 3000 to 12000 yen, amounts verified against the National Police Agency's bicycle rules handbook

This article has a lot of numbers; if you are short on time, scroll straight to the quick summary at the end, where the amounts and age thresholds are, and you can come back and read slowly later.

Rushing out in the morning, riding a bicycle to the station, your phone vibrates in your pocket, and many people reach for it. From April 1, 2026, if you take out your phone and talk while riding, or stare at the screen, the cost may be 12000 yen. Bicycles have been included in the traffic violation notification system, commonly known as the blue ticket, and if a rider aged 16 or over commits a listed violation and it is established, they must pay a 反則金, with amounts from 3000 yen to 12000 yen, covering a total of 113 types of behavior. It has nothing to do with whether you have a driver's license; riding a mamachari to buy groceries, commuting by road bike, or riding to take a child to nursery school are all governed by the same system.

🚲 The old system for handling bicycle violations was awkward. Previously, bicycles were not part of the fine system, so if a violation was reported it went straight to the criminal procedure of a red ticket; the procedure was heavy, and once sent to prosecutors it often ended in non-prosecution, with the result that people could neither be fined nor deterred. The blue ticket fills in this middle part: pay the fine within the deadline and the case ends, without a criminal trial and without leaving a criminal record. That deadline is within 7 days starting the day after you receive the blue ticket; take the payment slip to a bank or post office to pay. People under 16 are not subject to blue tickets and in principle are still handled with guidance and warnings; high school students aged 16 and over are held to the same standard as adults, which families with high school students should explain especially clearly.

The amounts follow the official table, picking the items residents are most likely to encounter. Talking on a phone held in your hand while cycling or staring at the screen is 12000 yen, the most expensive tier in the whole table. Forcing through a level crossing after the barrier has come down is 7000 yen. Running a red light is 6000 yen, and passage classification violations such as riding against traffic on the right side of the road or cycling on a sidewalk without a bicycle passage sign are also 6000 yen. Failing to stop at an intersection where you should, riding at night without lights, cycling with an umbrella or wearing earphones so you cannot hear surrounding sounds, and other violations of public safety commission compliance matters are all 5000 yen, and poor maintenance such as broken brakes is also 5000 yen. Riding with a passenger and riding side by side are 3000 yen. Riding with a child for drop-off and pick-up has a separate exception for legally compliant seats; 幼児用座椅 that meet the specifications are not within the passenger-riding scope discussed here, check the details against the official rulebook.

The everyday picture of enforcement is stated clearly by officials. General violations are mainly handled with guidance and warnings on the spot. The blue ticket is for malicious and dangerous riding, such as forcing pedestrians to stop suddenly, forcing other vehicles to brake suddenly, or continuing to ride after being warned. Riding after drinking and riding while drunk are more serious; these two are not in blue ticket scope and go directly to the red ticket criminal process, and using a phone while riding in a way that endangers traffic is the same. So the introduction of blue tickets does not mean police will now stand at every intersection issuing tickets; it means those truly dangerous ways of riding can now actually be fined.

There is another very practical reminder. Tokyo's official page specifically warns that scams have appeared in which fake police collect money on site under the name of the blue ticket; real police do not collect 反則金 at the scene of an enforcement action, 反則金 is paid by taking the 納付書 to a bank or post office. If someone on the street asks you to hand over cash on the spot, call 110 first.

Life goes on as usual, just change a few riding habits. Put your phone in your bag; if you need to look at a map, stop and pull over to look; before going out at night, press your bike light once; at that stop sign at the intersection, put your foot down and stop fully. These are originally things you should do for safe cycling, and after the blue ticket came into effect, some violations also have clear 反則金, do the math, and the money for one phone ticket is enough to buy a very good bike light plus a phone holder.

Quick summary

  • From April 1, 2026, the blue ticket system applies to bicycle riders aged 16 and older, including high school students who have turned 16
  • Using a phone while cycling 12000 yen, forcing a level crossing 7000 yen, running a red light and riding against traffic 6000 yen
  • Failing to stop momentarily, not turning on lights, riding with an umbrella, and faulty brakes: 5000 yen; riding with a passenger and riding side by side: 3000 yen
  • If you pay the 反則金 within the deadline, the case is closed and leaves no criminal record; cycling after drinking is not covered by the blue ticket and goes through the red-ticket criminal process
  • Police will not collect money on the spot; someone at the roadside asking you to pay cash on the spot is a scam, call 110
