A 9-square-meter room in Tokyo: you only know what "just right" means once you live in it

The area on advertisements is calculated by 壁芯, and the actually usable area is even smaller; bringing a tape measure when viewing a property is more useful than looking at photos

If you are in a hurry to head out, scroll straight down; the quick summary at the end collects everything you can follow step by step.

Units in the 9-square-meter range have always been listed on Tokyo rental sites. They look interesting in photos, but before actually living in one, you need to think about whether your things will fit. How big is that, roughly? In rental listings, 1 畳 is calculated as 1.62 square meters or more, but a unit of just over 9 square meters usually also includes the bathroom, toilet, and a mini kitchen. The space actually available for a bed, a desk, and luggage is even smaller than the figure on paper. One single bed, one small desk, and once you open a suitcase, there is nowhere left to stand.

The rent gap is so large it feels like two cities. In アットホーム's listing rent statistics, the average listing rent for apartments under 30 square meters in Tokyo's 23 wards in September 2026 was 114,460 yen, a record high month after month. At the same time, on エイブル's property page, a 9.91-square-meter 1R in Hon-Komagome, Bunkyo Ward, was listed at 35,000 yen per month, management fee 5,000 yen, a wooden older apartment, registered on November 11, 2026. But the two sets of figures cannot be compared directly. The 114,460 yen is the overall average for properties under 30 square meters, while 35,000 yen is the asking price for a single small older property. Age, facilities, and actual area differ a lot, so you cannot judge which is better value just by rent. The price of cheap is written in the conditions: old age, small area, basic facilities; the rent you save is traded for living space.

The area figures themselves also have a catch. The exclusive area shown in rental listings is calculated by 壁芯, measured from the center line of the wall thickness; the range people can actually use is the 内法 inside the walls, and registration and real feel are both based on 内法, with industry common knowledge putting 内法 about 5% to 8% smaller than 壁芯. For a normal home that difference is imperceptible, but for a unit of around 9 square meters the difference is about 0.7 square meters, roughly half a tatami mat, and whether a storage box fits comes down to that half mat. When viewing a property, measure the inner width of the wall where the bed will go, and you will have a sense of it against the listing figure.

🏠 Living in this kind of layout depends on three things taking turns giving way. For sleeping, use the ロフト loft, move the mattress up there, and free up the floor for a desk and chair; the cost is climbing a ladder every day, and in summer heat builds up above, so the loft is stuffier than the floor. Some small properties use a three-point ユニットバス where the bathroom, washbasin, and toilet share one space. Space to move while bathing is limited, so when viewing it is best to check the shower position and how it drains. If there are no closets for storage, stack things upward, using walls, the backs of doors, and under the bed; once you have a lot of things it overflows, and people who live in rooms like this usually pause for three seconds before buying something, thinking about where it will go.

Who this suits is very clear. People with little luggage, who come home only to sleep and bathe, whose company or school is within walking distance or one train ride, and who spend the money saved on rent outside, can live this way very smoothly. People who work from home all day should think twice; the bed is right next to the desk, you finish a meeting and fall straight asleep, work and rest stick together, and after a few months you go dull. People who invite friends over, like to cook, and have a lot of clothes and books should not be lured away by low rent; after living there a while you may want to move, and once moving costs and initial fees are counted back, you have not saved anything at all.

Do one extra thing on viewing day: stand in the center of the room and turn in a circle. The walls your hands can reach are your whole world. If it still feels okay after that, then sign.

Quick summary

  • The advertised area is 壁芯, the actual usable area is by 内法, about 5% to 8% smaller; at the 9-square-meter level, the difference is about half a tatami mat
  • The average advertised rent for apartments of 30 square meters or less in Tokyo's 23 wards is 114,460 yen (September 2026). Older properties in the 9-square-meter class can be brought down to around 40,000 yen, depending on age and facilities
  • Sleep in the ロフト above, leave the floor for a desk and chairs; for a three-piece ユニットバス, check the shower position and drainage first
  • It's not suitable for people who work from home, often invite friends over, or have a lot of belongings; the rent you save will be eaten up by moving costs

From the editor-in-chief

When I was viewing properties I brought a tape measure, and the agent watched me measure the walls with a slightly awkward expression. Only after measuring did I realize the numbers in the listing were not quite the same as the space I could actually stand in. Since then, when I look at property photos, I first look at where a suitcase would go; if it does not fit, it does not count.
