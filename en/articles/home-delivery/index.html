Came home from work to another delivery notice? Do not miss the holding periods for Japanese deliveries

Once you have set up 'okihai' (leaving packages at the door), you can come home, open the door, and your package will already be waiting for you in the entrance.

A little after seven in the evening, after swaying on the train all the way home, still carrying a convenience store bag in hand, you open the mailbox and an absence notification slip is lying inside. The driver has long gone. For office workers living in Japan, this scene plays out several times every month.

When you get a delivery notice, calling to schedule a redelivery is the standard response. You can also do it online or via LINE, but scanning the QR code on the notice is the fastest way. What fewer people pay attention to is the date; packages aren't held at the branch for long. Yamato Transport's (Kuroneko) standard storage period is 7 days including the first delivery attempt, while Cool TA-Q-BIN is only 3 days. Sagawa Express holds packages for 8 days including the first attempt (4 days for Cool Express), and this cannot be extended. For special items like cash-on-delivery, the storage policy might differ, so check the delivery notice for details. Japan Post generally holds standard packages for 7 days. Since calculation methods and terms vary by carrier, the most accurate deadline is always on the delivery notice or the official tracking page. Once the deadline passes, the package is returned to the sender. If it's an online purchase, you can try to negotiate with the seller again, but if it's something from home or official documents, you'll be responsible for the extra shipping costs and time to have them resent.

By the time I saw the ticket it was already past 7 p.m., and if you want to arrange delivery again that same day, it's usually too late. With Kuroneko, applying online or by automated voice, the cutoff for same-day delivery is around 5:40 p.m., varying by area. 7 to 9 p.m. is a time when people are more likely to be home after work, so many office workers may want to choose that slot. But if you've already missed the application cutoff that day, it's more practical to reschedule for the next day.

I have switched to okihai (leaving packages at the door). Since June 2024, Yamato Transport members can specify okihai for regular Takkyubin and Takkyubin Compact deliveries. Once the shipping notification pops up, you can select the location in the app—front door, delivery box, gas meter box, garage, or storage room. You can even choose to have the building manager receive it. You don't have to wait at home. Refrigerated/frozen items, cash-on-delivery, and items requiring payment upon delivery are not eligible; those still require a signature. Some bank cards or official mail like 'simple registered mail' cannot be left as regular okihai. These usually require face-to-face delivery to confirm the recipient, depending on the type of mail.

Apartment residents face another hurdle. In buildings with auto-lock main entrances, the delivery driver cannot enter the lobby, so the package cannot reach your door. Some building management bylaws explicitly prohibit leaving packages in the hallway. If the delivery locker is full, the driver can only take the package back. If your apartment is not convenient for unattended delivery, check if the package can be redirected to a convenience store or use a PUDO delivery locker. Not all packages support this, so it is safer to check the delivery company's notification or app first. Pick it up on your way home and you won't have to race against the clock.

📦 According to a Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism survey, the redelivery rate in April 2024 was about 10.4%, meaning one out of every ten packages required a second trip. The government set a goal to reduce this to 6%. News about driver shortages has been around for years. My habit is simple: as soon as the shipping notification pops up, I change the delivery time, or if I am too busy, I choose 'okihai' (leave at the door). When I get home and check the mailbox, it is clean, with no missed delivery slip.

Quick summary

  • Yamato Transport holds items for 7 days including the first day, but Cool TA-Q-BIN is only 3 days. Sagawa Express holds items for 8 days (4 days for Cool Express) and cannot be extended. Japan Post holds items for 7 days (based on the delivery attempt notice and official website).
  • Online applications for same-day redelivery: Kuroneko cutoff is around 17:40, varies by area
  • Yamato Transport members can specify unattended delivery (okihai), which is also available for regular TA-Q-BIN; it is not available for refrigerated/frozen items, cash on delivery, or payment collection.
  • If the apartment auto-lock keeps drivers out and the delivery box is full, having it sent to a convenience store near your company or a PUDO locker is more reliable

From the editor-in-chief

My delivery notice once lay in my mailbox until it expired, and the parcel was sent back. The sender was my family back home, and my mother called to ask if I had moved. Now I've learned to photograph the slip as soon as I see it; the deadline on it is far more reliable than my memory.
