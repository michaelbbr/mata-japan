Bank accounts and mobile numbers waiting on each other, how to order your first week after landing

The back of the residence card does not have an address yet, so you cannot proceed with banks or mobile carriers. If you get the order right, you can settle your account and phone number in the first week.

If you are short on time, the summary at the end of the article has the order sorted out; just scroll down and follow it.

A room in your first week after landing usually does not look great. The share house I lived in back then was signed from photos alone, and when I got there the room was so dark I needed the light on in daytime, a single bed would not fit, and I slept in a sleeping bag for a week, still cold through the bag in winter. I did not know how to turn on the water heater, assumed it was broken, and gritted my teeth through cold showers. Everything piled up in those days, the bank, a phone number, the ward office, and every counter told me something was missing. Looking back, what those days lacked was a list with the order worked out.

It is a situation where the bank and the mobile carrier are waiting for each other, which many people encounter upon arrival. Opening a bank account requires a residence card, and if the address field on the back is empty, the counter staff can only ask you to fill it in and come back. During the application process and subsequent online banking verification, many banks require a Japanese phone number to receive SMS verification codes; without a number, the process stops halfway. It is the same for mobile carriers; signing a contract requires identity verification. According to the list of documents in the Mobile Phone Improper Use Prevention Act compiled by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, the residence card is a valid form of official identification, but if the card information does not match, online verification will not pass. Neither side is wrong. Both sides are waiting for the other.

Your address is the foundation for everything. The Immigration Services Agency of Japan has clear rules: new mid-to-long-term residents must report their address to their local city, ward, town, or village office within 14 days of establishing residency. This is what everyone calls a tenshutsutodoke (moving-in notification). Once processed, your address is printed on the back of your residence card, and your juminhyo (certificate of residence) is officially created. According to the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, once you have a juminhyo, you are assigned a My Number, and the notification is mailed to the address on your record. If that address field is empty, every form you fill out later will be incomplete.

The 'six-month residency' rule also comes up at bank counters. Under the Foreign Exchange Act and bank practices, those who have been in Japan for less than 6 months are often treated as non-residents. Some banks only offer restricted accounts, while others decline applications outright; policies vary significantly. The English account opening page for Seven Bank is the most specific, stating that the condition for foreigners is living in Japan for over 6 months or having at least 3 months remaining on their period of stay. However, these are just the basic conditions listed; whether you can actually open an account depends on the bank's review. If you are not employed or a student, don't assume you qualify—prepare your documents according to the official page before applying. Japan Post Bank (Yucho) has a different approach: they check your residence card for nationality, status of residence, and period of stay. If your period of stay expires within 3 months of your application date, you must wait until you have completed the renewal process. Since there are many post offices, 📮 Yucho is relatively easy for newcomers to approach, but whether you can open an account still depends on the confirmation at each counter. Also, keep this date in mind: for the new residence cards issued from June 14, 2026, Seven Bank has announced that ATMs cannot yet process account openings; this is scheduled to begin on December 22, 2026. If you have the new card, please apply online during this period rather than going to an ATM only to find you can't open an account.

The order of operations is actually quite simple. Once you have filed your moving-in notification and have a residence card with your address on the back, go sign up for a mobile phone plan. You can check both budget SIM providers and major carriers; prepare the identity verification documents listed on their official websites. Once you have your phone number, go to the bank to open an account. Prioritize Yucho Ginko and Seven Bank, as they clearly publish their procedures for foreigners. After your My Number notification arrives, you can gradually sign up for services that require your individual number, such as certain online banking features or international remittances. If your company requires a specific bank for salary deposits that isn't one of these two, bring your residence card, mobile phone, and juminhyo; you will have almost everything the counter needs.

Before you leave, flip your residence card over and take a look. If the address is there, follow the sequence above for your phone and bank account. You can get more done in your first week than you might think. The lesson from that week is practical: if you get the order of procedures right, your life won't be in chaos.

Quick summary

  • Go to the city or ward office as soon as you arrive to file your moving-in notification. Once your address is on the back of your residence card and your juminhyo is established, you have the foundation needed for a phone plan and a bank account.
  • The deadline for reporting your address is within 14 days of settling there, the counter is the municipal office, it cannot be done at immigration
  • When signing up for a phone plan, bring your residence card with your address. Prepare identity verification documents according to each carrier's official website. If the address on your card is missing, online verification will get stuck.
  • For banking, start by asking at Yucho Ginko or Seven Bank, as they publish their procedures for foreigners. If you have less than three months left on your period of stay, many banks will ask you to renew it first.
  • Your My Number notification will be mailed to your juminhyo address. Once received, you can complete applications for online banking and remittance services that require your individual number.
