Bank accounts and mobile numbers waiting on each other, how to order your first week after landing

With no address on the back of your 在留カード, neither the bank nor the carrier can proceed. Get the order right and you can settle your account and number within the first week

Short on time? The 懶人包 at the end has the order laid out, just scroll down and follow it.

A room in your first week after landing usually does not look great. The share house I lived in back then was signed from photos alone, and when I got there the room was so dark I needed the light on in daytime, a single bed would not fit, and I slept in a sleeping bag for a week, still cold through the bag in winter. I did not know how to turn on the water heater, assumed it was broken, and gritted my teeth through cold showers. Everything piled up in those days, the bank, a phone number, the ward office, and every counter told me something was missing. Looking back, what those days lacked was a list with the order worked out.

Many people run into the bank and the phone number waiting on each other as soon as they land. Opening an account requires your 在留カード, and if the address field on the back is blank, the counter can only ask you to fill it in and come back. During the application and later internet banking verification, quite a few banks use a Japanese mobile number to receive SMS codes, and without a number the process stops halfway. The number side is the same, a carrier contract needs identity verification, and in the document list under the 携帯電話不正利用防止法 compiled by 総務省, the 在留カード is one of the official certificates, so if the card details do not match, online verification fails. Neither side is wrong. Both are waiting for the other.

The address is the foundation of the whole thing. The 出入国在留管理庁 rules are clear, a mid to long term resident who has newly landed must report their address at the municipal office of their place of residence within 14 days of settling there, the so-called 転入届. Once done, the address is written on the back of the 在留カード and the 住民票 is established. According to 総務省, mid to long term residents with a 住民票 are assigned a My Number, and the notice is mailed to the address on the 住民票. With that address box empty, every form after it is missing a corner.

The six month line also shows up at bank counters. Under the Foreign Exchange Act and individual bank practice, people who have been in Japan for less than 6 months are often treated as non-residents, some banks only offer restricted accounts, some decline outright, and the wording differs a lot by bank. Seven Bank's English account page is the most specific, a foreign applicant qualifies by having lived in Japan for more than 6 months or by having more than 3 months left on their period of stay, meeting either one is enough. ゆうちょ銀行 works differently, you present your 在留カード at opening, and the bank checks nationality, status of residence and period of stay, and anyone whose period of stay expires within 3 months of the application date must apply after renewing. Post office counters are everywhere, 📮 and for someone who has just landed, ゆうちょ is a relatively approachable first bank, but whether the account opens still depends on each counter's checks.

Laid out, the order is actually simple. After the 転入届, take the 在留カード with the address on its back and sign up for a number, you can ask budget SIM providers and major carriers alike, and prepare the identity documents each company's official page lists. Once the number is in hand, go open the bank account, asking first at banks like ゆうちょ銀行 and Seven Bank that publish their process for foreigners. After the My Number notice arrives, fill in the services that need a personal number at your own pace, such as some internet banking functions and international remittances. If the bank your employer designates for salary is not one of these two, bring your 在留カード, number and 住民票, and almost everything the counter asks for will already be with you.

Before heading out, flip your 在留カード over and take a look. If the address is there, the number and the account just follow the order above, and more gets done in the first week than you would expect. The lesson that week left me is a practical one, get the order of the procedures right and daily life does not have to fall into disorder along with them.

Quick summary

  • After landing, do the 転入届 at the municipal office first, once the address is on the back of the 在留カード and the 住民票 exists, the number and the bank have a foundation
  • The deadline for reporting your address is within 14 days of settling there, the counter is the municipal office, it cannot be done at immigration
  • Bring the 在留カード with an address when signing up for a number, prepare identity documents per each carrier's official page, and if the address on the card is wrong, online verification will get stuck
  • Start asking at banks whose process for foreigners is public, like ゆうちょ銀行 and Seven Bank, and if less than 3 months remain on your period of stay, many banks will ask you to come back after renewing
  • The My Number notice is mailed to the 住民票 address, after it arrives, fill in the internet banking and remittance services that need a personal number
