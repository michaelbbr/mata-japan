Going to Fuchu on Japan Cup day: buy your ticket at home first

November 29, 2026, Tokyo Racecourse, turf 2400 meters, no tickets sold on site that day, admission tickets 1000 yen available only through an online lottery

By the end of November in Fuchu, the wind already feels like winter. The 2026 Japan Cup will be held on Sunday, November 29, at Tokyo Racecourse. It is a 2,400-meter turf race, an international invitational G1 for horses aged 3 and up, officially named the Longines Cup, the 46th edition. The race starts at 3:40 PM as the main event. If you live in the capital region and want to attend in person, do your homework before heading out; this day is different from a regular race day, and if you just show up, you will be turned away at the gate.

Let's be clear about the tickets. The racecourse does not sell admission tickets on the day of the event; there are no cash windows, and common coupon tickets cannot be used. Only those who have purchased tickets through the JRA online reservation system for reserved seats or general admission can enter. Admission is 1,000 yen per person, and children under 15 are free. However, there are entry restrictions on this day, so you must enter with an adult over 20 who has also purchased a ticket. The official number of tickets available is 80,000, distributed via lottery. The application period is from 6:00 PM on November 22 to 1:00 PM on November 24, with results announced at 5:00 PM on November 25. If you don't win, it's not the end: if there are remaining tickets after the lottery, they will be sold on a first-come, first-served basis online from 6:00 PM on November 25 to 3:00 PM on November 29, until sold out. The general lottery for reserved seats is from 6:00 PM on November 17 to 1:00 PM on November 19. If you want a seat, check the application deadline—don't wait until you're about to leave to remember to buy tickets. You need to register as a member to book, and those under 20 cannot register themselves. On the day of entry, show your QR code ticket on your phone; the QR code becomes available starting at 0:00 AM on the day of the event.

🏇 The venue is at 1-1 Hiyoshicho, Fuchu, Tokyo. There are three train routes: get off at Fuchukeiba-seimonmae Station on the Keio Line and walk about 2 minutes via the dedicated pedestrian bridge to the main gate, which is easiest on your legs. From Fuchu-Hommachi Station on the JR Musashino Line and Nanbu Line, on race days there is a temporary ticket gate, and it is about 5 minutes via the dedicated pedestrian bridge to the west gate. From the south exit of Higashi-Fuchu Station on the Keio Line, it is about 10 minutes to the east gate, a slow walk that works as a stroll. The gates open on the day are the main gate, west gate, and east gate; the south gate is closed. Just arrive according to the entry time slot on the ticket you bought; admission tickets are divided into 12 slots, the first starting at 8 a.m. and the last at 10 a.m., to spread out the flow of people.

The real day for a middle-aged man begins once he enters the venue. The races run one after another in the morning. With a race card in hand, eyes darting between the track and the screen, I grab lunch at one of the shops inside the venue. After eating, I find a spot with a view of the home stretch and stand there. Tokyo Racecourse has a very long final straight, and the intensity of the horses charging toward you is something a television screen just cannot convey. When betting on horses, stay within your means. Set a limit for yourself and stick to it—that is the rule for a middle-aged man. Whether you win or lose, save a little bit of excitement for the main event. The Japan Cup at 3:40 PM is the real highlight. When the pack of horses charges into the final stretch, the tension in the air alone makes the trip to Fuchu worth it.

Do not force your way through the crowds on the return trip. After an event ends, people heading to the station pack together; stay inside a little longer, eat something or watch the final ceremony before leaving, and the pedestrian bridge will be emptier instead. This trip to Fuchu, if arranged well it is a weekend; if arranged badly it is just queuing at the station. The difference is only whether you bought the right ticket and timed it right.
