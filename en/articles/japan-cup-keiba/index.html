Going to Fuchu on Japan Cup day: buy your ticket at home first

November 29, 2026, Tokyo Racecourse, turf 2400 meters, no tickets sold on site that day, admission tickets 1000 yen available only through an online lottery

In Fuchu in late November, the wind already feels like winter. The 2026 ジャパンカップ will be run on Sunday, November 29, at Tokyo Racecourse, over 2400 meters on turf, an international invitational G1 for horses aged 3 and older, officially titled with the ロンジン賞, the 46th running. The start time is 15:40, the final race of the day. If you live in the greater Tokyo area and want to attend in person, do your homework before you leave home. This day is not like a regular race day, and if you just wander over, you will be turned away.

Let's be clear about tickets. On the day, the racecourse does not sell admission tickets, there is no cash window, and common 回数券 cannot be used either; only people who bought tickets through JRA's 指定席 and admission ticket online reservation can enter. Admission is 1,000 yen per person, free for under 15, the official number of tickets released is 80,000, by lottery, application period from 6 p.m. on November 22 to 1 p.m. on November 24, results announced at 5 p.m. on November 25. The general lottery for 指定席 is from 6 p.m. on November 17 to 1 p.m. on November 19; if you want to watch seated, check the application deadline first and don't wait until you're about to leave to remember to buy tickets. Reservation requires 会員登録, those under 20 cannot register themselves, and on the day you show the QR ticket on your phone, the QR can be displayed from midnight that day.

🏇 The venue is at 1-1 Hiyoshicho, Fuchu, Tokyo. There are three train routes: get off at Fuchukeiba-seimonmae Station on the Keio Line and walk about 2 minutes via the dedicated pedestrian bridge to the main gate, which is easiest on your legs. From Fuchu-Hommachi Station on the JR Musashino Line and Nanbu Line, on race days there is a temporary ticket gate, and it is about 5 minutes via the dedicated pedestrian bridge to the west gate. From the south exit of Higashi-Fuchu Station on the Keio Line, it is about 10 minutes to the east gate, a slow walk that works as a stroll. The gates open on the day are the main gate, west gate, and east gate; the south gate is closed. Just arrive according to the entry time slot on the ticket you bought; admission tickets are divided into 12 slots, the first starting at 8 a.m. and the last at 10 a.m., to spread out the flow of people.

After entering is when the man's day really begins. The morning races run one after another; with a race program in hand, his eyes move back and forth between the racecourse and the screen, lunch is settled at a restaurant inside the venue, and after eating he finds a spot with a view of the home straight to stand. Tokyo Racecourse has a long final straight, and the pressure of the pack charging toward you is something television cannot give. Bet within your means; set your own limit for 馬券 and do not raise it once set, that is the man's rule, win or lose, save some mood for the feature race. The Japan Cup at 15:40 is the main event. When the pack charges into the final straight, the tension on site alone is worth a special trip to Fuchu.

Do not force your way through the crowds on the return trip. After an event ends, people heading to the station pack together; stay inside a little longer, eat something or watch the final ceremony before leaving, and the pedestrian bridge will be emptier instead. This trip to Fuchu, if arranged well it is a weekend; if arranged badly it is just queuing at the station. The difference is only whether you bought the right ticket and timed it right.
