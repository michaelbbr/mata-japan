Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.25

The illuminations at Oi Racecourse are only open on non-race days, and they are closed entirely during the week of Christmas.

Tokyo Mega Illumi is open until January 10, 2027, from 4:30 PM to 9:00 PM. Enter through the North Gate. The 100-meter light tunnel and fountain show are both inside the venue.

- Period: October 31, 2026 to January 10, 2027 (Open only on non-horse racing days; official closures are November 16–20, November 30–December 4, and December 24–January 1; closed entirely during the Christmas period) - Hours: 16:30 to 21:00 (Last entry at 20:00; opening times may be earlier on some days, please check the official calendar) - Location: Oi Racecourse (2-1-2 Katsushima, Shinagawa Ward, Tokyo); entry is via the North Gate only - Access: 2-minute walk from Oikeibajo-mae Station on the Tokyo Monorail; 12-minute walk from Tachiaigawa Station on the Keikyu Line - Admission: Dynamic pricing with two tiers, A and B (check the official calendar for specific dates). Advance tickets: Adults 800 or 1,300 yen, Children (elementary to high school) 400 or 700 yen; Same-day tickets: Adults 1,000 or 1,600 yen, Children 500 or 900 yen; Preschoolers are free

Oi Racecourse in Shinagawa shows a completely different face in winter. On days without horse racing, the entire venue lights up to become 'Tokyo Mega Illumi.' The track and the grandstands are wrapped in light. The air is cold and the venue is vast, creating a sense of empty quietness that feels very different from the illuminations crowded under street trees in the city center. 😊

Check the schedule carefully this year. The event runs until January 10, 2027, from 16:30 to 21:00 (last entry 20:00), but it only operates on non-racing days—it is closed all day on race days. The official calendar lists three closure periods: November 16–20, November 30–December 4, and December 24–January 1. The last one is the easiest to trip up on; Christmas Eve and Christmas Day are dark, so don't plan your Christmas outing here. The week around the New Year is also almost entirely closed.

The main attractions are the 100-meter light tunnel and the fountain show. Standing in front of the water jets as they rise and fall with the lights, you can feel the cool mist on your face. Since this is a racecourse, there are ponies out to greet everyone on every operating day. On weekends and holidays, there are also guide horses for photos, joint performances with the fountain show, and pony rides. The rides require advance reservation, an extra fee, and have limited spots. There is no racing or betting when the illuminations are on, so children can enter safely, provided they are accompanied by an adult if they are elementary school age or younger.

Ticket prices are dynamic, divided into A and B levels depending on the date. Advance tickets are 800 or 1,300 yen for adults, and same-day tickets are 1,000 or 1,600 yen for adults. Preschool children are free. Check the official calendar to see which date falls into which category. Advance tickets can be purchased online until 7:00 PM on the day of your visit. Same-day tickets are sold at the ticket machines in front of the North Gate starting 30 minutes before opening. On busy days, same-day tickets may be suspended, so the official site also recommends buying in advance. There are no ATMs or machines to top up transit cards inside the venue, so make sure your wallet and phone battery are ready before you enter.

Getting there is easy: take the Tokyo Monorail to Oikeibajo-mae Station and it's a 2-minute walk, or a 12-minute walk from Keikyu's Tachiaigawa Station. There is only one entrance, the North Gate, so just follow the crowd. The official recommendation is to allow 1 to 1.5 hours for a leisurely stroll. Since it gets dark early, arriving right at 16:30 means you can catch the sky while it's still a bit blue; the tunnels and water fountains look different than they do in total darkness, so going early lets you see both versions.

From the editor-in-chief

This article mentions that the illuminations are only open on non-race days, and after reading it, I only thought of one thing: the people frowning at their betting slips here during the day might be the same ones holding their kids' hands to watch the fountain show at night. The venue is the same, but once night falls, people's moods change. I belong to the daytime crowd, so when I go to see the lights at night, please remind me not to let my occupational disease kick in and start looking at the horses.
