How to read supermarket half-price stickers: 五百圓引 is the easiest to get wrong

Evening discount patrol: read the stickers before you buy

People rushing to buy groceries in the evening do not need to read the whole article; there is a quick summary at the end, read that before entering the supermarket.

Walking into a Japanese supermarket in the evening, the prepared foods section smells of freshly fried food, and in front there is always a small group of people moving slowly, their eyes scanning like mine detectors. They are waiting for the moment a staff member appears pushing the sticker cart.

After living in Japan for a while, you'll realize that discount stickers are a language you have to learn. 半額 means the price is cut straight in half, 三割引 means 30% off, and 五百圓引 means 500 yen taken off the original price. People often mistake it for 50% off, and only realize at checkout that it's not that cheap. I've misread them myself, only noticing after getting home and looking at the receipt. Some stickers print the new price directly; what's written on them is already the discounted price, so if you deduct again yourself, you'll be acting out a little drama in your head at checkout. Cooked foods bought at the supermarket to eat at home usually qualify for the reduced tax rate of 8%. Some price tags print the tax-excluded price very large and the tax-included price in small print beside it, so check the tax-included price before checkout so you don't miscalculate.

🏷️ Next to the sticker it often says 本日中, or what time today the consumption date expires. Prepared foods and bento like this are portions to finish tonight if you buy them; cheap as they are, they are not suitable for stocking up for a week. The Consumer Affairs Agency's explanation of food date labeling also reminds us that 消費期限 and 賞味期限 are two different things, and you should not take chances with things past their 消費期限. Money saved with a half-price sticker is not worth it if it ends up paying for a doctor's visit.

I decide what to eat tonight before I leave home, then go wait for the stickers, rather than letting the stickers decide dinner. For raw foods like sushi and sashimi, I only buy as much as can be finished tonight. For fried foods and simmered dishes, only if the use-by date has not passed and they can be refrigerated properly do I consider taking an extra portion for tomorrow's bento. Anything marked with a same-day use-by date gets finished that day. After shopping at the same store for a long time, I have gradually figured out roughly when it puts out the first round of stickers and when it goes to half price. That timetable will not appear in any tourist guide; it is something people who live here learn by pushing a shopping basket around, lap after lap.

Now my evening loop feels like a stroll, and I bring dinner home along the way.

Quick summary

  • 半額 = half off, 三割引 = 30% off, 五百圓引 = a flat 500 yen off
  • Items with a new price printed on the sticker are already at the discounted price; do not deduct it again
  • Items marked 本日中 or with a 消費期限 of today: finish them tonight, don't stockpile them
  • Only buy as much raw food as you can finish tonight; for fried foods and simmered dishes, only bring an extra portion for tomorrow's bento if the 消費期限 hasn't passed and it can be properly refrigerated

Source materials

  • Consumer Affairs Agency explanation of food date labeling: 消費期限 and 賞味期限 have different definitions, and expiry judgments should be based on the type of labeling and storage conditions. The expiry reminders in this article are written accordingly
  • Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and Consumer Affairs Agency published estimates on 食品ロス: discount sales such as 見切り are one way the retail sector reduces food waste. This article does not cite discount times for individual stores; each store's times are as posted on site.

From the editor-in-chief

I have fought on the half-price battlefield for three years, and my record is this: at the very second the sticker goes on, I am always at the other end of the shelf. By the time I get around there, only an empty tray and a crooked half-price sticker remain, as if laughing at me.
