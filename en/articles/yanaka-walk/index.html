Want to slow the pace this weekend? Take a walk around Yanaka Ginza

From Nippori Station, leave a little unhurried time for the shopping street.

After living in Tokyo for a long time, when you want to go out for a walk on the weekend without planning anything too tiring, Yanaka Ginza is probably that kind of option that comes to mind first. It is a shitamachi shopping street still seriously going about daily life: people coming to buy groceries, prepared foods, and snacks walk the same road as people who came specifically for a stroll. For people who live here, going to Yanaka does not feel much like going out for fun; it feels more like slowing down the pace of the weekend a little.

How to get there

It is about a 5-minute walk from the JR Nippori Station West Exit. The Yamanote Line, Joban Line, Nippori-Toneri Liner, and Keisei Line all stop at Nippori, making it very convenient for those living in the eastern or northern parts of the city. Another way is to take the Tokyo Metro Chiyoda Line to Sendagi Station, also a 5-minute walk, and enter from the other end of the shopping street. If you come from the Nippori side, the entrance is the famous 'Yuyake Dandan' staircase. Walking down the stairs, the entire shopping street unfolds before you. This opening is Yanaka's way of welcoming you.

What to explore

The entire street is about 170 meters long. It is not very long, but both sides are packed with about 60 to 70 small, privately owned shops: fishmongers, delis, Japanese confectionery shops, and daily goods stores, all selling things that local residents buy every day. If you find a croquette or a minced meat cutlet you want to eat, you can buy one and stop to eat it where the shop allows🍡. After you finish, keep walking and dispose of the packaging according to the shop's instructions; do not just throw it into another shop's trash can. The Yanaka area is also famous as a 'cat town.' You will often see cats sunning themselves at street corners or by shop doors. If you are lucky, you can meet several in one trip. Let me be clear: there are no large shopping malls here. The attraction is the small shops one after another along the road, and its charm is that sense of atmosphere where time moves a bit slower.

How to plan a walking route

Two hours is actually plenty of time; the key is not to overschedule. My route is to exit at Nippori Station, stop for a moment at Yuyake Dandan (sunset stairs), then slowly stroll to the other end of the shopping street. Stop and buy whatever looks good to eat. If you want to photograph the evening sunset, you can walk back along the shopping street toward the stairs. On the way back, walk on the other side of the street to visit the shops you missed, decide if you want to get dinner at a deli, and then return to the stairs to watch the sunset paint the whole street orange. That is where the name Yuyake (sunset) comes from! If you do not want to turn back, just keep walking to Sendagi Station and take the Chiyoda Line; it is like walking straight through the Yanaka area. Both ways work, the two-hour plan remains the same, and the route is clearer. The only thing to remember is that every shop has different business hours and holidays. If there is a shop you really want to visit, check the shop list on the shopping street's official website before you go to avoid disappointment.

How people who live here browse

For people living in Tokyo, Yanaka Ginza is more like a 'weekend grocery trip, with a stroll thrown in': buy two side dishes for dinner at a deli, bring a box home from a wagashi shop to have with tea, and the stroll is just a bonus. This street also has its own events all year round: the Yanaka Seven Lucky Gods pilgrimage in January, the Yanaka Hyakkoi Festival in summer, and the year-end grand thanksgiving sale in December; people who live nearby put these straight into their calendars. There's another small detail that says a lot about this street's character: it has its own point system, where you can collect points when you spend at participating shops, and when you've collected enough, you can exchange them for gift certificates. This kind of point design is made for neighbors who will come again and again. So there's no need to treat it as a check-in attraction; pick a free weekend and walk it at a local's pace, and that's just right.

From the editor-in-chief

I went to Yanaka Ginza intending to stroll slowly, but I ended up spending 20 minutes just queuing for a croquette stall. After eating, I stood at Yuyake Dandan watching the crowd and suddenly realized that waiting in line is part of the experience. It was slow, but only because of my stomach.
