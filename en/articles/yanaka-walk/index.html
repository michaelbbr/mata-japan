Want to slow the pace this weekend? Take a walk around Yanaka Ginza

From Nippori Station, leave a little unhurried time for the shopping street.

After living in Tokyo for a long time, when you want to go out for a walk on the weekend without planning anything too tiring, Yanaka Ginza is probably that kind of option that comes to mind first. It is a shitamachi shopping street still seriously going about daily life: people coming to buy groceries, prepared foods, and snacks walk the same road as people who came specifically for a stroll. For people who live here, going to Yanaka does not feel much like going out for fun; it feels more like slowing down the pace of the weekend a little.

How to get there

From the west exit of JR Nippori Station, it's about a 5-minute walk. The Yamanote Line, Joban Line, Nippori-Toneri Liner, and Keisei Line all stop at Nippori, so it's convenient for people living in the east or north of the city. Another way is to take the Tokyo Metro Chiyoda Line to Sendagi Station, also about a 5-minute walk, entering from the other end of the shopping street. Coming from the Nippori side, the entrance is the famous '夕やけだんだん' steps; walk down the steps, and the whole shopping street spreads out before your eyes. This opening is Yanaka's greeting to you.

What to explore

The whole street is about 170 meters long, not long to speak of, yet both sides are crowded with about 60 to 70 small independently run shops: fish shops, delicatessen shops, 和菓子 shops, daily goods shops, all selling things nearby residents buy every day. If while strolling you see a croquette or fried meat patty you want to eat, you can buy one and stop to eat it in a place the shop allows🍡. After eating, continue strolling, and handle the packaging according to the shop's instructions as well; do not casually throw it into another shop's trash bin. The Yanaka area is also known as a “cat town”, and at alley entrances and beside shop doors there are often cats basking in the sun; if you are lucky, you can meet several on one trip. Let me say this first: there is no large shopping mall here; what attracts people is the small shops one after another along the way, and its charm is that air of time moving a little more slowly.

How to plan a walking route

Two hours is actually plenty; the point is not to pack too much in. My way is to exit at Nippori Station, pause at 夕やけだんだん, then browse slowly to the other end of the shopping street. Stop and buy anything you want to eat when you see it. If you want to photograph the evening sunset, walk back along the shopping street toward the steps: on the way back, walk on the other side of the street to catch the shops you missed, decide whether to settle dinner at a prepared-foods shop, then return to the steps and watch the sunset dye the whole street orange. That is where the name '夕やけ' comes from! If you do not want to turn back, keep walking to Sendagi Station and leave by the Chiyoda Line, which takes you straight through the Yanaka area. Either way works, the two-hour plan stays the same, and the route is clearer. The only reminder is that every shop has different opening hours and closing days, so if there is a shop you especially want to visit, check the shop list on the shopping street's official website before setting out to avoid a wasted trip.

How people who live here browse

For people living in Tokyo, Yanaka Ginza is more like a 'weekend grocery trip, with a stroll thrown in': buy two side dishes for dinner at a deli, bring a box home from a wagashi shop to have with tea, and the stroll is just a bonus. This street also has its own events all year round: the Yanaka Seven Lucky Gods pilgrimage in January, the Yanaka Hyakkoi Festival in summer, and the year-end grand thanksgiving sale in December; people who live nearby put these straight into their calendars. There's another small detail that says a lot about this street's character: it has its own point system, where you can collect points when you spend at participating shops, and when you've collected enough, you can exchange them for gift certificates. This kind of point design is made for neighbors who will come again and again. So there's no need to treat it as a check-in attraction; pick a free weekend and walk it at a local's pace, and that's just right.

From the editor-in-chief

I went to Yanaka Ginza intending to learn from others and browse slowly, but just queuing in front of the croquette stall took twenty minutes. After eating, I stood at 夕やけだんだん watching people come and go, and suddenly felt that queuing was also part of the itinerary. It was slow, just slow at the stomach stage.
