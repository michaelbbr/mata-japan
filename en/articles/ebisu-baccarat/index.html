Stopping by Ebisu after work to see a crystal chandelier that is lit even in the daytime

November 14, 2026 to January 11, 2027, Central Plaza at Yebisu Garden Place, lights on from 11:00 to 23:00, Christmas market only until December 25

Winter in Tokyo often starts to feel cold on the way home from work. It gets dark early. The wind is cold. When the wind blows between the buildings and you have to take out your gloves, that is about the time this event season begins. For those living along the Yamanote Line, there is an extra stop to make this winter: the Baccarat ETERNAL LIGHTS at Yebisu Garden Place has been lit up. The exhibition runs from November 14, 2026, to January 11, 2027, at the Center Plaza. This year's theme is 'Into the Light'.

This light has a special lighting time; it comes on at 11 in the morning and stays on until 23:00 at night. When you pass during the day, the crystal in the display case lets daylight through and the color is faint; in the evening as it gets dark, with 250 lights on together, the air of the whole plaza seems to warm a little too. You can see it if you pass with children during the day on a holiday, and you can see it on a walk after dinner at night; you hardly need to adjust your schedule to it at all, which is very friendly for people who live nearby.

You only get a sense of the chandelier's size when you stand underneath it. Official information says it is about 5 meters high and about 3 meters wide, with a total of 8,500 crystal parts, housed in an aluminum display case about 10 meters high. Officials call it one of the world's largest Baccarat chandeliers. In photos it just looks like a beautiful lamp, but when you walk up to the case and look up, your neck gets sore, and everyone around you is also looking up. The scene is a bit funny.

✨ Beyond the Central Plaza, the illuminations elsewhere in the complex will continue until February 28, 2027. About 100,000 champagne-gold lights will be on from November 14, 2026 through February 28, 2027, lit from 16:00 to 23:00, covering the entrance, Clock Plaza, the slope promenade, and Castle Plaza. Walk slowly from Ebisu Station along the Sky Walk, and the trees on both sides of the slope light up first; when you reach the plaza, the chandelier is waiting ahead of you. That walk itself is part of the stroll.

The Christmas atmosphere part ends earlier. At the Clock Plaza there is a Christmas tree about 10 meters high, hung with more than 300 ornaments, on display until December 25. The Christmas market in the same plaza is also from November 14 to December 25, weekdays 5 p.m. to 8 p.m., weekends and holidays from 12 p.m. to 8 p.m., with food trucks and Christmas goods. The market at Castle Plaza starts a little later, November 27 to December 25, same hours. Markets may be cancelled due to rain or strong wind; the official page announced on November 17 itself that the Clock Plaza market was closed due to rain, so glance at the official page before going out to avoid a wasted trip.

How to arrange the time, people who live here would probably use it like this. On weekdays, drop by on the way home from work; the market is only open until 20:00, so if you get off work too late only the lights are left to see, and the chandeliers stay lit until 23:00, so there is still time to look slowly. On weekends, go during the day; the market opens from noon, steam from hot drinks and hot food disperses in the cold air, and standing at the edge of the square holding a cup, winter has its flavor. Seeing the lights themselves does not require a ticket; the square is an open space, and the official page does not list an admission fee either, whether you buy anything is another matter.

After January 11, the chandelier will be taken down, while the illuminations elsewhere in the complex will remain until the end of February. If you want to see that chandelier, mark the date in your calendar, pick an evening after work when you are not too tired, and stop by for a while. The cold wind on your face, the light catching on the crystal and sparkling in small flashes — just standing there, your shoulders start to relax.

From the editor-in-chief

On the opening weekend, I went to look at that light at noon. The sun was bright, and it was shining on its own. I stood in front of the display case, unable to tell which ray was sunlight. I didn't give up and went back at night, looking up until my neck was sore, before I was satisfied enough to go home. Looking at the same light twice in one day seems a bit too serious.
