Weekend outings · AYAKA · 2026.11.24

Enoshima's 'Shonan no Hoseki' opens on December 1, with seaside lights shining until the end of February.

Shonan no Hoseki 2026-2027 runs for 90 days. Weekdays are only until 8:00 PM, while weekends and the year-end period go until 9:00 PM. The schedule is different from city illuminations.

- Period: December 1, 2026 to February 28, 2027 (90 days total, 27th annual event) - Hours: Weekdays and December 31 to January 3: 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM; Weekends, holidays, and December 19 to 30: 5:00 PM to 9:00 PM; Lighting starts at 5:30 PM from January 16, 2027 onwards. - Location: Centered around Enoshima Samuel Cocking Garden and Enoshima Sea Candle, including the island of Enoshima and the Katase Coast area (Fujisawa City, Kanagawa Prefecture). - Fees: Nighttime garden admission is 500 yen for adults and 250 yen for elementary school students; Sea Candle admission is 800 yen for adults and 400 yen for elementary school students; A combo ticket including nighttime garden admission is 1,550 yen for adults.

In December, Enoshima is all about the sea by day and light by night. The 27th annual Shonan no Hoseki (Jewel of Shonan) kicks off on December 1 and runs until February 28, 2027. While it was named one of Japan's top three illuminations in 2025, the atmosphere is surprisingly serene. As the sea breeze blows, the lights gently sway among the trees and paths in the garden. ✨

The main venue is the Samuel Cocking Garden at the top of the island. The Sea Candle tower casts a vast expanse of light, and this year features a new installation by the art collective MIRRORBOWLER. Sunset in December is around 16:30, so time your arrival to catch the sun dipping into Sagami Bay from the tower before the lights come on. The orange sky blending with the blue and white lights is the quintessential winter evening stroll for locals in the Shonan area.

Pay special attention to the schedule, as this event is quite different from the illuminations in central Tokyo. On weekdays, the lights are only on until 8:00 PM, with the last entry to the garden at 7:30 PM. It's almost impossible to make it there after work from central Tokyo, so the weekday sessions are better suited for those living in the Fujisawa or Chigasaki areas. On weekends and holidays, it's extended until 9:00 PM, and the year-end period from December 19 to December 30 follows the weekend schedule. After January 16, 2027, the lighting time changes to 5:30 PM as the sun sets later, so the lights turn on a bit later as well.

Regarding tickets: Garden entry is free during the day, but there is a fee after 5:00 PM. Adult admission is 500 yen, with an additional 800 yen to go up the Sea Candle. There is a combo ticket for 1,550 yen that includes the Escar escalator, which is a lifesaver if you don't want to climb the island's slopes. The island has significant elevation changes, and the evening sea breeze is much colder than during the day. Your neck will get cold while watching the lights, so bring a scarf and hand warmers. Holding a hot drink will also help you stay patient while enjoying the view.

Don't worry too much about the last train home. The last Odakyu train from Katase-Enoshima Station toward Shinjuku on weekdays is after 23:00, and the last Enoden train toward Fujisawa and Kamakura is around 23:30. After the lights go out at 21:00, take a slow walk down from the island. It gets windy on the bridge, so hold onto your hat—it's a classic Shonan way to end the night.

From the editor-in-chief

This article clearly states that the lights are only on until 8:00 PM on weekdays, which saved a lot of people. I'm one of those people who rushed from Tokyo after work on a weekday, sat on the train for an hour, and arrived on the island just in time to watch the lights turn off—the whole island went dark in perfect synchronization. I've noted the 9:00 PM weekend closing time; I won't be a cautionary tale this time.
