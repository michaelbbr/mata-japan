Mixed metal into regular trash and got corrected on the spot; Shinjuku's sorting guide is actually very detailed.

In Shinjuku Ward, burnable trash is collected twice a week, but metals, ceramics, and glass are only collected twice a month. If you put them out on the wrong day, you'll likely be stopped at the collection point.

If you're short on time, there's a summary of Shinjuku Ward's collection rules at the end of this article. This part is about that one time I got corrected. I once mixed metal in with regular trash and brought the bag to the collection point, only for the man organizing the trash to catch me on the spot and tell me it wasn't allowed. My face turned red instantly. 😅

I went back to check the Shinjuku Ward sorting table, and I was definitely wrong. Shinjuku Ward categorizes household waste into several main groups: burnable garbage, metal/ceramic/glass garbage, recyclables, and oversized garbage. Metal is categorized separately from the start and follows a completely different collection route than food waste or paper scraps. Burnable garbage is collected twice a week, while metal/ceramic/glass garbage is collected only twice a month. The schedules are so different that if you mix them in one bag, the collection crew can't process them as the same type of waste. Small frying pans, broken umbrella frames, and ceramic shards must wait for the monthly metal collection day in Shinjuku; you can't just toss them out on a burnable garbage day. For larger items, check the rules for oversized garbage first.

Resources are another line, collected once a week. Cans, glass bottles, and PET bottles fall under resources. As of April 2024, containers, packaging, and plastic products in Shinjuku have been merged into resource plastics, and old paper is also collected on the resource day. This means that next to the same kitchen sink, you have to keep track of at least three different days. Shinjuku also divides collection days by town and block name; if someone living in Nishi-Shinjuku uses the Kagurazaka calendar, they will still end up throwing it out on the wrong day.

There are also rules for timing. Every page of the Shinjuku Ward collection calendar states that trash must be placed at the resource/trash collection point by 8:00 AM on the collection day. If you put it out late the night before, or if you oversleep and take it out at 8:30 AM, there's a good chance the whole bag will be left behind. Even if you sort it correctly, if the day or time is wrong, the result is pretty much the same.

Move to the next ward, and the names change instantly. Setagaya Ward calls trash 'burnable' and 'non-burnable.' A frying pan is 'non-burnable' there, while most general plastic products are 'burnable.' PET bottles and certain containers have separate recycling rules, which don't align with Shinjuku's practice of collecting plastic as recyclables. Shibuya Ward also uses the term 'non-burnable' for metal, glass, and ceramics, but the frequency is once a month—half as often as in Shinjuku. In principle, trash must be put out by 8:00 AM, and in the city center, you often have to put it out by 7:30 AM. Collection and sorting in Tokyo's 23 wards are managed by each ward individually. If you just grab any random 'How to throw away trash in Tokyo' guide online and compare it to your home, you'll likely trip up on at least one category.

After being corrected by that man, I put two small cardboard boxes under my kitchen sink. I rinse and dry empty cans and keep them until the resource day, and put small metal items in another box, waiting until the metal day to dump the whole box into a bag and take it down. The method is a bit primitive, but I haven't been stopped at the collection point since. Printing out the sorting guide and sticking it on the side of the fridge also helps; checking the chart takes only ten seconds, which is much cheaper than being lectured at the collection point. You also have to make sure you're looking at the page for your own address. Shinjuku's calendar is printed by area, so if you get the town name or block number wrong by even one line, the resource and metal days won't match up at all. The risk of your bag being left at the collection point overnight is real, and just thinking about that scene is embarrassing enough.

Quick summary

  • In Shinjuku Ward, metals, ceramics, and glass are categorized together and collected twice a month; they cannot be mixed in with the burnable trash that is collected twice a week.
  • Resources are collected once a week. Cans, glass bottles, PET bottles, resource plastics, and old paper are all collected on this day. As of April 2024, plastic products in Shinjuku have been merged into resource plastics.
  • Place it at the designated resource/garbage collection point by 8:00 AM on collection day. The day of the week for each type of waste is determined by your town and block (cho-me). You need to check the calendar page for your specific address.
  • Setagaya Ward uses the terms 'burnable garbage' (kaneru gomi) and 'non-burnable garbage' (funeru gomi), with most general plastic products classified as burnable. In Shibuya Ward, non-burnable garbage is collected only once a month. Rules vary by ward, so you must re-check the sorting table for your specific ward whenever you move.

From the editor-in-chief

I understand the shame of being corrected on the spot at the collection point. I once placed a whole bag of trash too far to the outside, and the cage door wouldn't close. I stood there stuffing bag after bag inside, sweating profusely, only to realize the bag at the very bottom was the one I had just placed crookedly. Ever since then, I squat down to place my trash neatly, and I look back once before leaving to make sure the cage door can close.
