First time at a 銭湯: don't bring your towel into the bath

There aren't many rules; they're all written on the wall, just follow them and you won't feel awkward

Living in Tokyo, when I want to soak away a week's fatigue slowly on the weekend for the least money, I go to the 銭湯 near home. The hot water rises to my shoulders, my whole body relaxes, and sitting by the tub watching the steam slowly rise, a week's tiredness seems to drift away with it.

Many Chinese friends who have just moved here hesitate about 銭湯, for the same reason: they're afraid there are many rules and afraid of being noticed for doing something wrong. I was incredibly nervous at first too, and only after going a few times did I realize that there are only a few things that actually get noticed. You must not put your towel in the bath water; put it at the edge of the tub, or fold it and place it on your head. Before entering the tub, wash your body clean at the washing area first and rinse off the soap. Before entering, you can also do かけ湯 first, that is, pour warm water over your body to let it slowly get used to the water temperature. Remember to tie up long hair, and don't let the ends soak in the bath water.

The Edogawa Ward Office's page for foreign residents spells these points out clearly: wash your body before entering the pool, do not put towels in the pool, tie up long hair, and dry your body before returning to the changing room. The pool water is shared by everyone; if you rinse yourself clean, the next person will be comfortable soaking too. When you think of it as water everyone shares, you naturally want to rinse a couple more times; that is probably what the rules are about.

♨️ One more thing I have observed for a long time: rules written on the wall do not mean everyone follows every one of them. Occasionally you see someone rinse off very casually and get straight in, and the regulars nearby just quietly move a little farther away. So on your first visit, there is no need to get so nervous that you memorize every rule; doing the few things above is quite enough.

I like to go on relatively quiet weekday evenings; after soaking, I buy a bottle of coffee milk from the vending machine, stand at the entrance and enjoy the evening breeze for a bit before going home. The glass bottle is ice-cold, held in a hand warmed by the bath, just right. Those twenty minutes are one of the few times in Tokyo life when I don't look at my phone at all.

Quick summary

  • Towels don't go in the bath. Leave them at the side of the tub or fold them and place them on your head
  • Wash your body clean before entering the pool, then use かけ湯 to get used to the water temperature
  • Tie up long hair so the ends do not soak in the pool water
  • Dry your body before returning to the changing room
  • Remember to bring your own small towel, toiletries, and coins (hair dryers mostly require coins)

From the editor-in-chief

The first time I went to a 銭湯, I headed toward the bath with a towel draped around my neck, and the older man next to me glanced at me, said nothing, and only pointed toward the edge of the bath. I put the towel away properly before getting into the water, and that day I soaked more quietly than usual, even coughing softly.
