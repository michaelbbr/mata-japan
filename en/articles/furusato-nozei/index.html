If you leave 故鄉納稅 until year-end, if it is not paid in full by December 31 it becomes next year's quota

With the ワンストップ特例 you do not need to file a 確定申告, but the application form must be mailed by January 10 of the following year, and this is the step most people forget

Tax articles have a lot of text. If you can't finish it, there's a quick summary at the end of the article; save it and read it slowly.

For people living in Japan who pay taxes, there is one more money-related thing to handle before the end of the year: 故鄉納稅. Which year's allowance it counts toward depends on the day the money is paid. Amounts paid in full between January 1 and December 31, 2026 count as 2026; if it drags past the 31st and payment is only completed in January, it will count as the next year's donation and can no longer be included in this year's 故鄉納稅 deduction. At year-end every website is crowded, card pages get stuck, and payment completion times slip later. If you want to use this year's allowance, finishing by mid-December feels more secure.

The way the deduction works is not hard to understand. Subtract the 2,000 yen self-payment from the donated amount, and the remaining portion is, in principle, fully deducted from income tax and the following year's 住民稅, which is equivalent to taking part of the tax you would have paid to your place of residence and allocating it to a municipality you choose yourself, and receiving a local return gift in return. Foreigners who work and pay taxes in Japan can also use 故鄉納稅; the system does not distinguish by nationality. People in their first year in Japan also cannot judge only by whether they are currently paying 住民稅. That year's income and your residence status on January 1 of the following year may both affect the deduction result. People preparing to leave Japan before year-end especially need to check first, and should not donate based directly on a trial calculation for a general salaried worker.

The limit has a ceiling, and this is what most people misunderstand. The special deduction portion for resident tax can be at most 20% of the income-based levy, but that does not mean simply multiplying the income-based levy by 20% gives the amount you can donate. Deductions for both income tax and resident tax have to be considered together; if you exceed the deductible limit, your out-of-pocket amount may be higher than 2,000 yen. Every site has a simulation tool; enter your annual income and family composition and a rough figure will appear. To know the exact limit, the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications says to ask the resident tax counter of the municipality where you live; simulations on websites are only estimates.

📮 Most employees use the ワンストップ特例 to avoid filing a 確定申告. The conditions are that you do not originally need to file a 確定申告, and that you donate to no more than 5 municipalities in a year. For each municipality you donate to, you must send the application form back to that municipality, by a deadline of January 10 the following year, based on the date it arrives. The application form usually comes with the return gift, or in a separate letter, and when unpacking it is very easy to throw it away together with the packing materials, which is the most common mishap in the whole process. If you have a My Number Card, first check whether the donation platform or municipality supports submitting the special application online; if it does, you do not need to wait for paper or rush mailing. For people using ワンストップ, the deduction appears entirely in the following year's 住民稅, with no income tax refund stage. Employees' 住民稅 is normally deducted from salary in installments from June to May the following year, and the deduction will be spread gradually across that whole year.

The name must also be consistent from start to finish. The deduction recognizes the person who made the donation. If the account is in your own name but payment is made with your spouse's card, and the names on the receipt and application do not match, the deduction may not count. If a couple wants to donate separately, each should use their own account and their own card, keeping each transaction clear; this is where things most easily get mixed up when rushing in December.

There is a rough level you can use for return gifts. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications requires the procurement cost of a 返禮品 to be within 30% of the donation amount. For a donation of 10,000 yen, for example, that cost cap is 3,000 yen, but that does not mean the market price of the 返禮品 is necessarily 3,000 yen. Keeping that ratio in mind when choosing gives you a better sense of it. From October 2025, 故鄉納稅 platforms can no longer give their own points in return for donations. General payment rewards such as credit card points are a separate matter and follow each company's own rules. Choosing returns to the item itself; look at where it comes from and how much you get, without having to calculate which site gives more points.

With a limit like this, leaving it until the 31st itself to deal with makes that minute of the card payment page spinning the hardest to endure. Finish it in mid-December, send out the application, and you can pass the year-end with peace of mind.

Quick summary

  • For the 2026 limit, only donations paid in full by December 31 count; anything paid in January counts toward 2027
  • The 住民稅 special deduction is capped at 20% of the 所得割 amount; the actual donatable amount needs to be calculated separately
  • The ワンストップ特例 exempts you from filing a 確定申告, provided you donate to no more than 5 municipalities; the application must arrive by January 10 of the following year, one form per municipality
  • Use the same person's name for the account, the payment card, and the application form; spouses should not mix and use each other's
  • The deduction appears in the following year's 住民稅, and for salaried workers the withholding from salary starting in June will decrease
