If you drag your hometown tax donation until the end of the year, any payment not completed by December 31st will count toward next year's quota.

The One-Stop Exception System means you do not have to do a final tax return, but the application form must arrive by January 10 of the following year; this is the step most people forget.

For those living in Japan who pay taxes, there is one more money-related matter to handle before the end of the year: furusato nozei (hometown tax donation). Which year's quota it counts toward depends on the day the money is paid. Payments completed between January 1, 2026, and December 31, 2026, count for 2026; if you delay past the 31st and complete payment in January, it will count as a donation for the following year and cannot be included in this year's furusato nozei deduction. Websites get very crowded at the end of the year, and credit card payment pages can freeze, pushing the completion time to a later date. If you want to use this year's quota, it is safer to finish by mid-December.

The deduction process is not hard to understand. After subtracting the 2,000 yen out-of-pocket fee from your total donation, the remaining amount is, in principle, fully deducted from your income tax and the following year's residence tax. It is essentially taking a portion of the tax you would have paid to your local municipality and redirecting it to a municipality of your choice, while receiving a local gift in return. Foreigners working and paying taxes in Japan can use the hometown tax donation system just the same; the system does not distinguish by nationality. Those in their first year in Japan should not judge their eligibility solely by whether they are currently paying residence tax. Your income for that year and your residency status as of January 1st of the following year can both affect the deduction results. Those planning to leave Japan before the end of the year should be especially careful to check their status and not just rely on standard calculations for office workers.

There is a cap on the amount, which is the most common misunderstanding. The special deduction for resident tax can only go up to 20% of the income-based levy (shotoku-wari). However, this does not mean you can simply multiply the income-based levy by 20% to get the amount you can donate. Both income tax and resident tax deductions must be considered together. If you exceed the deductible limit, your out-of-pocket expense may be higher than 2,000 yen. Every website has a calculation tool; enter your annual income and family composition, and it will pop out an approximate number. To confirm the calculation method, you can ask the resident tax window at your local municipal office or use the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' calculation tool to calculate it yourself. The calculators on various websites are just estimates; what the municipal office can confirm is the calculation method, but they cannot give you a guaranteed, accurate cap in advance.

📮 Most office workers use the One-Stop Exception system to avoid filing a tax return (kakutei shinkoku). The conditions are that you wouldn't normally need to file a tax return anyway, and you donated to 5 or fewer municipalities in a year. You must mail an application form to each municipality you donated to by January 10 of the following year (based on the arrival date). The form usually comes with the return gift or in a separate envelope; it's easy to accidentally throw it away with the packaging, which is the most common mistake. If you have a My Number Card, check if the donation platform or municipality supports online submission of the exception application—if they do, you don't have to wait for paper forms or rush to the post office. For those using the One-Stop system, the deduction is applied entirely to the following year's resident tax; there is no income tax refund. One thing often missed: if you use the exception but then file a tax return for other reasons (like tax refunds or side jobs), the exception application becomes invalid, and you must include the donations in your tax return. Resident tax for office workers is normally deducted from your salary in installments from June to the following May, so the deduction will be spread out over that year.

The name must also be consistent from start to finish. The deduction recognizes the person who made the donation. If the account is in your own name but payment is made with your spouse's card, and the names on the receipt and application do not match, the deduction may not count. If a couple wants to donate separately, each should use their own account and their own card, keeping each transaction clear; this is where things most easily get mixed up when rushing in December.

You can get a general idea of the level of 'furusato nozei' (hometown tax donation) return gifts. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications stipulates that the procurement cost of return gifts must be within 30% of the donation amount. Taking a 10,000 yen donation as an example, this cost limit is 3,000 yen, but this does not mean the market value of the return gift is necessarily 3,000 yen. Think according to this ratio when choosing, and you will have a better idea. From October 2025, hometown tax platforms can no longer offer their own points for donations. General payment rewards from credit cards are a different matter and depend on each company's own rules. Focus on the product itself: look at the origin and the quantity, and you won't need to calculate which website offers more points.

With a limit like this, leaving it until the 31st itself to deal with makes that minute of the card payment page spinning the hardest to endure. Finish it in mid-December, send out the application, and you can pass the year-end with peace of mind.

Quick summary

  • For the 2026 limit, only donations paid in full by December 31 count; anything paid in January counts toward 2027
  • The special deduction for residence tax is capped at 20 percent of the income-based levy; the actual deductible amount must be calculated separately.
  • The One-Stop Exception System exempts you from a final tax return, provided you donate to 5 or fewer municipalities, and the application form must arrive by January 10 of the following year; you must send one form per municipality.
  • Use the same person's name for the account, the payment card, and the application form; spouses should not mix and use each other's
  • The deduction appears in the following year's residence tax, so office workers will see lower tax withholdings from their paychecks starting in June.
