Want to run the Imperial Palace on a weekend morning? What is the smoothest way to plan 5 kilometers?

One lap is about five kilometers, so factor in luggage storage and the time after you finish running.

Living in Tokyo, when you want to fit in some exercise on the weekend between sleeping in and going out to run errands, the area around the Imperial Palace is the choice that requires the least worrying. No sign-up, no reservation; take a few train stops from home, one lap is exactly five kilometers, then go home, shower, and eat breakfast, and a whole morning is neatly arranged.

What the route looks like

The Imperial Palace running route, organized by the Runners Foundation, is about 5 kilometers per lap with an elevation change of about 30 meters. The entire lap has some undulations, so it won't feel boring. Many people choose to start running near Sakuradamon, Takebashi, or Hanzomon; check for nearby restrooms and warm-up spaces before you start. The kilometer markers on the map are calculated starting from Sakuradamon. Starting from Sakuradamon, the first section is flat, so just build up your speed gradually—this is the friendliest part for beginners. The hills in the second half are the highlight of the lap, so don't sprint too hard at the beginning. The whole route follows the outer perimeter of the Imperial Palace, so you don't need to memorize the path; just follow the perimeter back to the start. If you want to train longer, do two laps to make it exactly 10 kilometers. A note on safety: the sidewalks around Takebashi and Hanzomon are narrow, and there are vehicle entrances near Sakuradamon. Watch for cars when passing through, and don't walk or run side-by-side and block the path.

How to time it

🏃 First, map out your total time: a casual 5km run takes about 30 minutes. It's normal for beginners to take 40 to 50 minutes, and 40 minutes is common if you're just zoning out while running. Work backward: include travel time to the start point, time to change and store gear at a running station, and the trip home. If you have plans later in the morning, arrive at the start point 20 minutes early so you don't have to rush your warm-up or bathroom break—your running rhythm will be much steadier. In the summer, the sun is strong, so try to finish before it gets too hot; move your departure time even earlier.

What to do about shipping belongings and changing clothes

There are many 'run stations' (run-ste) around the Imperial Palace, mostly near starting points like Hibiya, Otemachi, Takebashi, and Hanzomon. They provide showers and lockers, and you can pay per visit without needing a membership. The shops listed on the foundation's page charge anywhere from 500 to 1,500 yen per visit. Some open as early as 6:30 or 7:00 AM on weekdays, so you can use them before work. However, weekend hours may differ, so if you want to store your bags or shower early in the morning, remember to check the holiday hours before you go. Some shops even rent out running shoes and towels, so you don't have to lug a whole bag of gear with you if you decide to run on a whim. A quick heads-up: some shops only accept electronic payments and no cash. Check the official website for hours and payment methods before you leave so you aren't stuck digging for change at the counter. If you're looking for running buddies, keep in mind that many run stations hold practice sessions almost every day, ranging from morning runs before work to group training after hours. Once you live in the area for a while, it's easy to get into a routine.

Reminders for your first run

Runners around the Imperial Palace outer loop usually go counterclockwise, give pedestrians the right of way, and don't force their way past in narrow spots. The sidewalks around Takebashi and Hanzomon are narrower and have vehicle entrances, so slow down there and watch for cars coming from both sides. For a first run, start from Sakuradamon and follow the kilometer markers. It makes the distance easier to judge, and it is the route least likely to get you lost! Carry as little as possible: leave your change of clothes and bag at the running station, and take only your phone and water to the start. Finish warming up in the plaza before heading out, and don't attack the uphill in the second half. The Palace slopes aren't long, but they feel especially tough right after the flat stretches, so save a little energy for the final kilometer. After the run, head back to the running station for a shower and a change of clothes, then grab breakfast at a nearby café, a perfect lead-in to the rest of the weekend.
