Want to run the Imperial Palace on a weekend morning? What is the smoothest way to plan 5 kilometers?

One lap is about five kilometers, so factor in luggage storage and the time after you finish running.

Living in Tokyo, when you want to fit in some exercise on the weekend between sleeping in and going out to run errands, the area around the Imperial Palace is the choice that requires the least worrying. No sign-up, no reservation; take a few train stops from home, one lap is exactly five kilometers, then go home, shower, and eat breakfast, and a whole morning is neatly arranged.

What the route looks like

The Imperial Palace running route compiled by 公益財團法人ランナーズ財団 is about five kilometers per lap, with an elevation difference of about thirty meters; the whole lap has some ups and downs, so running it is not boring. People choose to start running near Sakuradamon, Takebashi, and Hanzomon; before starting, check nearby toilets and warm-up space. The kilometer markings on the map are calculated starting from Sakuradamon. Starting from Sakuradamon, the first section is flat, so just bring your speed up slowly; it is the friendliest for first-time runners. The slopes in the second half are the focus of this lap, so do not push too hard at the front. The whole lap follows the outside of the Imperial Palace, no need to memorize the route, just follow the perimeter back to the start; if you want to train longer, do two laps, exactly ten kilometers.

How to time it

🏃 First work out the time for one lap: an easy five-kilometer run takes about thirty minutes, and forty minutes is also common if you zone out while running. Then work backwards: the travel time from leaving home to the starting point, the time to change and store your things at a running station, and the trip home after the run all need to be counted. If you have plans later in the morning, it is best to arrive at the starting point twenty minutes early, so warming up and using the toilet are not rushed and your running rhythm is much steadier. In summer when the sun is strong, try to finish before the sun gets high, so you need to move your departure time even earlier.

What to do about shipping belongings and changing clothes

There are quite a few running stations (ランステ) around the Imperial Palace, mostly near starting points like Hibiya, Otemachi, Takebashi, and Hanzomon, equipped with showers and lockers. You can use them with a single-use fee, no need to sign up for a membership first. For the shops listed on the foundation's page, single-use fees range from just over 500 yen to 1,500 yen. Some shops open as early as 6:30 or 7:00 on weekday mornings, so you can use them before work too. However, weekend hours may differ, so if you want to store your things or shower early in the morning, remember to check the shop's holiday hours before you head out. Many shops also rent running shoes and towels, so if you suddenly feel like running, you don't need to carry a whole bag of gear. One reminder first: some shops only accept electronic payments and not cash, so check the hours and payment methods on the official website before you go out, so you don't end up standing at the counter digging through your bag for change. People looking for running companions can also take note: many running stations hold practice sessions almost every day, from morning runs before work to group training after work. If you live nearby for a while, it's easy to settle into a routine.

Reminders for your first run

Runners on the Imperial Palace outer loop generally follow the counterclockwise direction. Pedestrians on the road have priority, and don't force an overtake in narrow places. For your first run, you can start from Sakuradamon Gate and follow the kilometer markers. Your sense of distance will be clearer, and it's the least likely way to get lost! The less you carry, the better: leave your change of clothes and bag at the running station, and just bring your phone and water to the starting point. Finish your warm-up in the plaza before setting off, and don't push hard on the uphill in the latter half. The Imperial Palace slopes aren't long, but they feel especially noticeable after the flat sections, so save some energy for the last kilometer. After your run, go back to the running station for a shower and a change of clothes, then have breakfast at a nearby café, which connects perfectly to the rest of your weekend plans.
