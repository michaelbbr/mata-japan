People who still need to file a personal tax return in February after completing their year-end tax adjustment.

The tax filing (Kakutei Shinkoku) period for the 2026 tax year is February 16 to March 15, 2027. The basic deduction has been raised to a maximum of 1.04 million yen. Medical expense deductions and first-year mortgage deductions must be filed manually.

If you're short on time, just check the summary at the end of this article against your own situation. In December, when companies collect the year-end tax adjustment forms, many people think their taxes for the year are done. But the moment you get your withholding tax slip (Gensen Choshu Hyo), that pile of medical receipts in your drawer is still sitting there, and your Furusato Nozei donation certificates haven't even been opened. These things aren't covered by the year-end tax adjustment; their time comes in February. 😅

For the 2026 tax year, the filing period is Tuesday, February 16 to Monday, March 15, 2027, with the payment deadline also on March 15. This year, the basic deduction has increased: for those with a total income of 4.89 million yen or less, the deduction is 1.04 million yen; for those between 4.89 million and 6.55 million yen, it is 670,000 yen. The minimum guaranteed amount for the employment income deduction has also increased from 650,000 to 740,000 yen. These figures are already being used for the December year-end tax adjustment (Nenmatsu Chosei), so be careful not to use old figures when filing your tax return.

The National Tax Agency provides a clear list of who cannot have their taxes settled by their company and must file a return themselves. Those with an annual salary exceeding 20 million yen are ineligible for year-end adjustment and must file independently. If you work for only one company but have other income exceeding 200,000 yen, you must file; this includes income from side gigs or speaking fees. If you work for two companies, the second salary is usually not included in the year-end adjustment; if that income plus other sources exceeds 200,000 yen, you must also file a return.

Others may have had too much tax withheld and need to file a tax return (kakutei shinkoku) to get a refund. Medical expense deductions cannot be handled through year-end tax adjustment (nenmatsu chosei). If the medical expenses you and your family paid throughout the year, minus any insurance reimbursements, exceed 100,000 yen, the excess amount can be deducted. For those with a total annual income of less than 2 million yen, the threshold is 5% of your income. The special deduction for housing loans, commonly known as 'housing loan deduction,' also requires you to file your own tax return for the first year; only from the second year onwards can it be handled through year-end tax adjustment. If you donated to more than 5 municipalities via Furusato Nozei, or if your One-Stop Exception application form didn't arrive by the deadline, you must file a tax return to claim the donation deduction. The situation is similar if you changed jobs mid-year and your new company did not include the withholding tax slip (gensen choshu hyo) from your previous employer in your year-end tax adjustment.

There is one small detail that is easily overlooked. If you are filing a tax return for medical expenses or the housing loan deduction, you must include your side income in the return, even if it is under 200,000 yen. That 200,000 yen threshold only applies to whether you 'need' to file; once you are standing in front of the tax return form, you cannot skip any income. Resident tax has a separate filing process—I've written about side income in another post, so don't confuse the two.

You don't have to wait until February to file a refund tax return (還付申告). If you are only filing for a refund, you can submit it starting January 1, 2027, and the deadline is 5 years. What you can do in December is simple: gather your withholding tax statement (源泉徴収票), receipts for the year's medical expenses, medical expense notifications from your health insurance provider, Furusato Nozei donation certificates, and deduction certificates for iDeCo and insurance into one folder. Your future self in February will thank you for not having to rummage through every drawer in the house the night before the deadline.

Quick summary

  • 2026 Tax Year Kakutei Shinkoku: February 16 to March 15, 2027. Tax payment deadline is also March 15.
  • Basic Deduction: 1.04 million yen for income up to 4.89 million yen; 670,000 yen for income between 4.89 million and 6.55 million yen. Minimum guaranteed employment income deduction: 740,000 yen.
  • Mandatory filing: Annual salary exceeding 20 million yen, side income exceeding 200,000 yen, or income from two companies where the unadjusted second salary plus other income exceeds 200,000 yen.
  • Deductions that cannot be handled through year-end tax adjustment and require you to file your own tax return: Medical expense deduction, first-year housing loan deduction, and Furusato Nozei if you donated to more than 5 municipalities or your One-Stop application was unsuccessful.
  • When filing a tax return for deductions, side income under 200,000 yen must also be declared. Tax refund filings can be submitted starting January 1, 2027, with a 5-year statute of limitations.

From the editor-in-chief

This article suggests putting receipts into a single folder in December, which makes a lot of sense. My own method is to stuff all my receipts for the year into an envelope in the entryway. When I open it in February, the medical receipts are sleeping soundly next to the split-bill receipts from izakayas, inseparable. I'll buy a folder this year, but I'll keep the envelope—maybe it will win again next year.
