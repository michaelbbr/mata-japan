Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.28

Omotesando's illumination this year is by Dior, lit from December 1 to 27.

Approximately 900,000 champagne-gold LED lights cover about 1 km of the zelkova tree-lined street. They are lit from sunset until 10:00 PM. It's free to walk and see, and there is also a Dior Christmas collaboration inside Omotesando Hills.

The Omotesando illuminations have a new sponsor this year, and the name has changed to Omotesando Dior Illumination 2026. It is hosted by the Harajuku Omotesando Keyaki-kai, with Dior as the sponsor. Last year it was Fendi, but this year it's Dior. 😊

The event runs from Tuesday, December 1, to Sunday, December 27, with the main lights on the Zelkova path shining for these 27 days. The lights on the low shrubs and planting areas will remain until Tuesday, January 5, 2027. If you go after December 28, only the low-level shrub lights will be on. The lighting time is listed as 'sunset to 10:00 PM'; the official site doesn't give a specific time, but sunset in Tokyo in December is around 4:30 PM. The route stretches about 1 kilometer from the Jingubashi intersection to the Omotesando intersection, with about 150 Zelkova trees on both sides decorated with roughly 900,000 lights in champagne gold. It's free, a street-walking style event that requires no tickets or reservations.

The illumination color this year is confirmed by the secretariat's public materials to be champagne gold, the same as in previous years. The scale figures—approximately 900,000 LEDs, about 150 zelkova trees, and about 1 kilometer—come from the secretariat's public materials and event information pages; the main text of the official organizer's website does not list these figures.

If you have time during the day, you can follow Yu's favorite walking route: from Shinjuku through Yoyogi, across Meiji Jingu, and then from Harajuku toward Omotesando. Yu once almost started running inside Meiji Jingu and only learned the rule after a guard noticed. Running is prohibited inside Meiji Jingu, and it closes at 4:00 PM in December, so start early if you want to take this route. If you are going to see the lights after work, just take the train to Harajuku Station or Omotesando Station. The walk from Omotesando Station Exit A2 toward Harajuku is downhill and easier. You can reach either end of the illumination stretch from Harajuku Station, Meiji-jingumae Station, or Omotesando Station.

Omotesando Hills also has a Dior Christmas collaboration, running from December 11 to December 27, ending on the same day as the street lights. The main building's atrium staircase features a Christmas tree inspired by powdered sugar and gingerbread. There is a pop-up store on B3F in Space O; it is free and reservation-priority, with reservations starting November 26. There is also a free Christmas treasure hunt where you visit 4 designated spots in the building to collect digital cards. The main entrance also has festive decorations.

You can also catch another illumination display on the same night. 'Ao no Dokutsu SHIBUYA 2026' runs from the Shibuya Koen-dori area to the Yoyogi Park Zelkova tree-lined path. This year, it’s from Friday, December 11, to Sunday, December 27, from 5:00 PM to 10:00 PM daily, featuring about 500,000 lights. Both events end on December 27, so between December 11 and 27, you can easily see both in one night. A new highlight for 2026 is the 'Ao no Dokutsu THE BLUE TREE,' a large digital billboard shaped like a Christmas tree on the Yoyogi Park Zelkova path, appearing for the first time. The two locations are close, so it's a convenient route to walk both in one evening.

Check the official website before you head out. The organizer's website currently lists the end date as December 27. Some early event summaries might say December 25, but stick to the official website and don't memorize the 25th. The official page hasn't announced specific measures for 2026, such as traffic lane restrictions or bans on stopping on pedestrian bridges. If you want to avoid the crowds, consider going on a weekday evening. It might be crowded on weekends and around Christmas, so be careful not to block other pedestrians when taking photos.

Tokyo nights are cold in December. If you plan on walking all the way to Shibuya, you'd better bring a scarf and gloves.

From the editor-in-chief

When visiting Omotesando for the illuminations, there's no need to hunt for the 'brightest' tree. Just walk from Harajuku toward Omotesando and stop to take a photo whenever you see a spot you like. The whole street is free anyway.
