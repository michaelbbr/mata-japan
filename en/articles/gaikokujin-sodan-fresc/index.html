When your paperwork gets stuck in a loop, there's a building in Yotsuya where you can ask for free advice.

Tokyo Immigration Bureau residency consultations are held at the Foreign Residents Support Center (FRESC). It is free, anonymous, and includes free interpretation. You can book both in-person and online appointments.

When dealing with bureaucracy in Japan, you sometimes get caught in a loop where both sides are waiting on each other. One counter says you're missing document A, and the office for document A says you're missing document B. You're stuck in the middle, clutching your files, which only seem to get heavier. 🏢

Opening a bank account was exactly like that. To open an account, they required a mobile number; when I went to get a phone plan, they required a bank account. Both sides made perfect sense, but together they formed an inescapable circle. It only moved forward because the staff member spent a long time communicating to bridge the gap. I only got through it because I met someone willing to take the time to make it work; the rules themselves didn't soften. If I had gone to a different window or dealt with a different person, the result might have been completely different.

When you can't get a straight answer, there's a building in Yotsuya you can go to. The Foreign Residents Support Center (FRESC) is located in Shinjuku Ward, Tokyo. The address is 13F, Yotsuya Tower, CO-MO-RE YOTSUYA, 1-6 Yotsuya, Shinjuku Ward, about a 1-minute walk from Yotsuya Station. It is open on weekdays from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM and closed on weekends, national holidays, and during the New Year holidays.

Let's clarify its scope. FRESC's residence consultation handles residence status and identity matters. It cannot process commercial procedures like opening a bank account or applying for a phone plan, nor can it force a bank to make exceptions. What it can do is help you isolate the issues behind your status, such as which identity document is incorrect or which step in the process was wrong. Once you have that clear, you'll have something to say when you return to the counter.

Tokyo Immigration Bureau residency consultations are held right here. The consultation is free, and if you need an interpreter, that is also free—don't worry about the cost. If you are worried about leaving a record, you can consult anonymously. Appointments are required. You can visit FRESC for a meeting or use Microsoft Teams for an online consultation, so those living far away don't need to make a special trip to Tokyo.

There are two ways to book an appointment. You can call 03-5363-3025, which supports 21 languages, including Chinese. For online appointments, please submit your request at least 3 business days before your desired consultation date, using either Japanese or English. Note that the appointment line is only for scheduling; they do not provide consultations over the phone. If you want to ask questions directly by phone, you should call a consultation window like the Foreign Residents Support Center. The two services have different roles, and if you mix them up, you'll just be waiting for nothing.

The list of supported languages is long. Chinese, English, Korean, Spanish, Portuguese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Nepali, Indonesian, Thai, and Easy Japanese are all included. If your Japanese isn't fluent enough yet, be sure to request an interpreter when making your appointment.

FRESC houses several other offices on the same floor. The Tokyo Labor Bureau's special consultation for foreigners, the Tokyo Foreign Employment Service Center, the Tokyo Legal Affairs Bureau's Human Rights Department, visa information from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Japan Legal Support Center (Houterasu), and JETRO are all on the same floor. The Immigration Services Agency notes that this floor brings together 8 different agencies. If your residence status is stuck, causing issues with your job or contract, at least you don't have to run between several different buildings. However, each office has different responsibilities, appointment requirements, and language support, so check which office handles your specific issue when you arrive. Don't assume you can just ask anyone on the floor for everything.

If you live outside of Tokyo, don't think this building has nothing to do with you. First, check the official website of your own ward, city, or town to see if there is a consultation window for foreigners. The content and languages supported vary by municipality, so just check before you go. Online consultation was originally created for those who live far away.

When you're running in circles with paperwork, the free consultation service is incredibly practical. Bring exactly what both counters told you, show them which document was rejected and why, and lay it all out at once. That way, the staff can actually figure out where the breakdown is happening. Next time you're being pushed back and forth, instead of standing helplessly at the counter, take that whole loop to Yotsuya and get it cleared up.

Quick summary

  • FRESC is located at Yotsuya Tower 13F, 1-6-1 Yotsuya, Shinjuku Ward. It is about a 1-minute walk from Yotsuya Station. Hours are 9:00 to 17:00 on weekdays; closed on weekends and national holidays.
  • The Tokyo Immigration Bureau offers free residency consultations with free interpretation. You can remain anonymous, and it is by appointment only. You can choose between an in-person meeting or a consultation via Microsoft Teams. Residency consultations cover status of residence and identity matters; commercial applications like opening bank accounts or getting phone numbers are not handled here.
  • For phone appointments, call 03-5363-3025. They support 21 languages. Online appointments must be made at least 3 business days in advance. Please note that the appointment line itself does not provide consultations.
  • Eight different service counters—covering immigration, labor, employment, human rights, visas, and legal affairs—are gathered on the same floor. The main Navi Dial number is 0570-011000. Please note that each counter has different responsibilities, appointment procedures, and language support.
  • If you live outside of Tokyo, first check your local municipality's official website for a consultation window for foreigners, as content and languages vary by municipality.
