Administrative reminders for next week, YU segment

November 23, 2026, is a Monday and also Kinro Kansha no Hi (Labor Thanksgiving Day). According to the Cabinet Office, this national holiday is fixed on November 23rd to honor labor, celebrate production, and encourage mutual gratitude among citizens. Since it falls right after the weekend, it creates a three-day long weekend. On national holidays, city offices and bank counters are closed, so if you go to handle paperwork, you'll just find the shutters down. Please schedule your errands for Tuesday, November 24th or later. Experience shows that Tuesday mornings are usually crowded, so if you're in a rush, make sure you have all your documents ready so you don't have to get back in line because you're missing a single photocopy. 🎫 Don't get your hopes up for Friday afternoon before a long weekend either; that's when everyone rushes to finish their business before the break. If your task isn't urgent, waiting until Tuesday afternoon is actually much quieter, and you'll have your pick of seats in the waiting area.

Weekend activities and shopping, AYAKA segment

For those looking to get out this weekend, there is an event in Yurakucho that brings the whole of Tokyo into one building. The TOKYO Shukaku-sai 2026 will be held at the Tokyo International Forum on Saturday, November 21, from 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM, and Sunday, November 22, from 10:00 AM to 5:00 PM. Admission is free, and the venue is just a one-minute walk from JR Yurakucho Station. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government and the Tokyo Convention & Visitors Bureau have brought together tourism associations from the 23 wards, Tama, and the islands. This year, 23 groups are exhibiting, featuring food trucks, local specialties, and regional sake. There will also be a footbath experience and hands-on workshops that require a lottery entry. Saturday’s workshop involves sanding nameplates made of high-quality wood, while Sunday’s features making food samples of shaved ice. With different content each day, walking through the event feels like getting a preview of all your future short trip destinations. The local specialties section is particularly useful for those living in Tokyo; items from the islands usually require a boat trip to acquire, but they will all be lined up in Yurakucho this weekend—you could easily knock out half your year-end gift list here. A heads-up for the following weekend: Sunday, November 29, is the 46th Japan Cup at Tokyo Racecourse. No tickets will be sold at the gate. Admission is 1,000 yen per person, available only via online reservation. The lottery application period runs from 6:00 PM on Sunday, November 22, to 1:00 PM on Tuesday, November 24. If you're interested, remember to submit your application after you finish exploring the event this weekend.

This week's 'mine-stepping' report, 腸鍋 rants

The protagonist of this story went to the racetrack for the first time. After exiting the station, he had no idea where to go. He scanned the crowd, picked out a middle-aged man who looked like a regular gambler, and followed him—and it actually worked. When YU heard this, he could only say that while he doesn't have a knack for finding his way, his eye for people is spot on. Once inside, it got even more exciting. He bought tickets without understanding the rules, felt the tension while clutching his betting slips, and ended up losing 10,000 yen. His wallet thinned out fast. He originally thought the racetrack was just for older men, but he was surprised to see so many young men and women there, with couples and groups of friends filling the rows, looking even more at home than he was. YU doesn't recommend following strangers to find your way—it relies too much on luck. Next time you go to Fuchu, just look up at the station signs; if you take your time, you won't lose 10,000 yen.
