Don't rush to submit the 年末調整 forms when you receive them; getting these items wrong will be troublesome

The 令和 8 年分 revision raises the basic deduction to 620,000 to 1,040,000 yen, the income walls move with it, and side income and medical expenses still need to be filed yourself

There's a lot of paper. Feeling sleepy halfway through is normal; the quick summary is at the end of the article, just check against it once before submitting the form.

The stack of papers your company gives you in November is for the year-end tax adjustment (Nenmatsu Chosei). It’s a simple process: the company compares the total income tax withheld from your salary throughout the year with the actual tax amount calculated at the end of the year. If they withheld too much, you get a refund; if they withheld too little, you pay the difference. This process is only handled by your company if you are a regular employee with an annual salary under 20 million yen and have submitted your deduction declaration forms (such as for dependents). If you didn't submit those forms, you aren't covered by the year-end tax adjustment. This is generally the scope defined by the National Tax Agency.

The scope of deductions the company handles in 年末調整 is wider than many people think. The basic deduction, 配偶者控除 and 配偶者特別控除, 扶養控除, and 所得金額調整控除 are all included. Life insurance, earthquake insurance, social insurance, and 小規模企業共濟 and similar contribution deductions also go through here, and iDeCo contributions count under 小規模企業共濟等掛金控除. The housing loan deduction can enter 年末調整 from the second year onward, but not in the first year; this is the rule most easily remembered backwards.

This year's figures are different from last year's. This year is for the Reiwa 8 tax year. The basic deduction ranges from 620,000 to 1.04 million yen based on total income; for those with a total income of 4.89 million yen or less, it is a flat 1.04 million yen (for those with higher income, the basic deduction is reduced in stages to 480,000, 320,000, and 160,000 yen, and drops to zero if total income exceeds 25.95 million yen). The minimum guaranteed amount for the employment income deduction has been raised to 740,000 yen. With both figures changing, the 'income wall' has shifted: the income requirement for dependents is now 620,000 yen, which translates to 1.36 million yen in employment income. For those whose only income is a salary, the threshold for when income tax begins to be levied is now approximately 1.78 million yen. Note that this is the threshold for income tax; the thresholds for resident tax and social insurance dependency are different, so don't confuse them. Last year's table is obsolete, so check the new figures before filling out your forms.

🧾 What 年末調整 cannot do, you make up for yourself with a 確定申告 from February to March the following year. Among those listed by the National Tax Agency as subject to 確定申告, the ones most relevant to employees include people with annual salary over ¥20 million, people whose total income other than salary exceeds ¥200,000, and people who receive salary from two or more sources at the same time and meet the filing conditions. The ¥200,000 here refers to income, the amount after deducting necessary expenses from revenue: side-business revenue of ¥300,000 with expenses of ¥120,000 gives income of ¥180,000, while revenue of ¥400,000 with expenses of ¥120,000 gives income of ¥280,000, and the conclusions are completely different.

醫療費控除, 雜損控除 and the first year of 住宅貸款控除 have no fields in 年末調整, so if you want to use them you have to file yourself. The donation deduction for 故鄉納稅 is the same, except for people using the ワンストップ特例. There is another place with a high rate of being missed: employees whose side-job income is 200,000 yen or less may, if certain conditions are met, be exempt from filing a final income tax return. But if you already need to file a final return for other matters, the situation is different. Resident tax needs to be checked separately; even if you do not need to file for income tax, you may still need to file with your municipal 市區役所.

Before handing the form back, take a few minutes to confirm the annual income of your dependent family members, and don't forget to attach the insurance deduction certificates. If you find a mistake after submitting, first ask the company if it can be corrected; if 年末調整 is already complete, check whether you need to file 確定申告 yourself.

Quick summary

  • 年末調整 is your company reconciling your taxes for you; salaried workers with an annual salary of 20 million yen or less who have submitted their declaration forms are mostly within its scope
  • Reiwa 8 basic deduction: 620,000 to 1.04 million yen; minimum employment income deduction: 740,000 yen; dependency wall: 1.36 million yen; income tax threshold: approximately 1.78 million yen.
  • The company can handle insurance deductions and the housing loan deduction from the second year onward; medical expense deductions, casualty loss deductions, and the first year of a housing loan require your own 確定申告
  • For side work, it is income that matters, not revenue. If the combined total exceeds 200,000 yen, you must file; if it is 200,000 yen or less, 住民稅 still mostly needs to be filed with the 市区役所
  • This year is for 令和 8 年分; before submitting the form, check dependents' annual income and insurance certificates once

From the editor-in-chief

When that packet of 年末調整 papers is handed out, every year I first stuff the whole packet into a drawer, and only on the day before the deadline do I take it out and fill it in slowly, following the example. Those dependent fields look the same every year, and every year I have to study them again from scratch; after studying I forget, and next year it continues.
