If you want a Taiwanese breakfast in Japan, on your day off you have to take the train to go find that bowl of savory soy milk

There are specialty shops in Gotanda and Iidabashi, Oshiage has limited-time danbing from November 20, and at home you can also make it yourself using soy milk and vinegar as the base

The homesickness article isn't long on words, but the road takes a bit of walking. For those who don't want to go far, there's a quick summary at the end of the article, and the at-home option is written there too.

For people living in Tokyo who miss Taiwanese breakfast, homesickness is sometimes very concrete. A hot bowl of savory soy milk in the early morning, the aroma of soy with a little vinegar sourness, youtiao soaked in it half soft and half crisp, and next to it a freshly pan-fried egg pancake, a little charred at the edges, the aroma of egg mixed with scallion. This kind of longing usually strikes on holiday mornings. You can buy soy milk at the supermarket, but drinking it alone always feels like it's missing that bowl of savory soy milk, so some people change their clothes, go out, and take the train to find it.

Places to find Taiwanese breakfast in Tokyo are more concentrated than you might think. 'Tokyo Toujan Seikatsu' in Gotanda is a specialty shop located at 1-20-3 Nishi-Gotanda, Shinagawa Ward. They make the soy milk in-house, and the menu includes savory soy milk, shaobing, rice balls, and congee. It's open weekdays from 8:00 AM to 3:00 PM, and weekends/holidays from 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM, closing once sold out. It gets crowded on weekends, so check their official Instagram for announcements on holidays or changes in business hours before heading out to avoid a wasted trip. For those craving a Taiwanese breakfast, having a specialty shop like this makes it a destination worth traveling for.

Wanna Manna in Iidabashi has a different atmosphere. The shop is on the second floor of Iidabashi Sakura Terrace, 2-10-2 Fujimi, Chiyoda Ward. The official shop page says it is open from 8:00 AM to 7:00 PM, seven days a week. The menu includes house-made soy milk, savory soy milk, signature rice balls, egg crepes, and sesame flatbread. Breakfast items are sold all day, so you can eat them even after work. For those living along the Tozai Line or Sobu Line, the location is very convenient. The seats by the window are bright, and if you sit down and eat slowly, the steam from the soy milk will fog up your glasses.

🥣 There is a limited-time option in Oshiage in November. The Taiwan Sweet Shop at Tokyo Solamachi is collaborating with its sister brand, Wanna Manna, from November 20 to December 20. Savory soy milk is 600 yen, and dan bing is also available; order both together for 100 yen off. A dessert shop is usually for afternoon tea, but having a bowl of savory soy milk while shopping and sitting down to eat feels a bit unconventional, but it is a nice feeling. The advantage of this limited-time offer is the low barrier to entry, but the downside is that it ends after December 20, so mark your calendar if you want to try it.

On days when you can't go far, you can get closer at home. The barrier to making savory soy milk is surprisingly low. The published recipe ratio is 200 ml of soy milk, with vinegar, soy sauce, a little salt, and dried sakura shrimp. After heating the soy milk, pour it into the seasonings to let it slightly coagulate into a tofu-like texture. Fried dough sticks (yutiao) are hard to buy in Japan, but even without that ingredient, the taste can still be 70-80% similar. In the kitchen on a Sunday morning, the pot simmers quietly, and outside the window is the silence of Tokyo. The moment the bowl is placed on the table, the distance between us is no more than the length of a bowl.

If you're craving it, there are places to go in Gotanda and Iidabashi. You can also try the limited-time menu in Oshiage from November 20 to December 20. If you really don't want to go out, just follow a recipe and make a bowl at home—it's fine if you don't have the fried dough sticks.

  • Tokyo Tounyu Seikatsu in Gotanda is a specialty shop, with savory soy milk, shaobing, and rice balls. Check the official Instagram for opening announcements before heading out
  • Iidabashi wanna manna, 8 in the morning to 7 in the evening, open all year, with breakfast items sold until night

  • If you live far away, it's more practical to schedule this meal for a weekend.
