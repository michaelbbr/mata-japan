If you want a Taiwanese breakfast in Japan, on your day off you have to take the train to go find that bowl of savory soy milk

There are specialty shops in Gotanda and Iidabashi, Oshiage has limited-time danbing from November 20, and at home you can also make it yourself using soy milk and vinegar as the base

The homesickness article isn't long on words, but the road takes a bit of walking. For those who don't want to go far, there's a quick summary at the end of the article, and the at-home option is written there too.

For people living in Tokyo who miss Taiwanese breakfast, homesickness is sometimes very concrete. A hot bowl of savory soy milk in the early morning, the aroma of soy with a little vinegar sourness, youtiao soaked in it half soft and half crisp, and next to it a freshly pan-fried egg pancake, a little charred at the edges, the aroma of egg mixed with scallion. This kind of longing usually strikes on holiday mornings. You can buy soy milk at the supermarket, but drinking it alone always feels like it's missing that bowl of savory soy milk, so some people change their clothes, go out, and take the train to find it.

Places where you can find Taiwanese breakfast in Tokyo are more concentrated than you might imagine. Tokyo Soy Milk Life in Gotanda is a specialty shop, located at 1-20-3 Nishi-Gotanda, Shinagawa Ward. The soy milk is ground in-store, and the menu lists salty soy milk, shaobing, rice balls, and congee. It's open from morning to afternoon and closes when sold out. Weekends get crowded, and the official Instagram announces holidays and changes to opening hours, so check it before you head out to avoid a wasted trip. For people who want Taiwanese breakfast, having a specialty shop like this gives you a place worth making a train trip for.

wanna manna in Iidabashi has a different atmosphere. The shop is on the second floor of Iidabashi Sakura Terrace, at 2-10-2 Fujimi, Chiyoda Ward, and the official shop page says it is open from 8 in the morning to 7 in the evening, all year round. The menu has house-made soy milk, savory soy milk, signature rice balls, 蛋餅 and 燒餅, and breakfast items are sold all the way until night, so you can have them after work too. For people living along the Tozai Line and Sobu Line, the distance feels much friendlier; the seats by the window are bright, you sit down and eat slowly, and the steam from the soy milk fogs up your glasses.

🥣 In Oshiage, November brought one more limited-time option. The 晴空塔 Solamachi store of 台灣甜商店 is offering a collaboration menu with its sister brand wanna manna from November 20 to December 20, with savory soy milk for ¥600 and 蛋餅 also appearing at the same time, and ¥100 off if you order both together. A dessert shop is normally a place for afternoon tea, and now it has an extra bowl of savory soy milk; sitting down to eat halfway through shopping, the order is a bit mixed up, but it puts you in a good mood. The advantage of this kind of limited-time offer is that the barrier is low; the disadvantage is that after December 20 it is gone, so anyone who wants to try it should make a note in their calendar.

On days when you cannot go far, you can still get a little closer at home. Savory soy milk is surprisingly easy to make; a published recipe uses 200 milliliters of soy milk with vinegar, soy sauce, a little salt and 櫻花蝦, heating the soy milk and pouring it into the seasonings so it sets slightly into a tofu-pudding-like texture. 油條 are hard to buy in Japan, and without that one element the flavor still gets about seventy to eighty percent of the way there. On a Sunday morning in the kitchen, the pot simmering quietly, Tokyo silent outside the window, the moment the bowl is brought to the table, the distance is only as long as one bowl.

When you want to eat it, there are places to go in both Gotanda and Iidabashi. From November 20 to December 20, you can also try the limited menu in Oshiage. If you really don't want to go out, make a bowl at home with soy milk and vinegar; it's fine even without youtiao.
