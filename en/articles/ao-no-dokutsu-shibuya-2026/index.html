Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.26

Shibuya Ao no Dokutsu is only lit for 17 days this year, with two extra days after Christmas.

Ao no Dokutsu SHIBUYA: December 11 to 27, every night from 5:00 PM to 10:00 PM. About 500,000 blue lights shine from Koen-dori to the Zelkova trees in Yoyogi Park. This year, there's an added Christmas tree sign that displays images.

- Period: December 11 (Fri) to December 27 (Sun), 2026, for a total of 17 days - Time: 17:00 to 22:00 (Illumination times and content may change due to rain or other conditions; check the official website before heading out) - Location: From Shibuya Koen-dori to the Zelkova trees at Yoyogi Park - Access: 10-minute walk from JR Harajuku Station or Tokyo Metro Meiji-jingumae Station; also accessible on foot from Shibuya Station, Yoyogi-koen Station, and Yoyogi-hachiman Station - Admission: No admission fees or reservation methods are listed in the official event summary. The event takes place along the streets and park trees, so it is viewed as you walk along. - Organizer: "Ao no Dokutsu SHIBUYA" Executive Committee (Shibuya Koen-dori Shopping Street Promotion Association, Tokyo Metropolitan Park Association, Yoyogi Park Service Center, etc.), supported by Shibuya Ward, with special sponsorship from Nisshin Seifun Welna

In the winter in Shibuya, there is a street that only has one color. Only blue. 😊

Ao no Dokutsu SHIBUYA is lit from December 11 to December 27 this year, every night from 5:00 PM to 10:00 PM. About 500,000 lights are all blue, stretching from Shibuya Koen-dori all the way to the Zelkova trees in Yoyogi Park. Walking into the tree-lined path, the branches above your head, the road at your feet, and even your own white breath are stained with a layer of blue. The cold air presses against your face, but your eyes feel quiet, and your pace slows down unconsciously.

There is a new face this year. At the end of the Zelkova tree-lined path in Yoyogi Park, a large Christmas tree-shaped display has been set up called 'Ao no Dokutsu THE BLUE TREE.' It plays Christmas-themed videos, and the tree's 'expression' changes as the scenes shift. The giant snowman balloon from last year is taking a break; this one stands in the blue lights, positioned right at the end of the path, waiting for you only after you've walked the entire way.

The path has two different atmospheres. The Koen-dori section is a gentle uphill slope with shops still lit up on both sides; the blue lights blend into the streetlights and window displays, making it feel like a colorful version of everyday Shibuya. Once you step into the Zelkova tree-lined path, the shops fall away, and there's nothing above you but branches and lights. The blue becomes very pure, the voices of the crowd soften, and the warmth of the hot coffee in your hand, felt through your gloves, is just enough to ward off the chill at your fingertips.

It didn't start in Shibuya. It began in Nakameguro in 2014 and moved here in 2016. It was suspended for two years during the pandemic and returned in 2022. Counting last year, it has been held in Shibuya 8 times, attracting over 16.5 million visitors. Even though the location changed, the color has remained the same; those who recognize this blue return every winter. The numbers sound impressive, and the crowds are very real. For those living in Tokyo, it's best to fit it into your commute home—just take a detour and walk a section of it rather than specifically going on a weekend to fight the crowds. To avoid the peak, consider going on a weekday, or right when the lights turn on at 5:00 PM, or after 9:00 PM when it's closer to closing time; the pace will be more relaxed. You don't have to enter from the Shibuya Station side either. Get off at Harajuku Station or Meiji-jingumae Station, walk 10 minutes to the Zelkova tree end, and slowly walk back toward Koen-dori; the route is just as complete.

If you only want to see what's new this year, head straight for the Zelkova tree-lined path. The video on THE BLUE TREE keeps changing; standing in front of the tree waiting for a full cycle to finish, with the cold wind pressing your scarf against your face and the blue light flickering slowly through the branches, is actually quite pleasant. Most people taking photos gather in that area, so if you want a cleaner shot of the path, walk a few steps toward Koen-dori for a clearer view.

There's a small detail about the dates worth noting. It stays open for two extra days after Christmas—December 26 and 27—which happen to be the weekend. It's scheduled to stay lit until 10:00 PM, so if you were too busy during Christmas week, you can still make it at the end of the year. The entire event lasts only 17 days, and the lighting hours or content may change due to bad weather, so check the official website for a minute before heading out to avoid a wasted trip.

This article suggests picking a weekday or a later time to walk through slowly, which makes a lot of sense. In the end, I still dragged myself there the weekend before Christmas. Standing in the crowd on Koen-dori, my arm got tired from holding up my phone, and half the photos I took were of the back of the head of the person in front of me. The blue lights are beautiful, and the back of the head is also very clear. Remind me to go on a weekday next year.
