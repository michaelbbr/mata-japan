You cannot run at Meiji Jingu, no matter how wide the approach, it is still a shrine

No matter how cool the shade or how wide the 參道, the official website says it clearly: no running within the grounds

Runners living in Tokyo have probably all had this thought: run from Shinjuku all the way to Harajuku, cutting through the forest of Meiji Jingu on the way. The shade is cool and the approach is wide; it is basically a ready-made running course in the city center.

I've had this thought too. The worship etiquette page on the Meiji Jingu official website is very clear: please do not run in the precincts, and please do not engage in sports. No matter how much that forest looks like a park, at its core it's a shrine precinct; people come to worship and to walk quietly, and if you're running laps beside them, you feel noisy yourself even without a guard saying anything.

Some people might think it's fine as long as you run slowly. But what Meiji Jingu prohibits is running within the shrine grounds itself, and jogging counts too. No matter how wide the 參道 is, leave it for people visiting the shrine and taking a stroll.

🌲 I now plan shrine visits and running separately. If I want to walk through the forest at Meiji Shrine, I walk; if I want to run, I go separately to Jingu Gaien. One lap around Gaien is about 1.3 km, which is good for a short run, and that way I do not have to worry about accidentally running inside the shrine grounds. Arranging it this way has another practical benefit: the walking part is just the right distance for a warm-up, so by the time I start running my body is already warmed up and my stride is actually steadier.

You should also check the times. The shrine's opening and closing follow sunrise and sunset and are different every month, and it closes early in winter. People who only go out for a walk in the afternoon should check the monthly timetable on the official website before departing, to avoid walking to the entrance and finding only an iron gate and the wind.

Source materials

  • Meiji Jingu official website explanation of worship times: opening and closing follow sunrise and sunset and are adjusted by month; before departing, please check the monthly timetable on the official website (same site)

From the editor-in-chief

The first time I walked the approach to the shrine at Jingu, my steps really did speed up on their own, the road is straight and flat, and I was already calculating lap times in my head. Seeing the people beside me walking slowly, I pulled my pace back. The hardest part of this road is not running, it is holding yourself back from running.
