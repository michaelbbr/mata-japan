業務スーパー is open to the general public too. Is it worth visiting if you live in Japan?

Frozen foods are good for stocking up; for fresh foods, compare prices more

The name "業務スーパー" really does look, at first glance, like a wholesale store supplying restaurants. Can ordinary people actually go in and shop there?

Yes, you can! Gyomu Super (nicknamed 'Gyo-Su') is a food supermarket chain operated by Kobe Bussan. You can even see 'General customers welcome' written at the storefront, meaning regular consumers are welcome to shop there. It focuses on 'Everyday Low Price.' Some products are displayed directly in cardboard boxes, and combined with large-capacity frozen foods, processed foods, and imported goods, the prices are very competitive. However, actual prices do fluctuate, so don't assume it will be just as cheap every time you go.

So, is it really useful for living in Japan? My answer is: used as part of a division of labor, it is very useful. 業スー is strongest for frozen vegetables, frozen meat products, large-pack seasonings and imported foods from various countries. For Chinese residents in Japan, you can also often find ingredients and seasonings close to the taste of home here; it is a lifesaver when you want to cook a familiar flavor. But for fresh fruit and vegetables and fish and meat to eat that day, I still compare prices at a regular supermarket near home, such as ライフ, or a discount supermarket like OK: 業スー's strength is “stocking up,” not “freshness for tonight's meal.”

🛒 Before I go, I use the store locator on the official website to find the nearest location. Stores in the Tokyo metropolitan area are dense, but business hours vary, and the selection of fresh food and alcohol differs by store, so check before you head out. If it's your first time, only buy what fits in your freezer—large volumes are a benefit but also a trap, as freezers in Tokyo studio apartments are tiny. Check the payment methods before checking out; some stores have limited support for electronic payments, so carrying cash or a credit card is safer (varies by store).

After living in Tokyo for more than twenty years, I've long treated supermarkets like a map to read: which one for stocking up, which one for tonight, which one for a taste of home. 業スー occupies a very solid corner on that map.
