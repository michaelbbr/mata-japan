Chinese residents in Japan · YU · 2026.11.25

The number of foreigners living in Japan has reached 4.28 million, with Taiwanese ranking 10th.

The latest figures released by the Immigration Services Agency in October 2026 show 4.28 million people, with 820,000 in Tokyo alone. Among the 73,628 Taiwanese residents, the largest group consists of Permanent Residents.

For those in a hurry, just look at the numbers in the summary at the end of the article. The main text is about what these numbers mean for those of us living here. The Immigration Services Agency released the foreign resident statistics for the end of June 2026 on October 16 of this year, with a total of 4,281,026 people, hitting a new high. The figure at the end of last year was 4,125,395, crossing the 4 million mark for the first time. Six months later, it has increased by another 155,631, a growth rate of 3.8%.

This statistic covers mid-to-long-term residents and special permanent residents. Tourists with short-term stay status and those with a stay of 3 months or less are not included. Whether it's 4.12 million or 4.28 million, it refers to people who are truly living their lives in Japan. Those of us living here can really feel this number—at the ward office counters, on station announcements, and with the staff in supermarkets, Japanese is no longer the only language being used. 😅

By nationality/region, the top is China (941,031), followed by Vietnam (693,750), South Korea (408,682), the Philippines (362,761), and Nepal (334,850). Taiwan ranks 10th with 73,628 people, an increase of 372 from the end of last year. While the ranking doesn't seem high, the breakdown is more interesting: the largest group of Taiwanese are Permanent Residents (26,762), followed by 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' (17,503), Students (8,856), and Spouses of Japanese Nationals (4,760). There is only 1 Technical Intern and 399 Specified Skilled Workers, a structure completely different from Vietnam or Nepal, which rely on technical internships and specified skills. Taiwanese in Japan generally follow the path of studying, working, marrying, and staying.

By visa status, the top category is Permanent Resident (961,342)—roughly one in four foreigners is a permanent resident. Second is 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' (516,184), which grew by 40,394 in just six months, showing a rapid increase in full-time office workers. Third is Student (479,626), fourth is Technical Intern (457,509), and fifth is Specified Skilled Worker (444,256). Specified Skilled Workers saw the largest increase among the top five, growing by 53,960 in six months, as industries facing labor shortages continue to recruit from abroad.

Where people live is also very concentrated. Tokyo has 824,486 residents, accounting for 19.3% of the total—roughly one in five foreigners lives in Tokyo. Next are Osaka Prefecture (389,846), Aichi Prefecture (369,849), Kanagawa Prefecture (331,515), and Saitama Prefecture (305,867). The total for the Greater Tokyo Area is substantial. It's no surprise that ward offices in the capital region have set up consultation counters for foreigners and that there are more notices in Chinese and Vietnamese at stations when you look at these numbers.

Even as the numbers grow, the friction encountered by newcomers hasn't decreased. I've written before about the catch-22 between opening a bank account and getting a mobile phone number. Yu's experience was exactly like that: the bank needed a phone number, and the phone company needed a bank account. It was only resolved because a staff member was willing to take the time to communicate. 4.28 million is the total, but for each individual, it still comes down to those ten minutes of back-and-forth at the counter. Statistics show the population is growing, but whether the administrative procedures have kept up is something those of us living here observe every year.

Quick summary

  • Latest statistics (as of the end of June 2026, released by the Immigration Services Agency on October 16, 2026): 4,281,026 foreign residents. An increase of 155,631 in six months. Statistics do not include tourists or short-term visitors.
  • Top 3 by nationality: China (941,031), Vietnam (693,750), South Korea (408,682); Taiwan is 10th with 73,628.
  • Composition of Taiwanese residents: Permanent Residents are the largest group at 26,762, followed by 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' at 17,503, and Students at 8,856.
  • The top visa status is Permanent Resident (961,342); the fastest-growing is Specified Skilled Worker, which increased by 53,960 to 444,256 in six months.
  • Tokyo accounts for 824,486 people, or 19.3%. The capital region remains the area with the highest concentration of foreign residents.

From the editor-in-chief

4.28 million people—it sounds like a distant number. What felt more real was waiting at the ward office, looking at a row of chairs filled with people from all over the world. Everyone was clutching the same white numbered slip, staring at the screen waiting for their turn. In that moment, I understood that statistics aren't just abstract; they mean there are exactly eleven people standing in front of me.
