On payday evening, for people living alone, convenience store or supermarket, which to choose

The 25th is payday at many companies, and the small indulgence and half-price bento that evening are the most honest meal of the whole month

Payday at many Japanese companies falls on the 25th. Besides the 25th, the 15th and 20th are also common; these days are called 五十日 in Japan, a set of dates left over from merchants' old habits of settling accounts and making payments, and companies followed in choosing these days as paydays. The Labor Standards Act only stipulates that salary must be paid at least once a month and the date must be fixed; which day to pay is decided by the company itself. The benefit of paying on the 25th is very practical: a pile of deductions and bills are lined up at month-end after it, so the money comes in first and your mind settles first.

On the evening of payday, people living alone tend to make one extra stop on the way home. The convenience store is the most on-the-way kind of treat. Normally you'd check the price when buying canned coffee, but that evening your hand reaches for the dessert case on its own: pudding, Swiss roll, a slightly better beer, none of them expensive individually. The amount isn't large. Added together, it won't hurt next month either, but the satisfaction is complete. Eating alone means you don't have to match anyone else's taste. What you buy depends entirely on what you feel like eating in the moment, and that freedom is especially clear on payday evening. 🌙

Another group of people detour to the supermarket. Prepared foods and fresh foods at supermarkets start getting discount stickers in the evening, and the closer to closing, the more aggressive they get. The common pattern is a small discount appearing in the evening, then later becoming 30% or 50% off, with half price mostly appearing in the final period. Each shop's timing and method differ, and weather and that day's customer numbers also affect it; on days when things sell well, nothing is left on the shelves by the half-price round. People standing in front of the prepared foods section on payday evening are calculating a different account in their minds: I got paid today, should I buy the good stuff at full price, or wait for half price to appear before buying? That waiting itself is enjoyable; standing there watching the labels go on one by one, dinner gradually takes shape.

People who cook for themselves also upgrade a bit that evening. Usually they buy chicken, that day they switch to beef; usually they buy frozen dumplings, that day they buy fresh shrimp to pan-fry themselves; the food is still home-cooked, the ingredients jump up a level, it costs less than eating out, and the satisfaction is no less. A solo kitchen is small, one burner and one pot, the variations you can make are limited, so better ingredients themselves are the easiest upgrade.

There is a survey that matches this feeling. In 2022, ビズヒッツ asked 500 people, and on average they began to feel short of money 6.2 days before payday; among the reasons for feeling short before payday, one type of answer was spending too freely in the week after payday, with those few treats for yourself adding up to quite a lot when you look back at the end of the month. Spending on the night of payday is spending the feeling of finally making it through a month; there is nothing wrong with that in itself, what is wrong is that it keeps flowing easily into the next week.

So the trade-offs on a payday night are probably like this. The small luxury of a convenience store is expensive for its convenience, the half-price items at a supermarket are cheap in exchange for time, and upgrading a home-cooked meal sits in between: spend a little, eat a little better. Either choice is fine, just do not choose in a way that makes the next 6.2 days arrive early.

From the editor-in-chief

That payday evening, I stood in front of the convenience store dessert shelf longer than usual, and in the end picked a pudding, and also took a can of beer I wouldn't normally buy. The amount wasn't much more, but my steps felt lighter on the way home. This kind of small spending feels the least guilty.
