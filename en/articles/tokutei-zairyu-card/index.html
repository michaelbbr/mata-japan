Life Information · YU · 2026.11.22

The Residence Card and My Number Card have been merged into one, effective as of June 2026.

Application for the 'Specified Residence Card' is optional and must be done alongside other residence procedures; delivery takes at least two weeks.

If you're in a hurry, feel free to skip the whole thing and just look at the summary at the bottom. Starting June 14, 2026, the 'Specified Residence Card' will be introduced, merging the Residence Card and the My Number Card into one. Local immigration offices will begin accepting applications the following day, June 15. Medium- to long-term residents registered in the Basic Resident Register are eligible to apply. For Special Permanent Residents, the corresponding document is called the 'Specified Special Permanent Resident Certificate.' 💳

Obtaining it is completely optional. It won't be automatically converted into one card; you can continue to hold both cards separately. The feeling of lightness from having one less card in your wallet is probably the most practical appeal of merging them. No need to rush.

The most convenient part of merging them is after finishing procedures at the Immigration Services Agency. Previously, if your period of stay changed, you had to go to the municipal office to update your My Number card information after finishing at the immigration office. With the 'Specified Residence Card,' the My Number information is updated simultaneously when the immigration procedure is complete, saving you a trip to the municipal office. Dealing with banks asking for phone numbers and mobile carriers asking for bank accounts—the staff often have to coordinate for a long time to get things done. If I can avoid even one of those scenes, it's worth it.

For residence cards issued on or after June 14, 2026, the period of stay, type of permission, date of permission, and date of issuance will no longer be printed on the card face; these items will instead be stored in the IC chip. The 'Specified Residence Card' will retain fields for name, date of birth, address, status of residence, and period of stay, with the Individual Number printed on the back. Companies or schools needing to verify these items can, with the holder's consent, use the Immigration Services Agency's residence card reader app to scan the chip. Per official specifications, the date of issuance cannot be displayed via the app.

You cannot apply for the 'Specified Residence Card' separately; it must be done in conjunction with other procedures, such as renewing your period of stay, changing your status of residence, applying for permanent residence, reissuing a residence card, or when registering your address at a municipal office. If you don't have any upcoming procedures but want to switch to the merged card, you can apply for a 'Reissuance of Residence Card' (requesting a switch) and submit the 'Specified Residence Card' application at the same time at a regional immigration office. The application for the 'Specified Residence Card' cannot be done online; those who want to combine them must go to the counter in person.

After obtaining the merged version, every time you go through a procedure that requires a new card to be issued, you must submit a separate application for the 'Specified Residence Card.' If you miss this step, the next card issued might revert to a standard Residence Card. The authorities state it takes at least two weeks, so you can't expect the same-day pickup rhythm of a standard card. Your electronic certificate on the old card may expire about a week before the new one is issued, which could affect functions like your My Number health insurance card, so try to schedule your applications to avoid days when you need medical care or have other important procedures.

Fees depend on the procedure and when you apply. Generally, there is no processing fee for the first card issued after June 14, 2026. If you reapply later due to a request for exchange, you may have to pay two separate fees in some cases: 1,900 yen to the Immigration Services Agency (paid via revenue stamps) and 600 yen to the Japan Agency for Local Authority Information Systems (J-LIS), or 800 yen if an electronic certificate is included. The official fee schedule is quite detailed based on card type and procedure, so check your specific category against the official table before applying. The actual amount is subject to the official announcement at the time of application.

Whether you should switch depends on how often you visit the administrative offices. For those who frequently handle procedures and have to visit municipal offices anyway, the time saved by the merged card is significant. For those whose procedures aren't due for a long time, it's most cost-effective to just do it when you next renew or change your status.

Quick summary

  • Applications open on June 14, 2026. Obtaining the card is optional; you may continue to use your existing two cards separately if you prefer.
  • The application must be done alongside other residence procedures. If you just want to switch cards, apply for a 'Reissuance' (requesting a switch) at a regional immigration office. The application cannot be done online.
  • Issuance takes at least about two weeks. The electronic certificate on your old card may expire about a week before the new one is issued, so try to avoid scheduling medical visits or important procedures during this time.
  • The first application for the card after June 14, 2026, is generally free of charge. Subsequent re-issuances may incur fees: 1,900 yen for the Immigration Services Agency plus 600 yen for J-LIS, or 800 yen if an electronic certificate is included. Please check the official fee schedule before making payment.
  • You must re-apply for the 'Specified Residence Card' every time your card is re-issued; if you forget to apply, you will receive a standard residence card.

From the editor-in-chief

I've lost count of how many times I've pulled out the wrong card at the counter. Residence card in the front slot, My Number card in the back slot—when it was time to show my My Number card, my hand would reach for the front slot. The clerk would look at me, I'd look at the card, and there would be two seconds of silence. Once they are merged into one, the chance of pulling the wrong one is cut in half. My only fear is that during those two weeks while waiting for the new card, I'll habitually head to the municipal office, only to realize halfway there that I didn't need to make the trip at all.
