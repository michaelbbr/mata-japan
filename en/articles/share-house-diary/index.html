I signed just from photos and slept on the floor of a share house for a week

The room could not fit a bed, and I did not know how to turn on the water heater either

The first time I lived in a share house, I didn't go see it in person; I only looked at photos online and signed. I swiped through photo after photo, the room looked quite nice, everything it should have was there, and after looking I signed, without ever confirming with my own eyes what the room actually looked like.

On the day I moved in, I opened the door and was stunned for a moment. As soon as I pushed the door open, it was dim inside, completely different from the photos. The room was much darker than in the photos, and could not even fit a single bed. The bed would not fit. The only place left to sleep was the floor.

Fortunately I had stuffed a sleeping bag into my luggage. For the next whole week, I spread the sleeping bag on the floor every night to sleep. It was winter at the time; the first night I lay down, the cold of the floor slowly seeped through the sleeping bag. For a week, the sleeping bag was my bed.

Showering wasn't any better. I thought the water heater was broken. I turned on the faucet and only cold water came out. Taking a cold shower in winter, those few seconds when the water hits you wake you right up. I only figured it out later: I simply didn't know how to turn on that water heater. It was perfectly fine.

Looking back now, it's still funny. The room couldn't fit a bed, I didn't know how to turn on the water heater and thought it was broken, and I spent a week sleeping on the floor and taking cold showers just like that. If I had gone to look at the room before signing, none of this probably would have happened. My first time living in a share house left me with this kind of impression.

From the editor-in-chief

Signing a contract based on photos, I've done that too. In the photos the room looked bright and spacious, so I even skipped the viewing. On moving day I carried my suitcase in, laid it down sideways, and the 玄関 was full. I had to squeeze in sideways. It turned out that spacious look in the photos was shot with a wide-angle lens.
