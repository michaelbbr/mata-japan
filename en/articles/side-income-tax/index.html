The 200,000 yen line for side income only applies to income tax; resident tax is calculated on a separate form.

What can be skipped under 200,000 yen is the income tax side. Resident tax is filed separately with the city or ward where you live, and the two filings go through different doors

For those who find the page a bit long, the 'cheat sheet' at the end of the article breaks down the five key points. Scroll down to compare your own numbers and stop at the row that applies to you.

Talk about the 200,000 yen line for side income with people around you, and eight out of ten remember only the first half. The editorial team itself makes content and writes for a living, so side income is a familiar topic here, and at year end, when a whole year's fees are added up, everyone does the same thing and stares at the calculator for a long time. Under 200,000 yen, no filing needed. As that sentence travelled, the resident tax part got skipped along with it, and that is where most of the trouble starts. 🧾

The National Tax Agency's wording is actually clear. A person who receives salary from one employer, all of it subject to withholding, must in principle file an income tax return when the total of income other than employment and retirement income exceeds 200,000 yen. What counts here is income in the tax sense, the amount after necessary expenses are deducted from revenue, and if there are several side jobs they are all added together. People paid by two or more employers are judged differently, and salary that did not go through year end adjustment is added by its revenue amount. The conditions are written in NTA No.1900. Conversely, a person whose total is 200,000 yen or less and who meets the conditions above does not have to file the income tax return, and that exemption reaches only the income tax side.

Resident tax goes through a different door. Both doors need attention. The City of Saitama's official answer is blunt. Even a person who meets the conditions and does not need to file an income tax return must in principle still file for city and prefectural inhabitant tax, and if no income tax return is filed, the side income is reported to city hall by the person themself. For a person who did file an income tax return, the documents are sent to the municipality, so resident tax does not need to be filed again. Windows and forms differ by where you live, so the details follow your own municipality's guidance. The NTA's tax return guidance page carries the same note, that resident tax may need a separate filing and details should be checked with the municipality.

A person who is going to file a tax return anyway cannot leave side income outside the door. In a year when you file a refund return for the medical expense deduction or the donation deduction, side income of 200,000 yen or less still goes into the return together with everything else. The NTA's explanation of this rule is that the 200,000 yen line defines cases where filing is not required, and it is not an option to drop small income when you do file. The filing period is in principle from February 16 to March 15 of the following year, moving to the next weekday when it falls on a holiday. The actual dates follow the NTA's announcement for that year.

The 650,000 yen blue return special deduction sounds big, and its conditions are packed in just as tightly. Only limited types of income can use the blue return. Business income, real estate income and forestry income are inside the scope, and an ordinary side job taxed as miscellaneous income is not. Even when a side job counts as business income, the maximum 650,000 yen deduction requires bookkeeping by double entry, attaching a balance sheet and a profit and loss statement to the return and filing within the deadline, plus either electronic book preservation or electronic filing through e-Tax. Without the electronic step it is 550,000 yen, and with simpler bookkeeping it is 100,000 yen. The figure is a deduction amount. Its job is to lower taxable income, which is a different thing from getting 650,000 yen back.

What you can do at year end is simple. Put a whole year's side income and expenses into one table, and which side of 200,000 yen the total falls on is visible on the spot. If it falls on the side where no income tax return is needed, remember to add the resident tax filing. With the table done, February does not mean turning the house upside down hunting for receipts, and there is no second trip to the tax office and city hall windows.

Quick summary

  • A person with salary from one employer, all withheld, must in principle file an income tax return when total income other than employment and retirement exceeds 200,000 yen, judged on income after expenses
  • With a total of 200,000 yen or less and the conditions met, the income tax return can be skipped, but resident tax is still filed with the municipality where you live, and the two are handled separately
  • In a year when you file a tax return for reasons such as the medical expense deduction, side income of 200,000 yen or less is filed together as well
  • The filing period is in principle from February 16 to March 15 of the following year, moving forward when it falls on a holiday, with actual dates in the NTA's announcement for that year
  • The blue return special deduction of up to 650,000 yen requires double entry bookkeeping, filing within the deadline, plus electronic book preservation or e-Tax, and a miscellaneous income side job is outside the blue return's scope
