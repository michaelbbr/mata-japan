Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.23

Yomiuriland's illuminations run until April next year, so you can still catch them after Christmas.

Jewellumination 2026 runs from October 29 to April 4, 2027. This year features a new green gemstone color, and the fountain show remains the main attraction.

- Period: October 29, 2026 to April 4, 2027 (excluding park closure days, 138 days in total) - Time: Lights on at 16:00. Most days until 20:30, some weekends and holidays until 21:00. Please check the official calendar. - Location: Yomiuri Land (4015-1 Yanokuchi, Inagi City, Tokyo) - Cost: Admission tickets start from 1,800 yen. Online discounts are available; check the official ticket page before you go. - Exception: On weekdays from March 1 to March 12, 2027, the park will only operate during the day with no illumination. Lights will also be off on park closure days.

Most illuminations in Tokyo wrap up by the end of January, but this one stays lit until April 4. Yomiuriland's Jewellumination is in its 17th season this year and started on October 29. Those living in the Tama area don't need to rush through the Christmas crowds; wait until after the New Year and go at your own pace—the lights will still be there. 🌿

This year's theme is 'Growing Light.' The new color, a green gemstone shade introduced for the first time in two years, is inspired by the energy of plants sprouting and growing. The lighting around the park entrance has been updated with jade and emerald tones. When placed next to the pinks and purples of previous years, the green section is the first thing that catches your eye. The lighting design is once again by Motoko Ishii; her gemstone-colored LEDs have been the signature of this event since 2010.

Make sure to time your visit for the fountain show. At the wave pool, the 15-meter-high giant circular water screen, combined with 226 nozzles, lasers, and flames, is a must-see—if you miss one, you'll have to wait for the next round. The Grand Sanctuary in the center of the park has been redesigned this year to be a walk-through structure. Previously, you could only take photos from the outside, but this year you can look out from inside the lights, offering a completely new perspective for your photos.

The best time to go depends on whether you're a resident or a tourist. The park is most crowded on weekends in late December. If you live in Tokyo, try a weekday evening in January. Enter at 16:00 when the lights turn on; it's just getting dark and the lines for rides are shorter. After watching the fountain show, take the gondola down—the sea of lights viewed from the gondola is a sight you can only get because of the park's high elevation. Note that from March 1 to March 12, the park only operates during the day and there are no illuminations, so don't get caught out if you're visiting in spring. Just check the official calendar before you head out.

The choice of ticket will affect your mood for the day. If you only want to see the lights and the fountain show, an entrance ticket is enough; it costs 1,800 yen for adults, and it's a bit cheaper if you buy it online. If you want to enjoy the rides until the evening, check out the One-Day Pass, which is 5,900 yen for adults. If you arrive in the afternoon and buy the wrong ticket, you'll end up spending money on rides you didn't get to use. Take your time and double-check what you want to do on the official ticket price page before you head out.

Transportation: Get off at Keio-Yomiuriland Station and take the park's gondola or a bus up the hill. It's windier up there than in the city center. When standing in front of the fountain waiting for the show, your neck and hands will get cold first, so bring a scarf and gloves, and hold a hot drink. The green lights reflecting on the water make the cold seem a bit more bearable.
