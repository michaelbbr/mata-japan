Follow this order for your procedures during your first month in Japan to avoid extra trips.

Residence Card, Health Insurance, Pension, Bank, and Mobile Phone—each has its own deadline. If you get the order wrong, you'll end up making extra trips.

The bank teller said you need a Japanese mobile number to open an account, as it's required for receiving SMS verification codes for online banking. When you go to a mobile carrier store, the clerk says you need a bank account or credit card to set up a payment plan, and you can't sign up without one of them. Neither side is wrong. The problem is the order of operations: someone who just arrived only has a passport and a residence card with a blank address field, missing the prerequisites for both.

For those who don't have time to read from the beginning, there is a summary at the end of the article. Follow that order, and you won't go wrong.

Can I get my Residence Card on the day I land?

Many people can get it, but it depends on which airport you arrive at. Mid-to-long-term residents arriving at the 10 airports of Sendai, Narita, Haneda, Chubu, Kansai, New Chitose, Kobe, Hiroshima, Fukuoka, and Naha will receive their residence card during immigration inspection. If you enter through other airports or ports, your passport will be stamped with 'Residence card to be issued later.' The card will be sent to your registered address via simplified registered mail only after you complete your address registration.

So, for those arriving at smaller airports, you can't just wait. You must go to the ward office to register your address so that your card can be mailed. If you just sit at home waiting for the card to arrive, nothing will happen during those days of waiting. It is normal for the address field on the back of a newly issued residence card to be blank; the address is added in the next step when you go to the municipal office.

Once you receive your card, you are legally required to carry it with you at all times. You must be able to present it if requested by immigration or the police. Those staying for 3 months or less will not be issued a Residence Card; this article is for those staying longer than 3 months.

Why is the first stop the ward office and not the Immigration Bureau?

Once you have settled on an address, you must register your residency at the municipal office of your new area within 14 days. For those moving from abroad, this is done as a 'Moving-in Notification' (Tennyu-todoke). Bring your Residence Card; if you haven't received it yet, bring your passport with the 'to be issued later' stamp. Once processed, your address will be written on the back of your Residence Card, and your Certificate of Residence (Juminhyo) will be established. Every address verification you need for future procedures will be based on this day.

The cost of procrastination is written in the Immigration Services Agency's instructions. If you fail to report within 14 days without a valid reason, you may be subject to a fine of up to 200,000 yen. If a newly arrived person fails to report within 90 days, their residence status may even be revoked. We cover the details and address changes after moving inThe article on changing your residence card address within 14 days of movingorganized inside. Both require reporting within 14 days, but the situations are different: one is establishing your residence in Japan for the first time, and the other is already living in Japan and moving to a new place.

The logic is simple: prioritize this trip above all else. The NHI and Pension counters are usually in the same building, so if you're lucky, you can finish all three in one morning. You will often need a copy of your Certificate of Residence (Juminhyo no utsushi) to open a bank account, so you can request it before you leave. However, requirements vary by bank—some require the Individual Number to be printed on it, while others only accept originals. Check what your bank needs and whether they accept copies before deciding how many to request, so you don't waste money on fees.

Can I handle Health Insurance and Pension on the same day?

Yes, and you should do it on the same day.

Whether you need to enroll in the National Health Insurance (NHI) depends on your category. If you are a dependent under a family member's employer-sponsored health insurance, you don't need to join; those who are employed and covered by their company's social insurance also don't need to apply. For everyone else, you must visit your local municipal office to complete the enrollment process within 14 days of becoming eligible. The eligibility date refers to the day you entered Japan, moved into a new municipality, or lost your previous insurance coverage. If you delay the process, your premiums will be calculated retroactively from your eligibility date—Katsushika Ward, for example, notes that they can backdate up to 2 years. More realistically, if you visit a doctor before completing the enrollment, you will have to pay the full cost out-of-pocket.

NHI premiums are calculated based on your household and your previous year's income. According to Katsushika Ward, you must file a resident tax declaration even if you have no income; otherwise, your premiums cannot be calculated correctly, and you may face issues later when applying for high-cost medical expense refunds. However, practices vary by municipality for those who had no income in Japan the previous year, so just check with the counter when you join the NHI.

Pension matters are also handled at the same office. Foreigners living in Japan between the ages of 20 and 60 are required to enroll in the pension system, as clearly stated by the Japan Pension Service, but there are three entry points. If you are employed and your company covers Employees' Pension Insurance, the company handles it, and you don't need to visit the office. If you are unemployed and supported by a spouse, and you meet the criteria, you are a Category 3 insured person; this is handled through your spouse's company, and you don't need to pay premiums yourself. Those who don't fall into either category must go to the pension window at the municipal office to enroll as a Category 1 insured person. The payment slips will be mailed to your home by the Japan Pension Service. If your income is unstable and you cannot pay, there are exemption and deferral systems. Not paying and filing an application are two different things; ask for clarification at the office before deciding.

As for which card to bring when seeing a doctor, the rules changed at the end of 2025. The old health insurance cards are no longer valid. Now, you should bring your My Number Card with the registration completed, or the eligibility confirmation document sent by your insurer,The article on old health insurance cardsThere is a complete comparison.

The bank needs a mobile number, and the mobile carrier needs a bank account—how do you sequence this?

I've experienced that deadlock scenario myself. In the end, it only worked out after the staff talked for a long time and coordinated between both sides. The rules have some flexibility, but that approach depends on luck and can't be planned into your schedule.

For most people, the safest route is this: once your address is set and printed on the back of your residence card, go apply for a mobile phone plan. Since the residence card is an official government ID that can be used for identity verification, the information on the card must match your application for the online verification to pass. Once you have your phone number, then go open a bank account. However, this is just a suggested path, not a one-size-fits-all standard procedure. If you already have a working overseas credit card, you can use that for the phone plan first. If your company helps you set up a payroll account, you might be able to open a bank account earlier. Everyone's starting conditions are different, so the order can vary. What follows is the path least likely to hit snags if you don't have a credit card or company assistance.

Start by asking banks that openly publish their procedures for foreigners. Japan Post Bank (Yucho Bank) checks your period of stay; if your residence card expires in less than 3 months, they will ask you to renew it first. Seven Bank requires you to have lived in Japan for at least 6 months, or have at least 3 months remaining on your period of stay; online applications require two forms of ID, and if you have been in Japan for less than 6 months, you must also provide proof of employment. There is a new change to note here: Seven Bank has not yet updated its system for the new version of the residence card issued starting June 14, 2026. ATM and online account openings are currently suspended, with ATM services not scheduled to resume until December 23, 2026. If you have the new card, you should look into other banks for now. For the full requirements of each bank,The article about the bank account and mobile phone number deadlockIt's broken down in great detail.

You can apply at major banks, but the requirements for foreigners are often not publicly disclosed, and there is a lot of inconsistency in judgment between different bank tellers. For the first week, just aim for any account that works. If your company designates a specific bank for payroll, wait until you have your phone number and Juminhyo (Certificate of Residence) before going to handle it; by then, you'll have everything the teller asks for.

When will the Individual Number notification arrive? Should I apply for the plastic card now?

Once your Certificate of Residence is established, your Individual Number will be assigned. ✉️ The notification will be sent via 'Simple Registered Mail' (Kan-i Kakitome) to the address on your certificate. Officially, it arrives about 2–3 weeks after residency registration, though the actual time depends on your local municipality's processing speed. Keep that letter safe when you see it in your mailbox; you will need that number for many future procedures. To be clear: this notification is just to inform you of your number and cannot be used as an ID document; the counter will not accept it as proof of identity.

The My Number Card is a separate matter; you must apply for it yourself via mail, smartphone, or online. About a month after applying, your local municipality will mail a 'Notice of Issuance' to your home. Follow the instructions on it to book an appointment, then bring the required documents to the counter to pick up your card. It is only considered complete once you have the card in hand. If you want to get your card within the first month, apply as soon as the notice arrives to keep your schedule on track.

You won't be fined for delaying the application, but you'll just be late in accessing two conveniences. First, you won't be able to print your Juminhyo no Utsushi at convenience store multi-function kiosks, meaning you'll have to work around the municipal office's opening hours. Second, once you complete the health insurance registration, your card becomes a 'MyNa Insurance Card' at hospital reception desks. If your address isn't stable and you might move again soon, you can wait until you're settled to apply. Since the address is printed on the card, every time you move, you have to go through the change process again.

How do I throw out the trash? Is joining the neighborhood association (Jichikai) mandatory?

Garbage sorting and collection days are determined by each municipality; there is no national standard. Ask at the counter when filing your moving-in notification, or check your municipality's official website for 'How to sort and dispose of garbage' (Gomi no wakekata/dashikata). Your local sorting guide and calendar will be there. Large items are considered 'oversized garbage' (Sodai gomi) and require an application and the purchase of processing tickets; you cannot just leave them on the curb. I once mixed metal into general waste and was scolded on the spot by the man managing the trash collection point. Only after that day did I understand that if you sort incorrectly, the trouble falls on the people who take shifts every day to organize the trash station.

Joining the neighborhood association is optional,The article on neighborhood associationsI checked, and the official page for Setagaya Ward clearly states that it is a voluntary organization and membership is optional. The real thing to clarify is something else: who manages the trash station in your area, whether you can use it without joining the association, and how the cleaning rotation is scheduled. For trash sorting, check the local government's announcements; for trash station usage and cleaning rotations, check your building's management bylaws or local arrangements. When neighbors and building managers give conflicting information, don't guess—confirm with the management company or the local government. If you live in an apartment, ask one extra question: some building management associations collect neighborhood association fees on your behalf. Check your management fee statement first to avoid paying twice. You can take your time deciding whether to pay the fees, but you should ask about the trash station rules in your first week.

Quick summary

  • Your trip to the ward office: Bring your Residence Card (or passport with the 'to be issued later' stamp if you haven't received the card yet) and file your residency registration within 14 days of your move-in date. Check your bank's requirements regarding how many copies of your Certificate of Residence you need and whether the Individual Number should be included before you apply.
  • Handle Health Insurance and Pension on the same day: If you are covered by your company's social insurance, you don't need National Health Insurance (NHI). If you are a dependent on a family member's workplace insurance, you don't need to join separately. Everyone else must join the NHI within 14 days of their eligibility date (date of entry, moving in, or loss of previous insurance). There are three types of pension: Employees' Pension (handled by your company), Category 3 (handled through your spouse's company), and Category 1 (handled by yourself at the pension office).
  • Arrange your mobile number and bank account based on your own situation: signing up for a mobile plan first and then opening a bank account is the safest route for most people. If you have an overseas credit card or company assistance for setting up a salary account, you can change the order. Requirements vary by bank; remember that Seven Bank will not resume accepting the new version of the residence card until late December 2026 (scheduled).
  • Check garbage rules in your first week: See the municipality's announcement for sorting, and check your building's management bylaws or local arrangements for trash station usage and cleaning rotations. If the information conflicts, ask your management company or the municipality.
  • The Individual Number notification arrives in about 2–3 weeks; keep it safe, as it cannot be used as an ID. After applying for the My Number Card, you will receive an issuance notice in about a month, which you then use to book an appointment to pick up the card.
