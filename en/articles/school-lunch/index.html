Japanese elementary school lunches started with rice balls, and now even the cost has changed

School lunch (kyushoku) is a full set consisting of a main dish, milk, and side dishes. Starting in April 2026, public elementary school lunch fees will be supported by the national and prefectural governments; the part parents need to handle is the paperwork for allergies.

給食 has more details than you'd imagine. For parents in a hurry, there's a quick summary at the end of the article; save it and read it slowly.

When children enter elementary school in Japan, most schools provide 給食, so you don't have to prepare a lunch box yourself every day at noon. Arrangements vary from school to school, but this system has a long history. In 1889, the private Chuai Elementary School in Tsuruoka, Yamagata Prefecture, prepared lunch for children from poor families who couldn't bring a lunch box, rice balls, grilled fish with pickles, provided free of charge. The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology's centennial history of the school system records this meal as the starting point of Japanese school 給食, for a simple reason: children who aren't full can't concentrate on studying either.

To understand what 給食 looks like today, look at the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology's definition. Most schools provide 完全給食, with all three components: a staple food, milk, and side dishes, with the staple alternating between rice and bread. In the Reiwa 3 (2021) survey, among schools implementing 完全給食, the rate of rice-based 給食 was 100%, averaging 3.5 times per week. Side dishes change with the seasons, soup is served hot into bowls, and the classroom quickly fills with the smell of miso soup. The overall implementation rate for elementary school 給食 is 99.0%, so almost every child eats lunch produced through the same process at noon.

Costs have changed significantly in the last two years, and this is the area parents need to update the most. According to a 2023 fiscal year survey, the average monthly cost for public elementary school lunches was 4,688 yen. This is not a small amount. Starting in April 2026, a new system co-funded by the national and prefectural governments, officially called 'Fundamental Reduction of the Burden of School Lunch Fees,' will take effect. It applies to public elementary schools, with a subsidy benchmark of 5,200 yen per student per month for full lunches, calculated over 11 months a year. If the lunch fee in your municipality is higher than the benchmark, the difference may still fall on the parents, as practices vary by region. Junior high schools are not included in this round of national subsidies; their fees follow the arrangements of each individual municipality. If you have two children—one in elementary and one in junior high—you need to look at the accounts separately.

🥛 The flow of money is different than before. This subsidy is funded half by the national government and half by the prefecture, and it goes through the municipality to support school lunch costs—it isn't paid directly to parents. Parents don't need to apply for the subsidy, and generally, no paperwork is required. Once the system is in place, many municipalities stop charging parents for school lunches within the standard amount. How the monthly deduction notice changes or how much the amount is reduced varies by location, so just compare it when you receive notices from the school or municipality. The junior high school portion is handled separately as usual. If the amounts don't match up, ask the school or the municipal office—the sooner you ask, the easier it is to resolve.

Allergies are another line, and the rules are much stricter than for costs. The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology's guidelines for handling food allergies in school meals are very clear: when a child needs the school to arrange food allergy accommodations, a 學校生活管理指導表 filled out by a doctor usually has to be submitted following the school's procedures. A parent's verbal explanation or own written note cannot directly replace the formal document, so it is best to confirm with the school before enrollment what materials are needed. The approach is, in principle, complete removal; if a child cannot eat a certain ingredient, that dish is not served, and the school also arranges individual interviews to confirm each child's situation before meals begin. If your child has a food allergy, organize their medical records before enrollment, and confirm from the school's notices whether the guidance form needs to be resubmitted or updated every year; leaving it until school starts puts the kitchen in a difficult position.

給食 has another scene rarely seen in Taiwan: lunch is carried and served by the children themselves. Those on 給食当番 duty put on aprons and hats, bring soup buckets and trays into the classroom, and collect them after the meal. For children with severe allergies, the guidelines specifically state that even tasks like serving food and cleaning up that may involve contact with allergens should be avoided, and the school will make other arrangements. These details are written in the guidelines; they look trivial, but what they protect is the sense of safety for other people's children when eating.

Quick summary

  • 完全給食 is a staple plus milk plus a side dish; rice-based school lunches average 3.5 times a week, with an implementation rate of 99.0% in elementary schools
  • Average monthly school lunch fee for public elementary schools is 4,688 yen (FY 2023). Starting in April 2026, the national and prefectural governments will provide joint support, with a standard amount of 5,200 yen per month.
  • The subsidy only goes up to public elementary school, the difference above the standard amount may still have to be borne by parents, and junior high school follows each municipality's arrangements
  • For food allergies, submit the 學校生活管理指導表 filled out by a doctor; accommodations are in principle by complete removal, and it must be done before enrollment
  • 給食当番 is rotated among the children, and for tasks with a risk of allergen contact, the school will help children avoid them

From the editor-in-chief

When I was a child, what I feared most was being on school lunch duty; the soup bucket was heavy, the corridor was long, and my hands were shaking by the time I reached the classroom door. Watching Japanese elementary school students in white clothes and hats lining up to serve meals, I just want to tell them, don't fill the soup too full, this is advice from a senior.
