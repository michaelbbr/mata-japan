The Suica penguin is graduating, how should you read the timeline?

The penguin handover and how to use the transit card are two things to check separately.

Updated 2026.11.18: The successor has been decided. On Suica's 25th anniversary, JR East announced the voting results: the purple rabbit in Proposal B won with 860,152 votes (out of 1,664,399 total votes) and will take over as the mascot character from April 1, 2027. The rabbit does not yet have an official name; nickname submissions are open until 3:59 p.m. on December 15, and the name will be announced at the handover ceremony on March 31, 2027.

People who tap their card on the ticket gate every day on their commute probably have some attachment to the penguin🐧 on the card. So after JR East announced that the Suica penguin would be "graduating," many people's first reaction was: can I still use my card? In fact, the character handing over the role and whether the card can still be tapped are two completely different things. Don't scare yourself by following forwarded messages on social media.

Understand the times first

On the timetable there are two dates, one about the card and one about the mascot. November 18, 2026 is the 25th anniversary of Suica's issuance: this system went online in 2001, which makes it exactly 25 years this year, and this day is more like the card's birthday. March 31, 2027 is the penguin's graduation activity day, the day this commemorative project called Penguin Years wraps up.

What to follow for commemorative events

For people who want to follow the events, the official Penguin Years special page is very comprehensive. From November 18 to December 31 there is a second thank-you prize campaign; from November 21 to 23, Tokyo Station hosts the thank-you festival 'ARIGATO Suica Market'; the penguin-painted train runs until December 2; and the digital stamp rally runs until December 25, with a total of 20 locations; the TRAINIART penguin retrospective feature on the special page is also listed as running until March 31. The page also collects 25 years of penguin merchandise, from T-shirts, badges, and plush toys to refrigerators and toasters. But most are marked as not for sale, you can see them but can't buy them; if you want to buy current products, the special page has a separate section for the latest merchandise. Each event has different times and ways to participate, so before you go, check the special page for the one you want to follow.

What to do about the cards in hand

Back to the card itself, which everyone cares about most. The announcement about the penguin's graduation is about the mascot character being handed over; it does not declare that existing Suica cards are invalid. This is the point most easily gotten wrong in this whole matter. The validity, balance, and terms of use of the card you tap every day are governed by Suica's own rules and have nothing to do with the Penguin Years event schedule. If you are really worried, do not look at forwarded messages; check JR East's official Suica page directly. The link is in the source materials at the end of the article; for which type your card is and how its rules are written, the official page prevails.

What people who live here need to watch out for

For people who live in Japan and pass through the gates with Suica every day, what you really need to watch out for are the small things. During the event period, the area around the venue at Tokyo Station may be busier. People who normally pass through this area can check the event location first, then decide whether to take a detour. For people who want to collect commemorative merchandise, check the special page first for the on-sale time and sales method, so you don't make a trip after work only to find it already sold out, or that it wasn't sold on site at all. As for the card, just tap it as usual. People who want to collect one more physical card while the penguin is still printed on it can now buy both personalized and unregistered Suica at station multifunction ticket machines and Midori no Madoguchi counters. The penguin has accompanied everyone through the gates for 25 years; before it graduates, when you pass the penguin plaza at Shinjuku Station, you might as well stop and take another look at it.
