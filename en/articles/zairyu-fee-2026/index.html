Chinese residents in Japan · YU · 2026.11.28

Residence status renewal fees are now based on the number of years granted; renewing a 1-year visa five times in a row costs more than double the fee for a 5-year visa.

Starting October 1, 2026, fees for status of residence changes and renewals are tiered by permit duration. For the 1-year-plus bracket, online applications are 5,670 to 9,450 yen cheaper than at the counter. The fee for permanent residence applications has also increased to 200,000 yen.

If you're short on time, there's a cheat sheet at the end of this article comparing the new and old fees. If you go to renew your residence status after October 1, 2026, the processing fee will be different from before. Previously, it was a flat 6,000 yen at the counter or 5,500 yen online, regardless of the number of years granted. Now, it's tiered based on the period of stay granted, and the fee difference between a 1-year and 5-year visa is more than double. 😅

The revised price list looks like this. The new system applies to applications accepted by the Immigration Services Agency from October 1, 2026. Change of status and renewal of period of stay share the same table. For a period of 3 months or less: 10,000 yen at the counter, 10,330 yen online. Over 3 months and up to 6 months: 18,000 yen at the counter, 15,330 yen online. Over 6 months and less than 1 year: 25,000 yen at the counter, 21,330 yen online. 1 year: 33,000 yen at the counter, 27,330 yen online. Over 1 year and less than 3 years: 48,000 yen at the counter, 42,330 yen online. 3 years or more and less than 5 years: 64,000 yen at the counter, 56,550 yen online. 5 years or more: 75,000 yen at the counter, 65,550 yen online. The online totals include a 330 or 550 yen payment processing fee.

The price difference between online and counter applications has widened along with the duration. For the '3 months or less' category, online is actually 330 yen more expensive than the counter. For the '1 year' category, online is 5,670 yen cheaper. For the '5 years or more' category, online is 9,450 yen cheaper. The Immigration Services Agency states that online is cheaper to encourage online applications and reduce congestion at counters. Starting October 1, 2026, revenue stamps (shunyu inshi) can no longer be used for online payments; instead, you must use convenience store or bank payments. After the screening is complete and you receive a notification, the payment service provider will send a payment link. The amount is only confirmed after approval, and payment must be made by the day before card pickup. The counter still uses revenue stamps, which the immigration office recommends buying at a large post office in advance.

The new system applies based on the acceptance date, not the approval date. Applications accepted before September 30, 2026, will be charged the old rate of 6,000 yen at the counter or 5,500 yen online, even if the approval comes after October 1.

Getting a 5-year period of stay now can save you a significant amount in fees. For a 5-year term, it costs 65,550 yen online or 75,000 yen at the counter. Renewing for 1 year five times in a row costs 136,650 yen online or 165,000 yen at the counter. If rates remain the same, the fee for a 5-year period is less than half the cost of five consecutive 1-year renewals. There is also a reason related to permanent residence: starting April 1, 2027, permanent residence applicants must generally hold the longest period of stay allowed for their current status (e.g., 5 years for 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services'). Applications filed by March 31, 2027, can still qualify with a 3-year permit. Additionally, those who hold a 3-year permit on March 31, 2027, and apply for permanent residence after April 1, 2027, will be eligible for transitional measures if they receive their results before that 3-year permit expires.

However, a 5-year permit is not guaranteed just by applying. The Immigration Services Agency's renewal guidelines state clearly that whether to grant a permit and for how long is at the discretion of the Minister of Justice, based on a comprehensive assessment of the applicant's activities, status of residence, and necessity. The guidelines list 8 representative factors, including whether the activity meets the requirements, good conduct, and tax/reporting obligations. The government has not published a formula or threshold for receiving a 5-year permit. The Immigration Services Agency categorizes the employers of some work visa applicants into 4 categories based on the nature of the company and tax data. Administrative scrivener practice suggests that the stability of the employer may influence the period of stay granted, but the Immigration Services Agency has not published fixed rules for issuing 1, 3, or 5 years based on company category.

The application fees for permanent residence have also been revised. For applications accepted on or after October 1, 2026, the fee will be 200,000 yen, up from the previous 10,000 yen. Currently, permanent residence applications are submitted at the counter and paid only upon approval, typically using two 100,000 yen revenue stamps (shunyu inshi). Cases accepted before September 30 will remain at 10,000 yen, even if approved after October 1. For those meeting criteria for financial hardship and humanitarian grounds, fees for changes and extensions can be reduced to 10,000 yen, and permanent residence to 20,000 yen. Reductions must be processed at the counter; online applications do not support fee reductions.

Before you renew, it's worth checking a few things against the 8 points mentioned above. Have you missed or been late on resident tax, income tax, pension, or health insurance payments? Have you completed all the necessary filings for job changes or moving? Are your employment contract period and job description consistent with your application? Those with a residence period of 6 months or more can generally apply for renewal starting 3 months before the expiration date. The official standard processing time is 2 weeks to 1 month, though actual time may vary depending on the case and review status. Special circumstances like long-term overseas business trips or pregnancy/childbirth may allow for earlier acceptance; consult with your local immigration office in advance.

There is also a timeline that many people hear about for the first time, called the 'special period of stay.' If you have submitted an application for renewal or change of status before (or on) the expiration date of your current period of stay, and you have not yet received the results by the time it expires, you may continue to reside in Japan and engage in your originally permitted activities until the decision is made, or until 2 months after your original expiration date, whichever comes first. This does not apply if your original period of stay was 30 days or less. Simply applying for permanent residence does not trigger this special period; you must still keep an eye on the renewal deadline of your current status while waiting for the permanent residence review. For in-person applicants, there will be a note on the back of the residence card indicating an application is in progress. Online applicants do not get this note, so you must check the system once the card's expiration date has passed. If you apply even one day late, this special period does not apply, and you would be considered an illegal overstayer. When renewing your period of stay, you can also apply to integrate your residence card with your My Number card into a 'Specific Residence Card (Tokutei Zairyu Card),' though you may need to pay an additional fee depending on the issuance status. The full process is detailed in our post from November 22, 2026.

Quick summary

  • As of October 1, 2026, fees for status of residence changes and renewals are tiered by the duration of the permit. For a 1-year permit, the fee is 33,000 yen at the counter or 27,330 yen online. For 5 years or more, it is 75,000 yen at the counter or 65,550 yen online.
  • The applicable rate is based on the date of receipt. Applications received on or before September 30, 2026, will still be charged at the old rates: 6,000 yen for in-person applications and 5,500 yen for online applications.
  • The fee for a 5-year permit is less than half the cost of renewing a 1-year permit five times consecutively. From April 1, 2027, permanent residence applicants are generally required to hold the longest period of stay for their current status, though transitional measures apply to some who currently hold a 3-year permit.
  • The application fee for permanent residence is increasing to 200,000 yen, payable only upon approval. For those with financial difficulties and humanitarian considerations, this can be reduced to 20,000 yen, which must be handled at the counter.
  • Those with a residence period of 6 months or more can generally apply for renewal 3 months in advance. There is a grace period if you submit before the expiration date, but if you apply even one day late, this grace period does not apply.
