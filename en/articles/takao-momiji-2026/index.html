Mount Takao's autumn leaves are running a little late, so head out early on the weekend

Takaosan Momiji Matsuri runs from October 24 to December 13, 2026. The cable car line light-up runs on Fridays, weekends and holidays until December 6. Peak dates are a forecast, so check the latest information before you go

When Tokyo residents want autumn leaves, the trip does not need much ceremony. Mount Takao is a 599-meter mountain in Hachioji; ride the Keio Line to Takaosanguchi Station and it makes an easy day trip, with time to do laundry after you get home. The mountain is not high. In autumn the whole mountain changes color, with foliage spots around Kiyotaki Station, along the cable car line, at Yakuoin and at the summit. The 2026 Takaosan Momiji Matsuri runs from October 24 to December 13, organized by the Hachioji Visitors and Convention Association, and we are right in the middle of the season now.

This year's colors are arriving a little late. In its fourth forecast, released on November 11, 2026, the Japan Meteorological Association put Mount Takao's peak from November 15, four days later than last year's November 11. The association's event page lists the usual peak as mid-November to early December, which roughly matches the forecast. Keep in mind this is still a forecast, and the actual colors on the mountain change every day. Before you set out, glance at this year's photos on the association's event page or the latest Mount Takao updates, so you are not surprised on site to find the summit already red while the foot of the mountain is still green.

The light-up only happens on certain days. The autumn leaves light-up and night viewing along the cable car line runs from November 1 to December 6, 2026, only on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, from sunset to 20:30. Weekday daytime is quieter, the trails are calm, the air is cool, and fallen leaves make a faint sound underfoot. If you want the light-up, plan for a Friday evening or a weekend, head up early in the morning, take your time on the mountain, and come down as it gets dark, and you will meet the lights on the way, with the timing flowing nicely.

You can save your energy for the paths on the mountain. The Takao Tozan cable car from Kiyotaki Station to Takaosan Station takes about 6 minutes; climbing the steep slope, the car tilts slightly and the maples outside the window slide past one by one. 🍁 Cable car times and the last departure change with the season, so check the Takao Tozan official page before you go. When the queue is long, walking up is also an option; take it slowly and you are surrounded by color the whole way.

After coming down, many people stop for a bath nearby. When your legs are tired, hot water is especially persuasive. The bath etiquette is worth remembering here: do not put your towel in the bath water, wash your body before getting in, and pour hot water over yourself with kakeyu to let your body get used to the temperature. Someone on our team knows this routine well, for an everyday reason: after a bath and the trip home, your shoulders relax, dinner tastes good whatever it is, and you do not spend the time before sleep replaying how far you walked today.

If the weather is good this weekend, set the alarm early and head out. The early train is empty, the houses outside the window slowly give way to mountains, and when you arrive at Takaosanguchi, the cold air touches your face first, with the autumn leaves waiting right ahead. Using a day like that leaves you feeling settled.

From the editor-in-chief

A light-up until 20:30 sounds like plenty of time. That is what I thought last time: I stayed an extra half hour on the summit watching the sunset, queued for the cable car down in the dark, and halfway through my bath started worrying about the last train, so my shoulders never relaxed at all. This year I will follow the early start this article describes. The alarm is already set.
