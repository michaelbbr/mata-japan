Don't forget to claim your pension money before leaving Japan; it only counts for a maximum of five years.

The maximum refund for National Pension is 537,600 yen, with Employees' Pension calculated separately. You must meet all the requirements, and if you don't submit the application within two years of leaving Japan, you lose the right to claim it.

In the final days before leaving Japan, your checklist keeps getting longer: moving-out notification, contract cancellations, lease termination. The lump-sum withdrawal payment often ends up at the bottom of the list because it isn't urgent on the day of departure, but there is a two-year deadline waiting for you. 💴

The Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment is money for foreigners leaving Japan, but you must meet all conditions. First, you do not have Japanese nationality. Second, at the time of application, you are no longer insured under the Japanese public pension system. Third, you must have at least 6 months of contributions to the National Pension or coverage under the Employees' Pension; these are calculated separately and cannot be combined. Fourth, you no longer have an address in Japan. Fifth, you must apply within 2 years from the date you lost your address in Japan. Sixth, you have never been eligible for a public pension, including disability pensions or disability allowances (the latter is a one-time payment, but the government counts its eligibility here). Seventh, your total coverage period is less than 120 months (10 years); those with 10 years or more cannot claim this and should apply for the old-age pension instead.

First, look at the National Pension amounts. For the period where the final payment month falls between April 2026 and March 2027, paying for 6 months or more but less than 12 months costs 53,760 yen. The scale increases up to 537,600 yen for 60 months or more. This 60-month cap was raised from 36 months in April 2021; for those whose final payment month was before that, the old 36-month cap still applies. The key is the cap: a maximum of 60 months is counted. Even if you pay for 8 years, you only get credit for 5 years. Once you claim, your entire contribution period is reset to zero. National Pension and Employees' Pension are calculated separately; you cannot use months from the Employees' Pension to meet the 6-month threshold for the National Pension.

Employees' Pension is calculated separately. The amount is the average standard remuneration multiplied by the payment rate. For 60 months or more, with the final contribution month after April 2021, the payment rate is 5.5. The average standard remuneration is roughly the average of your monthly salary and bonuses during the contribution period; the higher the salary, the higher the refund, but it is still capped at 60 months. The lump-sum withdrawal for Employees' Pension is subject to a 20.42% withholding tax. Before leaving Japan, designate a tax agent, and later file a tax return for retirement income with the tax office; you may be able to get the tax refunded. The National Pension lump-sum withdrawal is not subject to withholding tax; this is the difference between the two. Don't skip this step.

The order of procedures matters. Before leaving Japan, remember to go to your municipal office to file a moving-out notification. If you mail your application to the Japan Pension Service before you leave, ensure it arrives after your scheduled moving-out date; if it arrives too early, they may assume you still have a residence in Japan. You will need to attach a copy of your passport and proof of a bank account in your name. If you have already filed your moving-out notification, you are exempt from providing a certificate of non-residence. You must also include documents proving your Basic Pension Number, such as the Basic Pension Number Notification or your Pension Handbook.

The application form itself is available in 14 languages, including English and Chinese. You can download it from the Japan Pension Service website or pick it up at pension offices, local pension consultation centers, or municipal offices. The waiting time after mailing it is not short, and there is no way to check the status online; you just have to wait for the notice from the Japan Pension Service to arrive. Packing your bags and booking your flight doesn't mean it's over; make sure you leave a contact address where you can still receive mail.

There is one situation you should think through clearly. If you have lived in Japan for 10 years and have a qualifying period of 120 months, you cannot receive the Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment; the system directs you toward the Old-age Pension. If you leave after paying for just over 9 years—falling just short of the 10-year mark—you have two options. One is to take the lump-sum payment, which only counts 60 months, and the remaining months are wiped out. The other is not to take it, keeping your contribution record so that if you return to work in Japan later, you can continue accumulating time, or use a totalization agreement to bridge your years and reach the 10-year requirement for the Old-age Pension. Decide which path to take before you leave, so you don't end up back home realizing the amount isn't what you expected.

Countries that have social security agreements with Japan have a different process, but there are two types of agreements. One only prevents double taxation and does not combine years of service; the UK, South Korea, China, and Italy fall into this category. The other allows you to combine the coverage periods of both countries to meet eligibility requirements. Taiwan is not on the agreement list, so Taiwanese readers don't need to consider this path. The list is subject to the latest announcements by the Japan Pension Service; checking their website before leaving the country is the safest approach.

Quick summary

  • Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment conditions must all be met: non-Japanese nationality, no longer insured under the public pension system at the time of request, at least 6 months of National Pension contributions or Employees' Pension coverage (calculated separately), no address in Japan, request made within 2 years of losing your address, never obtained rights to a pension or disability allowance, and a total coverage period of less than 120 months (otherwise you qualify for the old-age pension).
  • For National Pension, if your final contribution month is between April 2026 and March 2027, the refund is 53,760 yen for 6 to 11 months, up to 537,600 yen for 60 months or more. The calculation is capped at 60 months (this was increased from 36 months in April 2021; for those who left before that, the cap was 36 months).
  • Employees' Pension is calculated by multiplying the average standard remuneration by the payment rate (5.5 for 60+ months). It is subject to 20.42% withholding tax. Designate a tax agent before leaving Japan and file a tax return for retirement income. National Pension lump-sum withdrawals are not subject to withholding tax.
  • Before leaving Japan, file your moving-out notification. The application for the lump-sum withdrawal payment must be sent to the Japan Pension Service after your scheduled moving-out date. Include a copy of your passport and proof of an account in your name. Once you've filed the moving-out notification, you are exempt from needing a certificate of no fixed address. There is no online tracking for the application after mailing; you'll just have to wait for the notification by mail.
  • If you don't claim the lump-sum withdrawal, you can keep your contribution record and accumulate it later or combine it to reach the 10-year requirement to receive the old-age pension. There are two types of totalization agreements; you cannot totalize periods for the UK, South Korea, China, and Italy. Taiwan is not on the agreement list.
