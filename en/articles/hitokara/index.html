You really do not need to feel embarrassed about going to karaoke alone in Japan.

The uncle's second round on the weekend: Introduction to hitokara (solo karaoke)

Many Chinese friends who have just moved to Japan ask me the same question: 'If I walk into a karaoke shop alone, will the staff think I'm weird?'

Don't worry at all. Japan has long had 'hitokara,' the practice of singing karaoke alone, and many chains accept solo customers. Some stores even have dedicated booths or plans. Large chains like Big Echo offer solo plans on weekday mornings and afternoons. Most chains charge per person every 30 minutes, and there are also 'free time' plans; actual prices depend on each store's price list. Weekday daytime is usually cheaper, but solo rates and drink policies vary by store, so it's best to check the official price list on the store's website before you go.

Why do people living here love hitokara (solo karaoke)? Because it solves the most practical stress-relief problem in daily life in Japan: no need to organize a group, no need to accommodate others, and you can repeat the same song five times until you master it. After work, walk into a karaoke shop near stations where office workers gather, like Shimbashi or Ikebukuro, pick songs alone, and sing alone without worrying about anyone else's playlist. Everyone is singing away the fatigue of the week in their own way.

🎤 If it's your first time, go during a weekday daytime or early evening slot. The prices are the friendliest, and you're less likely to be turned away for a full room. When you arrive, ask if there is a solo surcharge (o-hitori-sama ryokin) and what the drink policy is: some shops have a 'one-drink' rule where you must order one, while others offer all-you-can-drink bars. Rules vary by chain, and the final bill can differ significantly. Check the official app for reservations and coupons before you go; many chain app member discounts are cheaper than the walk-in price.

I mostly sing old songs myself. Singing is like running; you don't need to compare yourself to anyone. Once you finish and walk out the door, the Tokyo night breeze hits you, and you're ready to face Monday again. You should go sing once, too, and come back to tell me what you sang.
