After moving, change the address on your 在留カード within 14 days, do not make a wasted trip to Immigration

Do not go to the 出入國在留管理局; bring your 在留カード directly to the 区役所 of your new place of residence

Anyone who has moved house in Japan knows how tiring it is: the cardboard boxes aren't unpacked yet, you have work the next day, and the resident change counter at the 区役所 usually only opens on weekdays. But there is one thing you really can't delay: changing the address on your 在留カード!

📍 The place most people get wrong is the level of the office: after moving, medium- to long-term residents must, within 14 days of moving into their new residence, file a change-of-residence notification at the municipal office (区役所 / 市役所) of the area where the new address is located. The procedure is at the same counter as the 転入届 and 転居届 for Japanese nationals; bring your 在留カード and the new address will be written on the back of the card. There is no need to go out of your way to a regional immigration services office. The Immigration Services Agency's official Q&A clearly states that residence notifications can only be made at a municipal office, and immigration counters do not accept them.

Why can't you put it off? The Immigration Services Agency's explanation is strict: if you fail to report within 14 days without a legitimate reason, you may be fined up to 200,000 yen; if you still haven't reported your place of residence within 90 days after first landing, it may also become grounds for revocation of your status of residence. The 90 days here refers to not reporting your place of residence after newly entering Japan, and should be viewed separately from the 14-day deadline for a regular move. Living in Japan, your address is the foundation of all your identities: bank, mobile phone number, health insurance, pension, and driver's license all follow the address on your 住民票 and residence card. If the foundation isn't laid properly, every step after that will get stuck.

In practice, I would arrange the order like this: in the week you move, first go to the 区役所 once to complete both the move-in registration and the address change on your 在留カード (if moving within the same ward, file a 転居届; if moving across wards, first get a 転出証明書 from the old ward, or process the move-out online with your My Number card, then file the move-in registration at the new ward); next, use your phone to file a 転居届 with the post office to have your mail forwarded; finally, slowly change the registered address for your bank and mobile number. Update the address on your 在留カード and 住民票 first, then deal with the registration details for your bank and mobile phone number, and things usually go more smoothly. Otherwise, when the bank asks you to confirm your new address, you'll have to make another trip. Don't ask me how I know. Also, Monday mornings or the turn of the month may be more crowded, so if you want to save time, you can first check whether your local 区役所 publishes information on how busy its counters are.
