You must update the address on your residence card within 14 days of moving; do not make a wasted trip to the Immigration Services Agency.

Do not go to the Immigration Services Bureau; just take your residence card to the ward office where your new home is located.

Anyone who has moved in Japan knows how tiring it is: the boxes aren't even unpacked yet, you have to go to work the next day, and the resident registration counter at the kuyakusho (ward office) usually only operates during weekday business hours. But there is one thing you really cannot delay: updating your address on your zairyu card (residence card)!

📍 The place where most people go wrong is the window level: after moving, long-term residents must report their new address at the municipal office (ward office/city hall) of their new residence within 14 days of moving in. The procedure is at the same window as the moving-in or moving-within-ward notifications for Japanese citizens. Bring your residence card, and the new address will be written on the back. You do not need to go to the Immigration Services Agency. The official Immigration Q&A clearly states that address reporting can only be done at municipal offices and is not handled at immigration windows.

Why can't you put it off? The Immigration Services Agency's explanation is very strict: if you don't report within 14 days without a justifiable reason, you could be fined up to 200,000 yen. If you don't register your place of residence within 90 days of your new entry, it could even be grounds for the revocation of your residence status. If you move and don't report your new address within 90 days, that too could lead to revocation. The 90-day rule here is different from the standard 14-day move-in deadline, so keep them separate. Living in Japan, your address is the foundation of your entire identity: banks, mobile phone numbers, health insurance, pensions, and driver's licenses all follow the address on your Residence Card and Certificate of Residence. If the foundation isn't solid, you'll hit a wall at every subsequent step.

In practice, I would arrange the order like this: In the week of moving, go to the kuyakusho (ward office) to finish the moving-in notification and update the zairyu card address (if moving within the same ward, file a tenkyo-todoke; if moving across wards, get a tenshutsu-shomeisho (moving-out certificate) from the old ward first, or use your My Number Card to file the moving-out notice online, then go to the new ward to file the moving-in notice). Next, use your phone to change your address with the post office so mail is forwarded. Finally, slowly change the registered address for your bank and mobile phone. It is usually smoother to update the address on your zairyu card and juminhyo (residence record) first, then deal with the bank and mobile phone registration data. Otherwise, when the bank asks to verify your new address, you will have to make an extra trip. Don't ask me how I know. Also, Monday mornings or the end/beginning of the month can be crowded. If you want to save time, check if your local kuyakusho has published information on counter congestion.
