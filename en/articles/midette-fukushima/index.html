When I miss Fukushima, I go for a browse at MIDETTE in Nihonbashi

An editor from Fukushima takes you around Fukushima's home in Tokyo

I'm from Fukushima and have lived in Tokyo for more than twenty years. The longer I live here, the more I realize that homesickness doesn't disappear, it just takes a very concrete shape: sometimes it's the taste of a bowl of Kitakata ramen, sometimes it's just wanting to hear a Fukushima accent.

That is why I go to 'Nihonbashi Fukushima-kan MIDETTE' in Nihonbashi every so often. It is Fukushima Prefecture's information hub in the capital region, known as an antenna shop (a regional specialty store). They sell Fukushima produce, local sake, processed goods, and souvenirs. There is also a dining corner where you can eat seasonal local cuisine and ramen. The name MIDETTE comes from the Fukushima dialect 'mitette,' which means 'come and take a look.' The name itself is so Fukushima—so welcoming that it's hard to refuse!

The address is 4-3-16 Nihonbashi Muromachi, Chuo Ward, Tokyo (Yanagiya Taiyo Building 1F). Business hours are 10:30–19:00. It is closed during the New Year holidays and building inspection days. For transportation, it is about a 3-minute walk from Exit A8 of Tokyo Metro Mitsukoshimae Station, about a 1-minute walk from JR Shin-Nihonbashi Station, and about a 5-minute walk from JR Kanda Station. It is recommended to check the official page again before you go, as event activities and the cafe menu change.

Antenna shops are actually a type of shop well worth getting to know when living in Tokyo. Many local governments set up these outlets to bring local specialty products and tourist information to Tokyo. Without making a special trip there, you also have a chance to buy distinctive local products. When you don't know where to go on the weekend, just pick an antenna shop for a prefecture you're curious about and browse.

As for me, every time I go to MIDETTE I buy 🍑 peach juice and drink it standing at the store entrance before leaving. That's my way of staying in touch with Fukushima; I can recharge without going back home.

From the editor-in-chief

I walked into the local products shop intending only to take a look, and ended up standing in front of the shelves for twenty minutes, wanting to buy even local snacks I had never tried. In the end I came out carrying two bags, and standing at the entrance I wondered whether I was homesick or just craving a snack. After thinking about it, it was probably both.
