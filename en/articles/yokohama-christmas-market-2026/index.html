Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.19

The Christmas market at the Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse is open until 10:00 PM this year, and there are free illuminations to see in the seaside area.

From November 20 to December 25, 2026, main venue admission starts at 500 yen. Free areas are located at Akarenga Park and Shinko Central Square.

Living in Tokyo, I often bundle up at home in the winter. To save on heating bills, I don't turn on the heater, wear two layers of sweaters, and even my fingertips feel cold while sitting on the sofa. Even with a hot cup of tea, the warmth doesn't last long. On weekend nights, I actually feel like going out for a walk. The Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse Christmas Market runs from November 20 to December 25, 2026. Once the seaside lights turn on, standing outdoors with a hot drink makes my shoulders relax, and my body warms up more easily than it does staying at home.

The main venue is at the Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse event plaza, where German-style wooden huts stand side-by-side, selling hot food, desserts, and Christmas trinkets. The air is filled with the scent of grilled sausages and mulled wine. Operating hours are divided into three periods: the opening day on November 20 is from 5:00 PM to 9:00 PM; from November 21 to December 4, it's 11:00 AM to 9:00 PM; and from December 5 to December 25, it's extended until 10:00 PM. Last order for food is 30 minutes before closing. The illuminations light up at 4:00 PM daily, and the large Christmas tree stays lit until 11:00 PM. After dark, the red brick walls are wrapped in warm light; the sea breeze blows, your face feels cold, but the colors you see feel so warm.

Admission fees depend on the date. General admission to the main venue is usually 500 yen, and 1,000 yen on 'special days' (December 5, 6, 12, 13, 18–20, 24, and 25). Children in elementary school and younger are free when accompanied by a parent; through December 4, junior high students and younger are also free. November 25 is Yokohama Citizens' Appreciation Day, with free admission for Yokohama residents that day. 🎄 If you want to avoid queuing at the entrance, you can buy a fast-track ticket for 2,000 yen on regular days or 2,500 yen on special days. General admission tickets can be purchased with cash at the venue entrance. If you want to pay using other methods, you must pre-order through official ticketing channels. Keeping a little cash in your wallet will give you peace of mind.

There are free areas again this year. The Christmas Village on the Akarenga Park side is a free zone, and from November 25 to December 25, you can see Pokémon illuminations—perfect for those who don't have much time after work and just want to take a walk by the sea and see the lights. The Shinko Central Square in front of the warehouses has a Christmas Gate, which is also free to enter and features market stalls and a large tree. The operating hours for the free areas and the main venue are not identical, and the content varies, so check the official page before you head out so you don't end up standing blankly at the entrance.

Transportation is simple. It's about a 6-minute walk from Bashamichi Station or Nihon-odori Station on the Minatomirai Line, about a 12-minute walk from Minatomirai Station, and about a 15-minute walk from Sakuragicho Station on the JR and municipal subway lines. It's windy by the sea. Bring a scarf and gloves so you won't regret it while waiting for hot food at the stalls. On the way back, walk slowly along the harbor; the Ferris wheel lights spin in the distance, and the cup in your hand still holds a bit of warmth. You don't need to pack a night like this with too much; leaving a little space makes it just right.

From the editor-in-chief

I originally planned to only visit the free areas and kept my wallet tight, but I stood at Shinko Central Square looking at that tree for so long that my feet got freezing cold, so I turned around and bought hot soup and gloves. On the train ride back, I did the math: I didn't spend a single yen on admission for the free areas, but I ended up spending more on staying warm than the main venue's ticket would have cost.
