Limited-Time Events · AYAKA · 2026.11.21

The Chichibu Night Festival is held on weekdays, with 4,000 fireworks on the night of December 3.

Floats climbing Dango-zaka, fireworks blooming over Hitsujiyama Park; wandering through the Chichibu Silk Market by day, and keeping your gloves on at night even if the cold makes you want to stand still.

The Chichibu Night Festival (Chichibu Yomatsuri) features the Yoimiya (eve festival) on Wednesday, December 2, and the Hon-matsuri (main festival) on Thursday, December 3. Both days are weekdays. If you live in the Tokyo metropolitan area and want to go, you'll need to take time off and plan your return train trip carefully. You can hear the sound of fireworks coming from over the mountains, and the air is cold enough that your breath turns white. 🎆

This festival is the annual grand festival of Chichibu Shrine, counted among Japan's three great Hikiyama festivals alongside the Gion Festival in Kyoto and the Takayama Festival in Hida. There are six floats in total—two Kasaboko and four Yatai—weighing approximately 10 to 20 tons each. As these massive wooden structures are pushed and pulled through the streets, the deep rumble of their wheels against the pavement is enough to make your shoulders tense up instinctively.

During the day, you can watch street performances (Yatai Shibai). On December 2, from 12:00 to 14:00 at Atago Shrine, and on December 3, from 12:30 to 14:30 at Chichibu Shrine. This year's host is the Miyaji Town Association. The Kabuki lines sound a bit distant outdoors, but the makeup and costumes look very bright under the winter sun. If you stand to watch, your feet will slowly get cold, so wear thick shoes.

The Chichibu Night Festival Silk Market is also held during the day. On December 2, from 11:00 to 19:00, and December 3, from 11:00 to 20:00, the location is the Kaitsugi-sho-dori and Kuromon-dori streets, a 1-minute walk from Chichibu Shrine. They sell silk fabrics like Chichibu Meisen and other crafts. The fabric feels smooth to the touch. Since the crowds haven't gathered for the night yet, this is the best time to browse if you want to take your time.

Fireworks are the highlight of the evening. On December 2, from 19:00 to 20:00, there will be about 500 fireworks, and on December 3, from 19:30 to 21:55, there will be about 4,000. The launch site is Hitsujiyama Park. Recommended viewing spots are the Chichibu City Hall parking lot and in front of Seibu-Chichibu Station. The moment the fireworks burst, the entire night sky lights up, and the 4,000 shots continue until nearly 10:00 PM.

The climax of the main festival is at Dango-zaka. As the floats climb the slope toward the Otabisho, the incline is steep, and the shouts of the people pulling the floats get louder. Spectators stand along the slope as the floats slowly approach, the sound of wood grinding against wood blending with the voices of the crowd—the atmosphere becomes electric. If you want a good spot, arrive early; if you're late, you'll be stuck behind the crowd, standing on your tiptoes to see the lanterns on the roofs of the floats.

Remember the traffic regulations. Many roads in the city will be closed to cars from 11:00 to 20:00 on December 2, and 10:00 to 22:00 on December 3. Temporary parking at Miyaji Ground is free on December 2 from 9:00 to 21:00. On December 3, there is a fee of 1,000 yen for standard vehicles across 8 locations. Officials encourage everyone to use public transportation. If you are driving, check for parking availability before you leave; if the lots are full, you'll have to detour, and the festival atmosphere will be half-ruined by traffic jams.

Paid viewing seats are already sold out. The official announcement on November 11 stated that all tickets were claimed, so if you were hoping for a paid seat, you're out of luck this year. You'll have to stand or look for a spot around the city hall parking lot. Standing has its own charm; when the cold wind blows, the aroma of hot food from nearby stalls drifts by. Buy a hot drink to hold in your hands, and you'll feel a bit warmer.

The mountains of Saitama in early December are truly cold at night. Bring hats, gloves, and heat patches, and choose comfortable shoes. The day I took off work was rewarded with a night of 4,000 fireworks and floats climbing Dango-zaka. Standing in the cold air to watch it all, my frozen fingers were worth it.

From the editor-in-chief

Usually, festivals on weekdays are less crowded, but the Chichibu Night Festival is an exception. The main festival falls on a Thursday, so if you're planning to go, take the day off and stuff your gloves into your bag. The December cold in the mountains of Saitama is no joke; just imagining standing on the slope waiting for the floats to climb up makes my fingers feel frozen already.
